ENGLISH · PEARSON

English for Chemical Engineering

A staged route from IELTS readiness to technical coursework, research, and professional communication.

Learning loop Diagnose the stage → produce a task → review evidence → advance

Based on the independent Pearson style learning route. Each route is studied independently.

Start studying →

An original, task-based route for a student entering chemical engineering with an overall IELTS score near 7. The aim is to turn general language proficiency into clear scientific study, defensible research communication, and reliable professional action. Each short lesson starts with a real decision, shows a model, and ends with a self-check.

SECTION 01

Entry decision

An overall IELTS 7 is evidence of strong general English, but a single score does not show whether a student can explain a process, write a reproducible method, or qualify a research claim. Check the four skill scores and use the diagnostic below. A student with a lower score or no score can use the same diagnostic, then strengthen the missing general-language foundation before attempting the harder tasks. This route does not replace the separate IELTS preparation path.

Read In ten minutes, read one short scientific article or lab handout. State its question, method, central finding, and one limitation without copying sentences.

Write In fifteen minutes, explain a simple data change in 120 words with units, comparison, possible cause, and an alternative explanation.

Listen After a five-minute technical explanation, write the main claim, evidence, condition, and one clarifying question.

Speak Explain a familiar process in two minutes to a peer, then restate the main consequence for a nontechnical listener.

Decide Mark each task 0 if the main meaning is missing, 1 if meaning is partly clear but evidence or conditions are weak, and 2 if another person can act on the result without guessing. Start at the first stage with a weak task; do not repeat elementary grammar merely because it exists in a course catalogue.

SECTION 02

How this route works

Recognise Look at the task cue and decide what the reader or listener needs to do next.

Try Cover the model answer and produce your own response before reading it.

Correct Use the repair note to identify the decision that failed. Revise the same response, then try a changed example.

Advance Complete the stage gate with a fresh task. Use the decision index only after your first attempt.

The stages are ordered by the communication demands a chemical engineering student usually faces. Move by demonstrated performance rather than by calendar year. The examples teach English for engineering; they do not substitute for engineering calculations, site procedures, or laboratory supervision.

SECTION 03

Transition from IELTS to university study

Decode an assignment before drafting

Task clue The task says analyse, evaluate, compare, or recommend.

First decision Identify the requested action, audience, evidence, format, and boundary. Analyse explains relationships; evaluate judges against stated criteria; recommend chooses and justifies an action.

Model Task: “Evaluate two water-treatment options for a small campus using energy use and reliability.” The deliverable needs two alternatives, two criteria, evidence for each, and a reasoned choice. A description of filtration alone would miss the task.

Your turn A prompt asks you to recommend a heat-recovery option using cost and maintenance. What must the final paragraph do?

Answer and reason Name one option, justify it against cost and maintenance, and state an important limitation or condition. A list of options does not satisfy recommend.

If it went wrong Underline the verb and the judging criteria before collecting facts.

Turn a lecture into a usable record

Task clue A lecturer moves from a diagram to an example and then to an exception.

First decision Capture the claim, supporting reason, worked example, and unresolved question; do not transcribe every sentence. Mark uncertainty for follow-up.

Model Notes: “Heat exchanger effectiveness falls when fouling increases → thermal resistance rises. Example: lower outlet temperature at same flow. Question: was inlet temperature controlled?” The note preserves the causal chain.

Your turn A lecturer says a larger pump can increase flow, but pipe losses may dominate. Write a three-line note.

Answer and reason Claim: higher pump capacity can raise flow. Condition: frictional losses in the line may limit the gain. Check: compare the system curve and pump curve.

If it went wrong If notes contain only isolated nouns, add verbs and conditions.

Build technical vocabulary from relationships

Task clue A familiar word has a precise engineering meaning, such as yield, conversion, recovery, or capacity.

First decision Record a term with its definition, measurement basis, near neighbour, and one complete sentence. Do not memorise a translation alone.

Model “Conversion: fraction of a specified reactant consumed. Yield: desired product relative to a defined basis.” These are not interchangeable, so “conversion increased” cannot be rewritten as “yield increased.”

Your turn Write one sentence that uses conversion and yield without treating them as synonyms.

Answer and reason “Reactant conversion rose after the residence time increased, but product yield fell because more material entered a side reaction.”

If it went wrong When two terms feel similar, ask what is counted in the numerator and denominator.

Explain a result with claim evidence and condition

Task clue A laboratory result must become a short explanatory paragraph.

First decision Start with the observed result, give the evidence and units, propose a bounded explanation, then note the condition or uncertainty.

Model “The outlet temperature fell from 52 °C to 47 °C at the same flow rate. This pattern is consistent with reduced heat transfer, although a change in inlet temperature would offer another explanation.”

Your turn Rewrite “The system performed badly because it was dirty” as a defensible technical statement.

Answer and reason “The measured heat-transfer rate was lower than in the clean-run baseline. Fouling is one possible cause; inlet conditions and sensor calibration should be checked before assigning cause.”

If it went wrong Replace vague judgement with a measurable comparison; do not state a cause as certain without a test.

SECTION 04

First year scientific coursework

Read a textbook section for the decision it supports

Task clue A chapter is long, but the current problem needs one principle.

First decision Preview the question, definitions, assumptions, worked example, and boundary conditions. After reading, close the text and explain when the principle applies.

Model For a closed-system energy balance, the reading note should identify the system boundary and energy terms before any numerical substitution.

Your turn A problem says a tank is filled while liquid also leaves. What question should guide your reading of a balance chapter?

Answer and reason “Does the chosen control volume accumulate mass, and what enters and leaves?” A steady-state formula cannot be assumed just because the chapter includes one.

If it went wrong If you only highlight sentences, write a one-line application condition instead.

Describe numbers without claiming more than they show

Task clue You must report a change in measured concentration or temperature.

First decision State what was measured, the comparison, magnitude with units or denominator, and the limits of the observation. Distinguish percentage points from percent change.

Model “Removal efficiency rose from 60% to 75%, an increase of 15 percentage points or 25% relative to the initial value.”

Your turn A concentration falls from 20 mg/L to 15 mg/L. Write one precise sentence.

Answer and reason “Concentration decreased by 5 mg/L, or 25% of the initial 20 mg/L value.” The result alone does not prove why it decreased.

If it went wrong Check the comparison base and units before using increased, improved, or significant.

Write a lab report that can be repeated

Task clue Someone else needs to understand what you did and judge the result.

First decision Separate purpose, method, result, interpretation, and limitations. Include materials, conditions, measurement method, and deviations that affect repeatability.

Model “Three 100 mL samples were filtered through identical membranes at 25 °C. Filtrate volume was recorded every two minutes. The pressure gauge was recalibrated before the final run.”

Your turn Which detail is more useful in a method: “we tested carefully” or “flow was recorded every two minutes”? Explain.

Answer and reason The timed measurement is useful because another person can repeat it and evaluate time-dependent results. “Carefully” is not an operational method.

If it went wrong Move interpretation out of Methods and give enough conditions to reproduce the procedure.

Ask for clarification in a technical class

Task clue The formula is clear but its assumption is not.

First decision Ask a narrow question that names your current understanding and the point of uncertainty; then record the answer and test it on a new case.

Model “I treated the reactor as steady state because the inflow was constant. Does the changing tank level mean I still need an accumulation term?”

Your turn Turn “I do not understand the energy balance” into a question a lecturer can answer in one minute.

Answer and reason “I included inlet and outlet enthalpy. Should the heat-loss term be negative with the sign convention used in this example?”

If it went wrong Point to the exact assumption, sign, unit, or step rather than requesting a full re-teaching.

SECTION 05

Core chemical engineering study

Explain a process from a diagram

Task clue You need to describe a process flow diagram to a classmate or supervisor.

First decision Follow the material path: feed, transformation, separation, recycle, and product. Name variables and controls only where they explain the decision.

Model “Feed enters the mixer, joins the recycle stream, and passes through the reactor. The separator removes product; unreacted material returns to the mixer.”

Your turn A stream leaves a reactor, enters a separator, and some unreacted material returns to the reactor feed. Give a two-sentence explanation.

Answer and reason “The reactor outlet is separated into product and unreacted material. The unreacted fraction is recycled to the feed, so the reactor inlet includes fresh and recycled material.”

If it went wrong Avoid saying “it goes there” when stream identity matters.

Read a technical paper without mistaking claims for proof

Task clue An abstract reports a promising new membrane.

First decision Extract the research question, comparison, method, measured outcome, and limitations. Then inspect the figure and experimental conditions before accepting the conclusion.

Model Claim: membrane A has higher flux than B. Check whether both were tested at the same pressure, temperature, feed composition, and duration before repeating the claim.

Your turn A paper reports a 30% flux gain after two hours. What evidence would you seek before claiming better long-term performance?

Answer and reason Look for longer-run data, fouling behaviour, uncertainty, repeated trials, and comparable operating conditions. A two-hour result alone does not establish durability.

If it went wrong Do not let the abstract replace the method and data.

Compare design options against a common basis

Task clue A report must compare two pumps, catalysts, or separation routes.

First decision State the same duty and constraints for both options. Compare only criteria supported by evidence and distinguish a measured result from an estimate.

Model “At the specified 10 m³/h duty, option A uses less power, but its installed cost is higher. The conclusion depends on operating hours and the assumed electricity price.”

Your turn Option A has a lower purchase price but higher annual energy use. What would make a recommendation credible?

Answer and reason Use a common service life and duty, estimate total cost on that basis, and disclose maintenance and price assumptions. Then choose an option conditionally.

If it went wrong A comparison is weak if the alternatives are tested at different duties or judged by different criteria.

Communicate uncertainty and limitations

Task clue Your calculated result is more precise than the measurements justify.

First decision Report the estimate with units, source of uncertainty, and decision relevance. Use “approximately,” a range, or an uncertainty interval when warranted.

Model “The estimated efficiency was approximately 72%; flow-meter calibration and heat-loss assumptions are the dominant sources of uncertainty.”

Your turn A value is calculated as 72.438% from measurements reported to two or three significant figures. How should you write the result?

Answer and reason Report approximately 72%, or a justified uncertainty range if available. Do not present 72.438% as measured precision.

If it went wrong Precision of formatting is not certainty of measurement.

Write safety language that names the action

Task clue A procedure must tell a reader what to do when a limit is exceeded.

First decision Specify the condition, immediate action, responsible role, and escalation path. Preserve approved site terminology and never invent an emergency threshold.

Model “If the approved pressure limit is exceeded, stop the feed according to the site procedure and notify the shift supervisor. Record the reading and time.”

Your turn Improve “Be careful if the pressure is high” for an operating instruction without inventing a number.

Answer and reason “If the pressure exceeds the approved operating limit, follow the shutdown procedure and notify the shift supervisor.” The actual limit belongs in the controlled procedure.

If it went wrong Safety text fails when “high,” “soon,” or “as needed” replaces an approved criterion or action.

SECTION 06

Capstone and research

Synthesize sources around a question

Task clue Several papers address the same treatment process.

First decision Group studies by method or finding, compare their conditions, and identify the unresolved question. Give attribution for each claim using the required citation style.

Model “Two studies report improved removal at higher contact time, but both used synthetic feed. The effect with variable real wastewater remains uncertain.”

Your turn You have three studies with different temperatures and feed concentrations. What should the synthesis say before comparing removal rates?

Answer and reason State the differing conditions and avoid ranking the methods as if tests shared a common basis. Then identify what comparison or experiment is still needed.

If it went wrong A literature review is not three disconnected summaries; it explains agreement, disagreement, and limits.

Frame a capstone proposal around a testable decision

Task clue The project idea is broad: improve wastewater treatment.

First decision Specify the problem, decision criterion, candidate approach, evidence to collect, constraints, and deliverable. Keep the scope feasible for the available time and equipment.

Model “Compare two adsorbents for dye removal at fixed pH and contact time. Measure removal and material cost; recommend one for a defined feed concentration range.”

Your turn Rewrite “Study greener solvents” as a project question with a measurable comparison.

Answer and reason “For a defined extraction duty, which of two solvent candidates provides higher recovery at acceptable energy use under the same test conditions?”

If it went wrong A topic name is not a proposal until a decision and method are visible.

Write an abstract that mirrors the actual work

Task clue A reader needs the project in one short paragraph.

First decision Use the sequence problem, method, principal result, and bounded conclusion. Report only findings supported by completed work.

Model “We compared two filtration membranes under the same feed and pressure. Membrane B produced 18% greater initial flux, but both declined during the six-hour run. Longer tests are needed before a durability claim.”

Your turn A draft abstract says “This innovative system will revolutionize filtration.” What information is missing?

Answer and reason The method, comparison, measured result, conditions, and limitation. Replace promotional language with the tested finding.

If it went wrong An abstract is a compressed report, not a promise or introduction.

Present one technical message to two audiences

Task clue You must explain a design choice to specialists and a nontechnical sponsor.

First decision Keep the same evidence but change the level of terminology and the decision emphasis. Define specialist terms when the audience needs them.

Model Specialists: “The pressure drop rose 12% at the specified flow.” Sponsor: “The new filter would require more pumping power at the same throughput.”

Your turn Rewrite “The residence-time distribution widened” for a sponsor deciding whether product quality may vary.

Answer and reason “Material now spends a less consistent time in the vessel, so the product may vary more between batches.” If the link is untested, say it may, not it will.

If it went wrong Simplifying language must not erase uncertainty or alter the finding.

Answer a hard question without bluffing

Task clue A reviewer asks whether the result scales to an industrial plant.

First decision Acknowledge what the study shows, separate it from what remains unknown, and propose the next test. State where the evidence ends.

Model “Our bench test supports the observed trend at this feed composition. We have not measured full-scale fouling or energy demand; a pilot run would be the next step.”

Your turn You have data at one operating temperature and are asked whether the result applies across a wider range. Respond.

Answer and reason “We tested only at the reported temperature, so the wider-range effect is unknown. We would repeat the experiment at several controlled temperatures before generalising.”

If it went wrong Confidence comes from a clear evidence boundary, not an unsupported yes.

SECTION 07

Graduation and professional practice

Write a handover that survives a shift change

Task clue The next engineer must know the current state and the next action.

First decision Record status, trend, action already taken, open risk, responsible person, and deadline. Distinguish a measured observation from an interpretation.

Model “At 14:00 the outlet temperature was 47 °C, down 3 °C from the morning baseline. Sensor calibration is pending. Do not change the set point until the check; Samira will update the log by 16:00.”

Your turn A pump vibrates more than usual. What belongs in the handover?

Answer and reason The measured or observed change, time, operating condition, any action taken, a safety or production consequence, and who will inspect it next. Do not write only “pump noisy.”

If it went wrong A handover without ownership or next action is incomplete.

Write an incident account without confusing fact and cause

Task clue An equipment failure must be documented.

First decision Use a timeline of verified events, readings, actions, and impacts. Label untested causal ideas as hypotheses and direct the reader to the investigation.

Model “The alarm activated at 09:12. The operator reduced feed at 09:14. The sensor displayed 85 °C. The cause has not been established; instrument and cooling checks are in progress.”

Your turn Why is “The operator caused the trip by overloading the unit” unsuitable when the investigation is incomplete?

Answer and reason It states an unverified cause and assigns blame. Report the observed sequence, relevant settings, and pending checks instead.

If it went wrong Do not turn correlation or chronology into proof of cause.

Write a decision memo for a manager

Task clue A manager needs a recommendation in less than a minute.

First decision Lead with the choice, then the operational reason, cost or risk, uncertainty, and requested approval. Put detailed calculations in an attachment if needed.

Model “Recommend a two-week pilot of filter B. It met the target flow in bench tests, but cleaning frequency is uncertain. Approval is requested for a controlled trial before full purchase.”

Your turn A draft begins with two pages of equipment history. How should you reorganise it?

Answer and reason Open with the decision and its reason. Move background below or to an appendix, and give the approver a clear next action.

If it went wrong A correct analysis is ineffective if the requested decision is hidden.

Run a meeting that ends with accountable work

Task clue A team discusses a process problem but leaves without a plan.

First decision Separate observation, options, decision, action owner, deadline, and evidence needed. Summarise disagreements fairly before closing.

Model “We agreed to compare sensor readings before changing the control loop. Omar will check calibration by Thursday; the team will review the result on Friday.”

Your turn Turn “We talked about poor recovery and will look into it” into a useful minute.

Answer and reason “Recovery was below the agreed target in the last two runs. Lina will check feed composition and sampling records by Wednesday; we will choose a corrective test after those checks.”

If it went wrong If no person or date is named, the action is not yet assigned.

Prepare for research and career conversations

Task clue You must contact a potential supervisor or describe your project in an interview.

First decision State the specific problem, your contribution, evidence, limitation, and what you want next. Tailor the emphasis to the recipient; avoid broad claims about being passionate or expert.

Model “In my capstone, I compared two adsorbents under identical feed conditions and built the results table. Adsorbent A showed higher removal in the tested range; we did not examine regeneration. I would like to study repeated-use performance.”

Your turn An interviewer asks what you learned from a project whose first trial failed. Give a three-part answer.

Answer and reason Describe the failure without blame, the diagnostic change you made, and the result or remaining uncertainty. Example: “The first readings drifted; I checked calibration, repeated the run, and reported the corrected trend with the original run documented.”

If it went wrong Specific evidence is stronger than fluent but empty self-description.

SECTION 08

Stage gates and portfolio

Keep one dated attempt and one revised attempt for every stage. For each gate, score task completion, evidence and precision, audience fit, and control of uncertainty from 0 to 2. A stage is ready at 7 of 8 with no zero in evidence and precision. If not, revise the weakest artefact and try a new case before advancing.

Transition from IELTS to university study

Produce Read one assignment prompt, take five-minute lecture notes, and write a 120-word result explanation.

Ready when The work states the requested action, keeps one causal chain, and distinguishes observation from interpretation.

First year scientific coursework

Produce Submit a one-page lab report with a method another student could repeat and a chart described in words.

Ready when The method has conditions and measurement steps; the result has units and a bounded explanation.

Core chemical engineering study

Produce Compare two engineering options in a 250-word note and explain a process diagram aloud in two minutes.

Ready when Both options share a comparison basis; the spoken path identifies feed, transformation, separation and recycle.

Capstone and research

Produce Write a 300-word evidence synthesis, a 150-word proposal, and a one-minute project explanation.

Ready when Sources are compared under stated conditions; the proposal has a testable decision; the explanation survives one challenge question.

Graduation and professional practice

Produce Produce a shift handover, a decision memo, and a response to a research or job interview question.

Ready when Each artefact names the audience, next action, evidence, owner or limitation where relevant.

SECTION 09

Mixed transfer check

Answer these without looking back. A fluent sentence is not enough: state the decision, evidence, and condition that make it trustworthy.

Assignment decision A task says evaluate two solvent-recovery methods. What must be explicit before you write?

Data language Removal changes from 40% to 50%. State the change without confusing percent and percentage points.

Research claim A paper tested one membrane for a single hour. How would you report its apparent advantage?

Safety instruction Rewrite “If it gets hot, deal with it quickly” without inventing a threshold.

Manager recommendation A pilot showed improved recovery but uncertain cleaning cost. Give the first sentence of a memo.

SECTION 10

Mixed transfer answers

Assignment decision The two methods, common duty and judging criteria, evidence for each, and a justified conclusion with conditions.

Data language It rises by 10 percentage points, which is a 25% increase relative to the initial 40%.

Research claim State the observed advantage under that test condition and withhold claims about long-term durability.

Safety instruction “If temperature exceeds the approved operating limit, follow the site procedure and notify the shift supervisor.”

Manager recommendation “Recommend a controlled extension of the pilot before purchase because recovery improved but cleaning cost remains uncertain.”

SECTION 11

Decision index

Use a symptom to locate the first useful move. Return to the lesson when the situation has more than one possible explanation.

I do not know what the question asks Decode the verb, audience, evidence, format, and boundary.

My notes are a transcript Capture claim, reason, example, and open question.

Two technical terms seem identical Define each by measurement basis and use it in a full sentence.

My paragraph sounds certain but the data are weak Separate observation, explanation, and alternative cause.

A paper sounds convincing Check methods, comparison conditions, data and limits.

My report cannot be repeated Add materials, conditions, timed steps, and deviations.

My presentation is too technical for its audience Keep the evidence; change the terms and decision emphasis.

I need to persuade a decision-maker Lead with recommendation, evidence, risk, and requested action.

I am unsure how to answer a challenge State the evidence boundary and the next test.

My handover is vague Add state, trend, action, risk, owner, and deadline.

SECTION 12

Final readiness evidence

Before graduation, a student should be able to submit a reproducible lab report, an evidence-based design comparison, a concise capstone abstract, and a short presentation that handles a challenge question honestly. After graduation, the same communication skill should produce a safe handover, an accurate incident account, a clear recommendation, and a specific research or career conversation. The portfolio is the evidence of progress; the IELTS score is the entry signal.
