Programme / CE 201 / First material balance

PRINCIPLES OF CHEMICAL ENGINEERING · ORIGINAL STARTER LESSON

Follow what enters, leaves and stays.

A material balance is an account of matter inside a boundary. Learn how to draw that boundary, choose units, and calculate a mixture before trying a new example.

The ideaWorked exampleExploreApply

1. Choose the system before the equation

Imagine a mixing tank. Draw an imaginary boundary around it. A feed stream crosses into the boundary; a product stream crosses out. Liquid already inside the tank is inventory. A mass such as 50 kg describes inventory; a mass flow such as 50 kg/h describes movement per unit time. They are different quantities.

For a chosen chemical species, a rate balance is:

Accumulation = input − output + generation − consumption

Every term must have the same units, such as kg/h. A reaction can create or consume a species. In an ordinary chemical process, total mass is conserved, so the reaction terms cancel when all species are counted together. Without reaction, they are zero for each species too.

Steady state means the inventory inside the boundary is not changing with time. It does not mean that the tank is empty or that flow has stopped. For steady, nonreacting mixing, input equals output.

Why can I not always write input = output?

If a tank receives 10 kg/h and releases 7 kg/h, it gains 3 kg/h. Setting input equal to output would erase that accumulation. Check the situation before simplifying the balance.

2. Read a mass fraction as part of a whole

A stream containing 20% solute by mass has mass fraction 0.20: each 1 kg of mixture contains 0.20 kg of solute and 0.80 kg of solvent. Multiplying total mass flow by mass fraction gives component mass flow.

100 kg/h × 0.20 = 20 kg/h of solute

The percentage is a mass percentage here. A mole percentage or volume percentage would require different quantities. Never silently exchange their bases.

Refresh percentages

Percent means “per hundred”. Divide 20 by 100 to get 0.20. To turn fraction 0.08 into a percentage, multiply by 100 to get 8%.

3. Work through a mixer

Feed A is 100 kg/h with 20% solute by mass. Feed B is 50 kg/h of pure water. They enter a mixer that runs at steady state with one outlet, no reaction and no material loss. Find the outlet flow and solute mass fraction.

Keep total flow and component flow in separate columns
StreamTotal, kg/hSolute, kg/h
A100100 × 0.20 = 20
B500
Outlet15020

Total balance: outlet flow = 100 + 50 = 150 kg/h.

Solute balance: solute outlet flow = 20 + 0 = 20 kg/h. The solute is diluted, not destroyed.

Composition: 20 ÷ 150 = 0.1333… = 13.33…% by mass.

Check the solvent too: 80 + 50 = 130 kg/h, and 20 + 130 = 150 kg/h. The outlet concentration is between the two feed concentrations, as it must be for this nonreacting mixture.

Why is the answer not the average, 10%?

The feeds have unequal mass flow rates. The 20% stream contributes twice as much mixture as the pure-water stream. An ordinary average of concentrations is correct here only when the total mass flows are equal.

4. Change the water feed

Feed A stays at 100 kg/h and 20% solute. Change only the pure-water feed. Predict what happens to concentration, then compare it with the result.

150 kg/h total; 20 kg/h solute; 13.33% solute by mass.

Adding water increases total flow while solute flow stays at 20 kg/h. That is why concentration falls. This model does not calculate temperature, volume change, mixing time or equipment size.

5. Recognise different question types

Find a missing flow: use the total balance. Find composition: add a component balance. Find inventory change: retain accumulation. Include a reaction: account for species generation or consumption using a balanced reaction. This starter works through the first two types; reacting and unsteady systems need further lessons.

6. Apply the idea to a new mixture

A steady nonreacting mixer receives 80 kg/h of a solution with 15% solute by mass and 70 kg/h of pure water. There is one outlet and no losses. What is the outlet solute percentage by mass?

Show the worked answer

Solute: 80 × 0.15 = 12 kg/h. Total: 80 + 70 = 150 kg/h. Mass fraction: 12/150 = 0.08. Percentage: 8%.

This check gives feedback on one example. Passing it does not establish mastery of material balances.

Explain it tomorrow

Without looking at the table, explain why adding pure water changes the fraction but not the solute mass flow. Then consider a tank that fills: which term must return to the balance?
