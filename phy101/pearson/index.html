PEARSON / 01

Measurement and motion

01

Units and conversion factors

Meaning

A measured answer needs a number and a unit. A conversion factor is a ratio equal to one, so multiplying by it changes units without changing the physical quantity.

First move

Write the starting unit, multiply by factors that cancel it, and check whether the new numerical size is plausible. Convert to one coherent unit system before substituting into an equation.

Worked reasoning

At 72 km/h, a car travels 72/3.6=20 m/s. In 6 s at constant speed it covers 120 m; the constant-speed condition matters.

Your turn

Convert 54 km/h to m/s. How far does it travel in 4 s at that constant speed?

Reveal answer and reasoning

54/3.6=15 m/s; distance=15·4=60 m.

If it went wrong. If the car accelerates, multiplying one reported speed by the full time need not give the distance.

02

Significant figures and dimensional checks

Meaning

Units can reject a wrong equation: terms being added must share dimensions. Report precision supported by measurements, but retain extra digits during intermediate calculations.

First move

Check dimensions before arithmetic. For multiplication or division, use the fewest significant figures in the inputs; for addition or subtraction, use the least precise decimal place.

Worked reasoning

A length 2.0 m divided by 3.00 s is 0.67 m/s to two significant figures. Adding 12.3 m and 0.456 m gives 12.8 m to one decimal place.

Your turn

A formula claims distance equals speed plus acceleration times time. Is it dimensionally valid?

Reveal answer and reasoning

No. Both terms on the right have units of speed, not distance.

If it went wrong. Dimensional agreement is necessary but does not prove that the physical model is valid.

03

Vectors and signed components

Meaning

Direction is part of a vector answer. Components are signed projections on stated axes; the resultant is found by summing components before taking a magnitude.

First move

Draw axes and the vector. Resolve each arrow using the angle measured from the chosen axis; then add x with x and y with y. Use the signs to choose the final quadrant.

Worked reasoning

A 3 N eastward force and a 4 N northward force have components (3,4) N. The resultant is 5 N at 53.1° north of east.

Your turn

Combine 6 N west and 8 N north. Give magnitude and direction.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Components are (−6,8) N; magnitude 10 N, at 53.1° north of west.

If it went wrong. Adding 6+8 gives 14 N only for forces along the same line in the same direction.

04

Position, displacement, and speed

Meaning

Displacement is the change in position and can be negative or zero. Distance is the length of the path. Average velocity uses displacement; average speed uses distance.

First move

Mark start and finish on an axis. Calculate displacement with signs, and total path length with absolute segment lengths. Divide each by the same elapsed time.

Worked reasoning

A runner goes 40 m east and 10 m west in 10 s. Displacement is 30 m east and distance is 50 m. Average velocity is 3 m/s east; average speed is 5 m/s.

Your turn

A walker goes 5 m right, then 5 m left, in 4 s. Find average velocity and average speed.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Displacement is zero, so average velocity is 0; distance is 10 m, so average speed is 2.5 m/s.

If it went wrong. An out-and-back trip can have zero average velocity while motion and nonzero average speed occurred.

05

Read motion graphs before equations

Meaning

A position-time slope gives velocity. A velocity-time slope gives acceleration, while signed area under a velocity-time graph gives displacement.

First move

Read both axis labels and units first. For a segment use rise over run. For area split into rectangles or triangles, preserving the sign below the time axis.

Worked reasoning

Velocity rises uniformly from 0 to 6 m/s during 4 s. The triangular area is (1/2)·4·6=12 m of displacement.

Your turn

Velocity is −2 m/s for 3 s. Find displacement and distance traveled.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Signed area gives −6 m displacement; distance is 6 m.

If it went wrong. The area under a position-time curve does not generally represent displacement. Use its slope instead.

06

Constant acceleration as a model

Meaning

These three relations describe one-dimensional motion only while acceleration is constant over the interval. Signs follow a chosen axis, not the everyday word “slowing.”

First move

Choose positive direction, list known quantities, and choose the relation that omits the unwanted unknown. If motion changes regime, solve one interval at a time.

Worked reasoning

From rest with a=3 m/s² for 4 s, v=12 m/s and displacement=(1/2)·3·16=24 m.

Your turn

A cart starts at 5 m/s and accelerates at −1 m/s² for 3 s. Find final velocity and displacement.

Reveal answer and reasoning

v=5−3=2 m/s; displacement=5·3−(1/2)·9=10.5 m.

If it went wrong. Using these equations across a change in acceleration silently assumes one constant value.

07

Free fall and the turning point

Meaning

With upward positive and negligible air drag, free-fall acceleration is −g throughout the flight, including at the highest point where vertical velocity is momentarily zero.

First move

State the launch height and target height. Use v=0 only at the top, not a=0. A return-time symmetry holds only for the same launch and landing height.

Worked reasoning

Thrown upward at 20 m/s with g=10 m/s², a ball reaches its highest point after 2 s and gains 20 m of height.

Your turn

A ball is thrown upward at 10 m/s. With g=10 m/s², how long until the top and how high above launch?

Reveal answer and reasoning

At the top 0=10−10t, so t=1 s; height=10·1−5·1²=5 m.

If it went wrong. A zero velocity at one instant does not mean zero acceleration.

08

Projectile motion in two axes

Meaning

In ideal projectile motion, horizontal velocity is constant and vertical acceleration is downward. The two axes share the same time, which joins their separate equations.

First move

Resolve launch velocity, solve the vertical equation for the time at the specified target height, and only then use horizontal displacement. Do not assume landing at launch height unless stated.

Worked reasoning

A ball leaves a 20 m ledge horizontally at 8 m/s. With g=10 m/s² it falls for 2 s and lands 16 m horizontally from the ledge.

Your turn

From a 45 m ledge, a ball launches horizontally at 6 m/s. With g=10 m/s², find flight time and range.

Reveal answer and reasoning

45=(1/2)·10t² gives t=3 s; range=6·3=18 m.

If it went wrong. The horizontal equation gives no flight time by itself; the height condition provides it.

09

Relative velocity and moving frames

Meaning

A velocity value depends on the reference frame. Velocities add as vectors, so opposing motion subtracts through signs rather than through a new rule.

First move

Label each velocity “object relative to reference.” Make the middle reference match before adding, and check direction in the ground frame.

Worked reasoning

A person walks 2 m/s east relative to a train moving 10 m/s east relative to ground. The person moves 12 m/s east relative to ground.

Your turn

The person instead walks 2 m/s west relative to the same train. What is the ground-frame velocity?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Take east positive: −2+10=8 m/s east.

If it went wrong. Subtracting speeds without naming reference frames can reverse the answer.

PEARSON / 02

Forces and constrained motion

10

Newton laws and the system boundary

Meaning

Acceleration is caused by the net external force on the chosen object or system. Zero net force means constant velocity, which may be nonzero.

First move

Name the body under study. List only forces acting on that body, pick axes, then write a separate signed force equation for each axis.

Worked reasoning

An 18 N force acts right on a 4 kg cart while 6 N acts left. Net force is 12 N right, so acceleration is 3 m/s² right.

Your turn

A 5 kg object has 25 N right and 10 N left. Find acceleration.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Net force 15 N right gives 3 m/s² right.

If it went wrong. The equal-and-opposite reaction force acts on another body, so it does not cancel a force on this body.

11

Free-body diagrams and normal force

Meaning

A free-body diagram is an inventory of real external forces on one isolated body. Weight acts downward; a surface normal acts perpendicular to contact, not always upward.

First move

Replace the body with a dot or box. Draw weight, contact forces, tension, and applied forces from their actual sources. Resolve tilted forces before writing equations.

Worked reasoning

A 5 kg mass hangs at rest from a light string. With g=10 m/s², tension is 50 N because vertical acceleration is zero.

Your turn

A 4 kg box rests on a horizontal floor with no other vertical force. What normal force acts, using g=10?

Reveal answer and reasoning

N−mg=0, so N=40 N upward.

If it went wrong. N=mg is a result of this particular vertical balance, not a definition valid for all surfaces.

Optional targeted videoIntro to Forces & Newton's Second Law ↗

12

Equilibrium with forces in two dimensions

Meaning

Static equilibrium and constant-velocity motion both require zero acceleration. In two dimensions, horizontal and vertical force components must each balance.

First move

Resolve every angled force, then solve two component equations. Check whether the resulting magnitudes can be drawn in the stated directions.

Worked reasoning

A point has forces 3 N right and 4 N up. A third force of 5 N down-left, with components (−3,−4) N, makes equilibrium.

Your turn

For 5 N right and 12 N up, what single force balances them?

Reveal answer and reasoning

A force (−5,−12) N, magnitude 13 N, directed down-left.

If it went wrong. Balancing magnitudes alone misses the direction required by each component equation.

13

Friction and incline decisions

Meaning

Static friction supplies only the amount needed up to a maximum. Kinetic friction approximately equals μkN and opposes relative sliding. On an incline, weight splits along and across the plane.

First move

Choose an axis along the plane. Predict actual or impending slip, draw friction opposite it, compute N from the perpendicular balance, then solve along the plane.

Worked reasoning

A 5 kg block slides down a 30° incline with μk=0.20 and g=10. Downhill gravity is 25 N, N≈43.3 N, friction≈8.66 N uphill, and acceleration≈3.27 m/s² downhill.

Your turn

A 2 kg block rests on a 30° incline with μs=0.8 and g=10. Is rest possible?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Downhill pull is 10 N; maximum static friction is 0.8·(20 cos30°)≈13.9 N. Rest is possible; actual friction is 10 N uphill.

If it went wrong. Do not automatically set static friction equal to μsN; that is only its limiting value.

14

Tension and connected bodies

Meaning

A taut massless string over an ideal pulley gives the connected bodies equal acceleration magnitudes. Tension is common only under the ideal-string, ideal-pulley model.

First move

Draw one diagram per mass, choose consistent positive directions along the string, and write both Newton equations before eliminating tension.

Worked reasoning

For hanging masses 2 kg and 3 kg with g=10, acceleration is (3−2)10/5=2 m/s²; the 3 kg mass moves down and the string tension is 24 N.

Your turn

For 1 kg and 4 kg masses with g=10, find acceleration and tension.

Reveal answer and reasoning

a=(4−1)10/5=6 m/s². For the 1 kg side, T−10=1·6, so T=16 N.

If it went wrong. The tension is not the heavier weight while the system accelerates.

15

Springs and elastic force

Meaning

An ideal spring exerts a restoring force proportional to displacement from its unstretched length. The energy stored depends on the square of that displacement.

First move

Mark the equilibrium or unstretched position and the displacement sign. Use the force law for direction and the energy relation for stored work.

Worked reasoning

A spring with k=200 N/m stretched by 0.10 m pulls back with 20 N and stores (1/2)·200·0.10²=1 J.

Your turn

For k=100 N/m and stretch 0.20 m, find restoring-force magnitude and stored energy.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Force magnitude is 20 N; energy is (1/2)·100·0.20²=2 J.

If it went wrong. Doubling extension doubles force but quadruples stored energy.

16

Circular motion and inward force

Meaning

An object can have constant speed and still accelerate because its velocity direction changes. Centripetal force is the name for the net inward component of real forces.

First move

Draw the circle center, choose inward positive, and identify which real forces have inward components. Use speed at the point of interest, not a force “outward” added to the free-body diagram.

Worked reasoning

At 10 m/s on a radius 5 m circle, centripetal acceleration is 20 m/s². A 2 kg object needs 40 N net inward force.

Your turn

A 1 kg object moves at 6 m/s around a 3 m radius. Find net inward force.

Reveal answer and reasoning

F=mv²/r=1·36/3=12 N inward.

If it went wrong. A circular path does not imply an extra physical “centripetal force” in addition to tension, friction, gravity, or the normal force.

17

Flat and banked curves

Meaning

A flat curve needs static friction to provide inward force. An ideal frictionless bank instead uses the inward component of the normal force.

First move

Draw the force diagram before selecting a formula. The flat-road expression describes the threshold of slip; the bank expression assumes no friction.

Worked reasoning

On a flat curve with μs=0.5, r=20 m, and g=10 m/s², the maximum speed before slipping is √(0.5·20·10)=10 m/s.

Your turn

With μs=0.4, r=25 m, and g=10, find the flat-curve speed limit.

Reveal answer and reasoning

vmax=√(0.4·25·10)=10 m/s.

If it went wrong. The two formulas describe different physical assumptions; a banked curve with friction needs a new force balance.

PEARSON / 03

Energy and momentum

18

Work by a force

Meaning

Work transfers energy through a force component along displacement. Positive work aids motion, negative work opposes it, and a perpendicular force does zero work.

First move

Mark force and displacement arrows. Use the angle between them, not necessarily the incline angle. For variable force, take signed area under force versus displacement.

Worked reasoning

A 20 N force acts at 60° to a 3 m displacement. Its work is 20·3·cos60°=30 J.

Your turn

A 10 N force acts at 120° to a 5 m displacement. Find work.

Reveal answer and reasoning

W=10·5·cos120°=−25 J; this force does negative work.

If it went wrong. Force magnitude times distance misses the sign and the along-path component.

19

The work-energy checkpoint

Meaning

Net work changes kinetic energy. This is useful when the requested speeds and positions matter more than travel time.

First move

Define the initial and final states and include work by every force, with its sign. A constant-speed path has zero net work even if individual forces do work.

Worked reasoning

For a 2 kg body speeding from 3 to 5 m/s, the net work is (1/2)·2·(25−9)=16 J.

Your turn

How much net work accelerates a 1 kg body from rest to 6 m/s?

Reveal answer and reasoning

ΔK=(1/2)·1·36=18 J.

If it went wrong. The theorem uses net work, not just the work of one convenient force.

20

Potential energy and conservation

Meaning

Gravity and ideal springs can store potential energy. Mechanical energy is conserved only when nonconservative external work is zero for the chosen system.

First move

Choose a height zero and a system boundary. Write initial and final kinetic plus potential energies, then place friction or an applied push as nonconservative work.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg mass falls 5 m from rest without drag. With g=10, it loses 100 J gravitational potential energy and gains 100 J kinetic energy, reaching 10 m/s.

Your turn

A 1 kg object starts at rest 2 m above the ground without drag, with g=10. Find speed at ground.

Reveal answer and reasoning

mgh=(1/2)mv² gives v=√(2gh)=√40≈6.32 m/s.

If it went wrong. Energy conservation without a loss term fails when friction converts mechanical energy to thermal energy.

Optional targeted videoConservation Of Mechanical Energy ↗

21

Energy losses and thermal transfer

Meaning

Energy of the larger system remains accounted for, but mechanical energy can decrease when friction or drag converts it to thermal energy.

First move

Compute the change in mechanical energy between states; the missing amount is energy transferred outside the mechanical store. Do not force final speed to match lossless motion.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg mass falls 5 m from rest and reaches 8 m/s with g=10. It loses 100 J potential energy, gains 64 J kinetic energy, and transfers 36 J to other forms.

Your turn

A 1 kg mass drops 2 m from rest and reaches 4 m/s with g=10. How much mechanical energy was dissipated?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Initial potential decrease is 20 J; final kinetic energy is 8 J; dissipated energy is 12 J.

If it went wrong. A negative mechanical-energy change is not missing energy; name the recipient of the transfer.

22

Power is a rate, not energy

Meaning

Power tells how quickly work is done or energy is transferred. One watt is one joule per second; the instantaneous dot-product form uses the along-motion force component.

First move

Decide whether average power over an interval or instantaneous power at one speed is requested. Keep the angle between force and velocity when using Fv.

Worked reasoning

Transferring 600 J in 20 s requires average power 30 W. A 10 N along-motion force at 3 m/s also delivers 30 W instantaneously.

Your turn

A machine transfers 900 J in 30 s. Find average power.

Reveal answer and reasoning

P=900/30=30 W.

If it went wrong. Do not label a joule value as watts; divide by time to obtain a rate.

23

Momentum and system direction

Meaning

Momentum is a vector. The total momentum of a system is a signed or component-wise sum, not the sum of all speed magnitudes.

First move

Choose an axis before multiplying mass and velocity. Add momenta with their signs; in two dimensions, resolve x and y separately.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg cart moving east at 3 m/s has momentum +6 kg·m/s with east positive.

Your turn

A 0.5 kg ball moves west at 4 m/s. Find signed momentum with east positive.

Reveal answer and reasoning

p=0.5·(−4)=−2 kg·m/s, meaning 2 kg·m/s west.

If it went wrong. Kinetic energy cannot substitute for momentum: it is scalar and depends on speed squared.

Optional targeted videoIntro to Momentum ↗

24

Impulse and force-time area

Meaning

Impulse is momentum change and equals signed area under a force-time graph. For the same momentum change, longer contact time means smaller average force magnitude.

First move

Find final minus initial momentum with velocity signs. Divide by contact duration only after determining the signed impulse.

Worked reasoning

A 50 N constant force acts for 0.20 s, delivering 10 N·s. A 2 kg object initially at rest gains 5 m/s in the force direction.

Your turn

A 0.20 kg ball changes from +15 to −5 m/s in 0.04 s. Find average force.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Δp=0.20(−5−15)=−4 N·s; Favg=−4/0.04=−100 N.

If it went wrong. A rebound reverses velocity direction, so subtracting unsigned speeds understates the momentum change.

25

Conservation of momentum

Meaning

The momentum of a chosen system stays constant when net external impulse during the event is negligible. Individual objects can change momentum while the system total remains fixed.

First move

Choose all interacting objects as the system, define a positive axis, and write signed initial and final momentum sums. Check whether an external impact invalidates isolation.

Worked reasoning

A 1 kg cart starts at +4 m/s and a 3 kg cart is still. If the first ends at −2 m/s after interaction, the second must move at +2 m/s because 4=−2+3·2.

Your turn

A 2 kg cart at +5 m/s hits a 3 kg cart at rest. If the first leaves at −1 m/s, find the second final velocity.

Reveal answer and reasoning

10=−2+3v, so v=4 m/s in the positive direction.

If it went wrong. Momentum conservation does not imply kinetic-energy conservation.

26

Collisions and energy bookkeeping

Meaning

In a completely inelastic collision, bodies stick and share a final velocity. Momentum can be conserved while kinetic energy decreases into deformation, sound, and heat.

First move

Write signed momentum first to obtain shared velocity. Then compare initial and final kinetic energies separately; never assume the latter is conserved.

Worked reasoning

A 1 kg cart at 4 m/s sticks to an identical cart at rest. Final speed is 2 m/s. Initial kinetic energy is 8 J; final kinetic energy is 4 J.

Your turn

A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s sticks to a 1 kg cart at rest. Find final speed and kinetic-energy loss.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Momentum gives v=2 m/s. Initial K=9 J and final K=6 J, so 3 J leaves the kinetic store.

If it went wrong. Using an elastic-collision kinetic-energy equation for bodies that stick contradicts the event description.

27

Center of mass as a weighted position

Meaning

Center of mass weights each position by its mass. It helps describe the overall motion of a group and lies closer to a larger mass in a two-object system.

First move

Choose one origin and coordinate direction; multiply every mass by its signed position, sum, and divide by total mass.

Worked reasoning

A 1 kg mass at x=0 and 2 kg mass at x=3 m have center of mass x=(0+6)/3=2 m.

Your turn

A 2 kg mass is at x=0 and a 1 kg mass at x=6 m. Locate the center of mass.

Reveal answer and reasoning

xcm=(2·0+1·6)/3=2 m, closer to the 2 kg mass.

If it went wrong. A plain average of positions works only when masses are equal.

PEARSON / 04

Calculus bridge

28

Calculus connects motion and accumulation

Meaning

A derivative gives an instantaneous rate; an integral accumulates signed change. These relations extend the constant-acceleration equations to changing acceleration.

First move

Check the variable and units before differentiating or integrating. State interval endpoints, preserve negative velocity contributions, and include an integration constant when finding a position function.

Worked reasoning

If x(t)=2t²+3t metres, then v(t)=4t+3 m/s and a(t)=4 m/s². At t=2 s, velocity is 11 m/s.

Your turn

If v(t)=3t² m/s, find displacement from t=0 to 2 s.

Reveal answer and reasoning

The integral is [t³] from 0 to 2, giving 8 m.

If it went wrong. Integrating speed instead of signed velocity gives distance, which differs when direction reverses.

TRANSFER

Mixed practice

Choose the model before calculating. Cover the answer until you have a result with units and a reason.

  1. Convert

    A vehicle travels 90 km/h for 8 s at constant speed. How far?

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Convert first: 90/3.6=25 m/s. Constant speed makes distance=25·8=200 m. An instantaneous speed alone would not be enough.

  2. Vector

    A force is 8 N east and another is 6 N north. Find resultant magnitude.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    The perpendicular components combine by Pythagoras: sqrt(8²+6²)=10 N. Directly adding 8 and 6 would assume the same direction.

  3. Graph

    A velocity is +4 m/s for 3 s and −2 m/s for 2 s. Find displacement and distance.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    The signed areas are +12 and −4 m, so displacement is 8 m. Absolute areas give distance 12+4=16 m.

  4. Acceleration

    A cart starts from rest and reaches 12 m/s in 4 s at constant acceleration. Find a and displacement.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    a=(12−0)/4=3 m/s². Average velocity is (0+12)/2=6 m/s only because acceleration is constant; displacement=24 m.

  5. Projectile

    A ball launches horizontally at 5 m/s from 20 m, with g=10. Find range.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Vertical fall sets time: 20=(1/2)·10t², hence t=2 s. Horizontal speed stays 5 m/s, so range=10 m.

  6. Forces

    A 3 kg body has +15 N and −6 N along one axis. Find a.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    The net force is +9 N. Newton’s second law gives a=+9/3=+3 m/s², in the positive direction.

  7. Friction

    A 2 kg mass on a horizontal floor has μk=0.25 and g=10. Find kinetic friction.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    With no other vertical force, N=mg=20 N. Kinetic friction is 0.25·20=5 N, directed opposite relative sliding.

  8. Turn

    A 1 kg mass moves at 4 m/s on radius 2 m. Find net inward force.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    The required inward acceleration is v²/r=8 m/s². Net inward force is ma=8 N; identify the real force that supplies it.

  9. Energy

    A 2 kg object speeds from 1 to 3 m/s. Find net work.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Work-energy gives ΔK=(1/2)·2·(9−1)=8 J. It is the work of all forces together, not necessarily one applied force.

  10. Momentum

    A 1 kg cart at +6 m/s sticks to a 2 kg cart at rest. Find shared velocity.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    If external impulse is negligible, initial momentum is +6 kg·m/s. The joined 3 kg mass moves at +2 m/s; kinetic energy need not be conserved.

  11. Impulse

    A 0.5 kg ball changes velocity from +4 to −2 m/s. Find impulse.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Final minus initial momentum is 0.5(−2−4)=−3 N·s. The negative sign identifies the impulse direction.

  12. Calculus

    For v(t)=2t m/s, find displacement from 0 to 3 s.

    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Integrate signed velocity: ∫₀³2t dt=[t²]₀³=9 m. Here velocity is nonnegative, so distance happens to be the same.
