INDEPENDENT BIOLOGY PROGRAM

Biology decision practice

A focused route with optional video only when motion or spatial structure helps.

HOW TO USECore model → cue → decision → transfer

Read, decide, test, and correct. Each lesson stands on its own; a video is optional and earns its place only when motion or spatial structure clarifies the idea.

PEARSON

The learning mechanism

Keep. A short concept explanation, an illustrative demonstration when useful, immediate practice, and feedback tied to the learner's error.

Repair. A large menu creates selection work before learning begins. Repeated, overlapping lessons dilute the main idea; a payment interruption breaks a reasoning chain. This program provides one default route through a compact set of concepts and no required video or purchase step.

Use. Read the core model. Predict the answer to the Try it prompt before reading its explanation. If the prediction is wrong, name the mistaken rule, revisit the worked decision, and try a fresh variation. Watch the linked video only when the visual process would help resolve the error.

Exit rule. Move on when you can explain the mechanism in your own words, solve a changed case, and explain why a tempting wrong answer fails. Revisit a missed idea after other lessons and again later; a familiar example alone is not mastery.

Reusable design. For any subject, the author supplies a core model, a question cue, one worked decision, a new transfer prompt, error-specific feedback, and at most one justified video. The text must remain sufficient if the video is unavailable.

Learning route

Investigating a biological claim

Core model. A hypothesis explains an observation and must allow a test that could show it is wrong. A prediction states what should be observed if the hypothesis holds. In a controlled experiment, change one independent variable, measure a dependent variable, keep other conditions comparable, and repeat observations. Correlation alone cannot identify a cause.

Question cue. When asked whether evidence proves a claim, find the control, sample size, and possible confounders before accepting the conclusion.

Worked decision. A fertilizer group grows taller than an untreated group, but it also receives more light. Light is a confounder. Equalize light and repeat the comparison before attributing the difference to fertilizer.

TRY FIRST

A drug-treated group improves, but the untreated group was younger. What must change in the design?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Use comparable groups, preferably random assignment, and the same measurement schedule. Age could explain the observed difference.

Visual data decision. Read the two CO₂ series and separate an observed trend from a causal claim.

Read the graph. Atmospheric and oceanic CO₂ both trend upward, although individual readings fluctuate.

TRY FIRST

What do the two series show? Does their joint rise alone prove that atmospheric CO₂ caused the ocean change?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Both series rise over time. The graph shows an association; a causal claim needs corroborating experiments or other independent evidence.

Common error. Short-term fluctuations do not erase a long-term trend, and correlation alone is not proof of cause.

Water and chemical interactions

Core model. Water is polar because oxygen attracts shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen. Hydrogen bonds between water molecules explain cohesion, high heat capacity, and much of water's solvent behavior. Ionic and polar solutes interact favorably with water; nonpolar molecules tend to cluster away from it. pH tracks hydrogen-ion concentration on a logarithmic scale.

Visual data decision. Use the trend line to interpret pH despite noisy measurements.

Read the graph. Ocean pH trends downward. Lower pH means greater acidity, even while pH remains above 7.

TRY FIRST

Is the water becoming less acidic because its pH stays above 7?

Reveal answer and reasoning

No. The pH is still alkaline, but a decrease in pH means increased hydrogen-ion concentration and greater acidity relative to before.

Common error. Do not confuse an alkaline pH with an absence of acidification over time.

Question cue. Classify the bond or molecule first; then predict whether water will surround it or exclude it.

Worked decision. A small amount of acid lowers pH from 7 to 6. Hydrogen-ion concentration increases tenfold, not by one percent.

TRY FIRST

Why can water rise through a narrow plant vessel?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Cohesion keeps water molecules connected, while adhesion to the vessel wall helps the column move upward. Evaporation from leaves supplies the main pull in a plant.

Molecules that build and fuel life

Core model. Carbohydrates contain sugars used for rapid energy and structural polysaccharides. Triglycerides store concentrated energy, while phospholipids form membranes. Proteins are amino-acid chains; folding gives them specific binding and catalytic functions. DNA and RNA are nucleotide polymers that store and express sequence information. The identity of monomers, bonds, and functional groups predicts behavior better than a name alone.

Question cue. Identify building blocks and role before naming the molecule class.

Worked decision. Heating can disrupt the weak interactions that maintain an enzyme's shape. Its activity may fall even when the peptide backbone remains intact.

TRY FIRST

A molecule has a phosphate-containing head and two fatty-acid tails. What role is most likely?

Reveal answer and reasoning

It is a phospholipid suited to a membrane: its hydrophilic head faces water and its hydrophobic tails face inward.

Cells as organized systems

Core model. Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus; eukaryotic cells compartmentalize many reactions in organelles. Ribosomes make proteins. The nucleus stores most eukaryotic DNA; the rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus process many exported proteins. Mitochondria convert chemical energy into ATP, and chloroplasts capture light energy in plants and algae. A structure's job follows from its location and membrane boundaries.

Question cue. Trace where a product is made, modified, packaged, and delivered rather than memorizing isolated organelle names.

Worked decision. A secreted protein is translated on a ribosome attached to rough ER, processed through ER and Golgi, and released when a vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane.

TRY FIRST

A cell loses functional Golgi apparatus. Which task is disrupted first: DNA replication or sorting secreted proteins?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Sorting secreted proteins. DNA replication primarily occurs in the nucleus; the Golgi modifies and routes many exported products.

Membranes, gradients, and osmosis

Core model. The phospholipid bilayer allows small nonpolar molecules through relatively easily but restricts most ions and large polar molecules. A channel or carrier can move a solute down its electrochemical gradient without ATP. Moving it against that gradient requires an energy source. Water moves across a selectively permeable membrane toward the side with greater effective concentration of nonpenetrating solute until forces balance.

Question cue. Ask three questions: What crosses? In which direction relative to its gradient? Does a protein or energy source participate?

Worked decision. An animal cell in a hypertonic solution loses water and shrinks. A plant cell also loses water, but its wall preserves external shape as the membrane pulls away.

TRY FIRST

A cell imports sodium from lower outside concentration to higher inside concentration. Is simple diffusion sufficient?

Reveal answer and reasoning

No. Net movement against the concentration gradient requires an energy-coupled transport mechanism; the full electrochemical gradient must be considered for ions.

Enzymes and energy coupling

Core model. Enzymes lower activation energy by stabilizing a reaction pathway; they do not change the reaction's overall free-energy difference or equilibrium. Temperature, pH, substrate concentration, inhibitors, and active-site shape affect rate. ATP hydrolysis can be coupled to an unfavorable process through a shared intermediate. A favorable reaction releases free energy, but that energy must be captured to do useful work.

Question cue. Separate reaction speed from energetic favorability. A catalyst changes the first, not the second.

Worked decision. At low substrate concentration, adding substrate can increase an enzyme's rate. At saturation, most active sites are occupied, so more substrate adds little until enzyme amount rises.

TRY FIRST

A drug occupies an enzyme's active site. Why may a high substrate concentration reduce its effect?

Reveal answer and reasoning

For a reversible competitive inhibitor, substrate and inhibitor compete for the same site; more substrate can outcompete the inhibitor. This does not apply to every inhibition mechanism.

Cellular respiration

Core model. Respiration transfers energy from fuel to ATP through linked oxidation–reduction reactions. Glycolysis breaks glucose into pyruvate and yields a small net ATP gain. Pyruvate oxidation and the citric acid cycle release carbon dioxide and load electron carriers. The electron transport chain uses those electrons to pump protons; ATP synthase uses the gradient. Oxygen accepts electrons at the end in aerobic respiration.

Question cue. Follow carbon, electrons, and the proton gradient separately; do not treat ATP as the direct product of every stage.

Worked decision. If oxygen is unavailable, the electron transport chain cannot pass electrons to its terminal acceptor. A cell may regenerate NAD+ by fermentation to keep glycolysis running, but ATP yield is much lower.

TRY FIRST

A toxin blocks ATP synthase while electron carriers still deliver electrons. What happens to the proton gradient initially?

Reveal answer and reasoning

It rises initially because protons are pumped out but cannot return efficiently through ATP synthase. Electron flow later slows as the gradient becomes difficult to increase.

Photosynthesis

Core model. Light reactions in thylakoid membranes absorb light, split water, release oxygen, and make ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle in the stroma uses ATP and NADPH to reduce carbon dioxide into carbohydrate precursors. Light supplies energy; it does not directly become matter. Most of a plant's new dry mass comes from carbon fixed from atmospheric carbon dioxide.

Question cue. Track the source of atoms: released oxygen comes from water, while sugar carbon comes from carbon dioxide.

Worked decision. Blocking photosystem II prevents normal water splitting, so oxygen release falls. Electron flow and production of ATP and NADPH are also impaired.

TRY FIRST

A plant is given water whose oxygen atoms are labeled. Where should the label appear first: released oxygen gas or newly fixed sugar carbon?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Released oxygen gas. The oxygen gas produced during the light reactions comes from water.

Signals and homeostasis

Core model. A signal works only when a target cell has a matching receptor and a way to transduce the message. Cascades can amplify signals and change enzyme activity or gene expression. Negative feedback counteracts a disturbance and stabilizes a variable around a range; positive feedback amplifies a process until an external event ends it. Homeostasis is dynamic regulation, not an unchanging number.

Question cue. Locate the receptor, the intracellular response, and the feedback direction.

Worked decision. After blood glucose rises, insulin promotes uptake and storage; as glucose falls, the stimulus for insulin release weakens. This is negative feedback.

TRY FIRST

A hormone circulates through the whole body, but only some cells respond. Why?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Only cells with an appropriate functional receptor and downstream signaling machinery can produce the specific response.

Chromosomes and cell division

Core model. DNA replication precedes division and produces two sister chromatids per chromosome without doubling the chromosome count. Mitosis separates sister chromatids and usually preserves chromosome number in daughter cells. Meiosis has one DNA replication followed by two divisions: homologous chromosomes separate first, sister chromatids second. Crossing over and independent assortment generate new combinations of inherited alleles.

Question cue. Count chromosomes by centromeres, distinguish homologues from sisters, and identify which separation occurs.

Worked decision. A diploid cell with four chromosomes has eight chromatids after replication. After meiosis I, each daughter has two replicated chromosomes; after meiosis II, each gamete has two unreplicated chromosomes.

TRY FIRST

A species has six chromosomes in a body cell. How many chromosomes are expected in a normal gamete?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Three. Meiosis halves the diploid chromosome number; each gamete receives one member of each homologous pair.

Inheritance and probability

Core model. An allele is a variant of a gene. A heterozygote carries two different alleles; a dominant allele determines phenotype in a complete-dominance heterozygote but is not inherently more common. Each gamete receives one allele per gene. For independent events, multiply probabilities; for mutually exclusive outcomes, add them. Linked genes and nonstandard dominance can change simple expected ratios.

Question cue. Write parental genotypes and possible gametes before using a familiar ratio.

Worked decision. In Aa × Aa, offspring genotypes are AA, Aa, Aa, aa. Under complete dominance, the recessive phenotype has probability one quarter.

TRY FIRST

In AaBb × aabb, with unlinked genes, what is the probability of aabb?

Reveal answer and reasoning

One quarter. The chance of aa is one half and the chance of bb is one half; multiply them because the genes assort independently.

DNA, RNA, and protein

Core model. DNA replication copies each original strand into a complementary new strand. During transcription, RNA polymerase reads a DNA template and makes RNA 5′ to 3′. During translation, a ribosome reads mRNA codons from a start codon; tRNAs deliver amino acids until a stop codon is reached. Sequence changes can alter a protein, but the effect depends on where the change occurs and whether it changes an amino acid or regulation.

Question cue. State which molecule is the template, the direction of synthesis, and the reading frame before converting a sequence.

Worked decision. DNA template 3′-TAC GGA ATT-5′ produces mRNA 5′-AUG CCU UAA-3′. Translation yields methionine–proline; UAA is a stop signal, not an amino acid.

TRY FIRST

One base is inserted immediately after the start codon in a coding sequence. What broad effect is likely?

Reveal answer and reasoning

A frameshift changes downstream triplets, often altering many amino acids and possibly introducing an early stop, unless another change restores the frame.

Gene regulation and biotechnology

Core model. Cells with the same DNA can differ because they express different genes. Regulatory proteins, chromatin accessibility, RNA processing, and RNA stability influence the amount of protein produced. In bacteria, related genes may be regulated together in an operon. Biotechnology uses sequence-specific tools to copy, compare, or alter DNA; an observed DNA change still needs functional evidence before a causal claim.

Question cue. When two cells differ, ask whether the DNA sequence differs or whether the same DNA is being used differently.

Worked decision. A liver cell and a neuron can carry the same genome yet produce different proteins because their regulatory programs activate different genes.

TRY FIRST

A variant is found in patients and healthy people. Can its presence alone prove it causes disease?

Reveal answer and reasoning

No. Compare frequencies and inheritance, test a plausible mechanism, and consider other variants and environmental factors.

Evolution by changing allele frequencies

Core model. Populations evolve when heritable allele frequencies change across generations. Natural selection requires variation and differences in reproductive success; mutation supplies new variants. Genetic drift changes frequencies by chance, especially in small populations. Gene flow moves alleles between populations. Neither selection nor mutation occurs because organisms consciously need a trait.

Question cue. Identify the source of variation and the process changing its frequency.

Worked decision. A few resistant bacteria survive an antibiotic and reproduce. Resistance rises because heritable resistant variants leave more descendants; the drug did not instruct bacteria to create that variant.

TRY FIRST

A storm randomly leaves a few survivors in a small population. Which mechanism is most evident?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Genetic drift through a population bottleneck. Survival was random with respect to the alleles being tracked.

Ecosystems and energy flow

Core model. Producers capture external energy; consumers and decomposers transfer matter and energy through food webs. Matter cycles among organisms and the environment, while usable energy enters and is dissipated as heat. Only a fraction of energy at one trophic level becomes biomass available to the next. Changes to one species may cause indirect effects through several paths in a food web.

Question cue. Separate energy flow from matter cycling, then trace more than one possible path through the web.

Worked decision. If a predator declines, its prey may increase and consume more plants. The final plant response depends on other predators, resources, and competition, so the direction is a hypothesis to test.

TRY FIRST

Why can a food web support fewer large predators than producers?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Energy is lost as heat and used in metabolism at each transfer, leaving less energy available as biomass at higher trophic levels.

Organ systems as transport and exchange

Core model. Multicellular organisms need specialized exchange surfaces and transport systems because diffusion alone is too slow over long distances. Lungs exchange gases across thin moist surfaces; circulation carries gases and nutrients; kidneys regulate water, ions, and wastes. Endocrine and nervous signals coordinate responses. For every system, follow a substance from entry through transport to use or removal.

Question cue. Trace the pathway and distinguish the exchange surface from the transport mechanism.

Worked decision. Oxygen diffuses from alveoli into capillary blood down its partial-pressure gradient, binds hemoglobin, and is delivered by circulation to tissues.

TRY FIRST

Why do flatworms tolerate diffusion better than a large thick-bodied animal?

Reveal answer and reasoning

A flat body gives most cells a short diffusion distance to the environment; a large thick body needs internal transport and specialized exchange surfaces.

Mixed transfer check

Answer these without rereading the route. Explain the causal step in each answer, then compare your reasoning with the answer key.

Challenge A nutrient supplement seems to improve growth, but only well-watered plants received it. What can the result establish?

Challenge A solution changes from pH 8 to pH 6. What happens to hydrogen-ion concentration?

Challenge A cell imports a charged solute against its gradient while ATP production is blocked. Which observation would test whether transport depends on ATP?

Challenge A mutant enzyme binds substrate normally but cannot convert it to product. What does this separate?

Challenge A plant in light releases oxygen whose atoms match labeled water. Which photosynthetic step is supported?

Challenge A diploid cell has eight chromosomes. After DNA replication but before mitosis, how many chromosomes and chromatids does it have?

Challenge Two parents are Aa for a recessive disorder. What is the probability that their first two children are both affected?

Challenge A coding DNA sequence changes, yet the protein sequence is unchanged. Give a plausible explanation.

Challenge An antibiotic is applied and the frequency of a resistance allele rises. What additional evidence supports selection rather than chance?

Challenge A predator is removed, but plant abundance does not rise or fall as predicted. What should the model include?

PEARSON

Answer key

A nutrient supplement seems to improve growth, but only well-watered plants received it. What can the result establish? It establishes an association in this design, not a supplement effect. Water and supplement changed together; keep water equal and repeat with comparable groups.

A solution changes from pH 8 to pH 6. What happens to hydrogen-ion concentration? It becomes one hundred times higher because each unit on the pH scale represents a tenfold change.

A cell imports a charged solute against its gradient while ATP production is blocked. Which observation would test whether transport depends on ATP? Compare solute uptake before and after ATP inhibition while checking cell viability and the solute gradient. Reduced uptake supports ATP dependence, though other energy sources must still be considered.

A mutant enzyme binds substrate normally but cannot convert it to product. What does this separate? Binding and catalysis are distinct. The active site may still recognize substrate while residues required for the chemical step fail.

A plant in light releases oxygen whose atoms match labeled water. Which photosynthetic step is supported? Water splitting in the light reactions is supported; the released oxygen does not come from carbon dioxide.

A diploid cell has eight chromosomes. After DNA replication but before mitosis, how many chromosomes and chromatids does it have? Eight chromosomes and sixteen chromatids. Replication doubles DNA molecules, not centromere-defined chromosome count.

Two parents are Aa for a recessive disorder. What is the probability that their first two children are both affected? One sixteenth if births are independent: one quarter for each affected child, then multiply. A prior birth does not change the next child's genotype probability.

A coding DNA sequence changes, yet the protein sequence is unchanged. Give a plausible explanation. The altered codon may specify the same amino acid. A change outside the translated region or a change not expressed in that cell is another possibility; locate the variant before deciding.

An antibiotic is applied and the frequency of a resistance allele rises. What additional evidence supports selection rather than chance? Show that the allele was heritable, carriers survived or reproduced more under treatment, and comparable untreated populations did not show the same change.

A predator is removed, but plant abundance does not rise or fall as predicted. What should the model include? Other predators, prey food choices, resource limits, and time delays can offset the simple chain. Measure these paths rather than assuming a single linear response.
