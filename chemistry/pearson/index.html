UNIT 01

Matter, measurement, and particles

Classify the material before calculating

Question cue. A question says pure substance, mixture, element, or compound.

Meaning. A pure substance has a fixed chemical identity. Elements contain one kind of atom; compounds contain elements in a fixed ratio. Mixtures can vary in composition and are separated physically.

First move. Ask whether composition can vary. If yes, classify the mixture as uniform or nonuniform. If fixed, decide whether one or several elements are chemically bonded.

Worked case. Air is a uniform mixture: nitrogen, oxygen, and other gases coexist in variable amounts. Water is a compound because its molecules have a fixed H:O atom ratio of 2:1.

Try this before revealing the answer

Is salt water a compound or a mixture? Can filtration remove the dissolved salt?

Reveal answer and reasoning

It is a uniform mixture. Ordinary filtration removes suspended particles, not dissolved ions; evaporation or distillation can separate the components.

If stuck. A clear sample is not necessarily pure. Transparency does not determine composition.

Convert units and preserve measurement meaning

Question cue. Numbers arrive in unlike units or with different precision.

Meaning. A conversion factor equals one and changes the form of a quantity, not the quantity itself. Significant figures report measurement precision; intermediate rounding can spoil the final answer.

First move. Write the starting unit and cancel it visibly. Apply the fewest significant figures for multiplication or division and the least precise decimal place for addition or subtraction.

Worked case. A density of 1.20 g/mL is 1.20×10³ kg/m³ because 1 g=10⁻³ kg and 1 mL=10⁻⁶ m³. Both units and numerical size pass the check.

Try this before revealing the answer

Convert 250 mL to L. If 2.50 g dissolves in that volume, what is mass concentration in g/L?

Reveal answer and reasoning

250 mL=0.250 L, so 2.50/0.250=10.0 g/L. The three significant figures come from the stated measurements.

If stuck. Do not convert only the number. Carry and cancel units at every step.

Read atoms, isotopes, and ions

Question cue. A symbol has a mass number or charge, or a question asks for particles.

Meaning. Atomic number Z counts protons and defines the element. Mass number A counts protons plus neutrons. An ion differs from the neutral atom only in electron count; isotopes differ in neutron count.

First move. Read Z and A first. Set protons=Z and neutrons=A−Z. Let zion be the signed ionic charge number, such as +3 or −1; then electrons=Z−zion.

Worked case. For ⁵⁶Fe³⁺, Z=26: it has 26 protons, 30 neutrons, and 23 electrons.

Try this before revealing the answer

How many protons, neutrons, and electrons are in ³⁵Cl⁻?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Chlorine has Z=17. The ion has 17 protons, 35−17=18 neutrons, and 18 electrons because its charge is −1.

If stuck. A positive ion lost electrons; its nucleus did not lose protons.

Bridge mass, moles, and particles

Question cue. The question changes among grams, moles, formula units, or atoms.

Meaning. One mole is a count of 6.022×10²³ specified entities. Molar mass converts grams to moles. A chemical formula then converts entities of compound into constituent atom counts.

First move. Name the counted entity. Compute formula molar mass from its atom subscripts, convert mass to moles, then apply Avogadro number or a formula subscript only if requested.

Worked case. 18.0 g of water is about 1.00 mol of H₂O molecules, or 6.02×10²³ molecules. It contains 2.00 mol of H atoms.

Try this before revealing the answer

How many oxygen atoms are in 0.250 mol of CO₂?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Each molecule has two O atoms: 0.500 mol O atoms, or about 3.01×10²³ O atoms.

If stuck. A mole of CO₂ molecules is not a mole of O atoms; the subscript matters.

UNIT 02

Formulas and reacting amounts

Build a neutral ionic formula

Question cue. Ion names or charges are given and a formula is required.

Meaning. Ionic formulas use the smallest whole-number ratio of ions that gives zero net charge. Polyatomic ions stay intact when their group is repeated.

First move. Write each ion with its charge. Choose a ratio that cancels charge, reduce it, and add parentheses only when more than one polyatomic ion is needed.

Worked case. Al³⁺ and SO₄²⁻ give Al₂(SO₄)₃: two Al ions contribute +6 while three sulfate ions contribute −6.

Try this before revealing the answer

Combine Ca²⁺ and NO₃⁻ into a neutral formula.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Ca(NO₃)₂. Two nitrate ions provide −2 to balance one calcium ion at +2.

If stuck. Changing the O subscript inside nitrate changes the ion; balance whole ions instead.

Derive an empirical formula from composition

Question cue. Percent composition or masses must become an atom ratio.

Meaning. Percent by mass is not an atom ratio. Convert each element mass to moles, divide by the smallest amount, and multiply all ratios together when a half or third remains.

First move. For percentages, assume 100 g. Divide each mass by its atomic mass, normalize the mole amounts, and check that a proposed molecular mass is an integer multiple of the empirical mass.

Worked case. A sample at 40.0% C, 6.7% H, and 53.3% O gives about 3.33:6.7:3.33 mol, hence CH₂O. At 180 g/mol the molecular formula is C₆H₁₂O₆.

Try this before revealing the answer

A compound has empirical formula CH₂O and molecular molar mass 60 g/mol. Find its molecular formula.

Reveal answer and reasoning

The empirical mass is about 30 g/mol. Multiplying the subscripts by 60/30=2 gives C₂H₄O₂.

If stuck. Rounding 1.5 to 2 distorts composition; multiply every normalized ratio by 2.

Balance without changing chemical identity

Question cue. A skeletal equation is given.

Meaning. Coefficients specify how many formula units or molecules react. Balancing conserves each element and, for ionic equations, total charge. Subscripts belong to the substance and must remain fixed.

First move. Confirm formulas, then adjust coefficients. Tally each element on both sides and reduce to the smallest integer set.

Worked case. For propane combustion, C₃H₈+5O₂→3CO₂+4H₂O. The two sides each have 3 C, 8 H, and 10 O atoms.

Try this before revealing the answer

Balance Fe+O₂→Fe₂O₃ with smallest integer coefficients.

Reveal answer and reasoning

4Fe+3O₂→2Fe₂O₃. Both sides contain four iron atoms and six oxygen atoms.

If stuck. Changing Fe₂O₃ to FeO would describe a different product, not a balanced equation.

Use coefficients as mole ratios

Question cue. An amount of one species must predict another species.

Meaning. The balanced equation relates amounts in moles. Grams require a molar-mass conversion before and after the mole ratio.

First move. Convert the known amount to moles, apply product and reactant coefficients, then convert to the requested unit. State the excess-reactant assumption when only one reactant amount is supplied.

Worked case. N₂+3H₂→2NH₃ predicts 1.00 mol NH₃ from 0.500 mol N₂ if H₂ is sufficient.

Try this before revealing the answer

For 2H₂+O₂→2H₂O, how many moles of H₂O can 1.5 mol O₂ make with excess H₂?

Reveal answer and reasoning

The 2:1 water-to-oxygen ratio gives 3.0 mol H₂O.

If stuck. A coefficient is a mole multiplier, not a direct gram multiplier.

OPTIONAL SHORT VIDEOStoichiometry Concept · 01:16 ↗

Find the limiting reagent and percent yield

Question cue. Two reactant quantities or actual product amount are given.

Meaning. Each reactant predicts an ideal product amount. The smaller prediction sets theoretical yield. Actual yield is what was collected and may be lower.

First move. Predict the same product separately from each reactant. Use the smaller result, convert it to the requested unit, and compare actual product with this theoretical maximum.

Worked case. For N₂+3H₂→2NH₃, 2.0 mol N₂ predicts 4.0 mol NH₃; 3.0 mol H₂ predicts 2.0 mol NH₃. H₂ limits. Collecting 1.5 mol gives 75% yield.

Try this before revealing the answer

For 2H₂+O₂→2H₂O, 3.0 mol H₂ reacts with 1.0 mol O₂. Which limits and how much water can form?

Reveal answer and reasoning

O₂ limits: it predicts 2.0 mol H₂O, while H₂ could predict 3.0 mol H₂O.

If stuck. The reactant with fewer moles is not always limiting; coefficients determine the comparison.

UNIT 03

Solutions, ions, and electron transfer

Convert solution volume into reaction amount

Question cue. A liquid concentration and volume appear in the question.

Meaning. Molarity is solute moles per liter of solution. Dilution preserves solute moles when no reaction or loss occurs. Mixing with a reacting solute requires stoichiometry after calculating each amount.

First move. Convert mL to L, calculate n=MV, and apply the reaction ratio if there is one. For a pure dilution, set starting and final solute moles equal.

Worked case. 20.0 mL of 0.100 M HCl contains 0.00200 mol HCl. It requires 10.0 mL of 0.200 M NaOH for 1:1 neutralization.

Try this before revealing the answer

What volume of 0.500 M stock contains 0.100 mol solute?

Reveal answer and reasoning

V=n/M=0.100/0.500=0.200 L, or 200 mL.

If stuck. Molarity uses liters of complete solution, not kilograms of solvent.

OPTIONAL SHORT VIDEOMolarity Concept · 00:53 ↗

Write the species that actually react

Question cue. A molecular equation includes aqueous ionic substances.

Meaning. A complete ionic equation separates soluble strong electrolytes. Spectator ions appear unchanged on both sides and disappear from the net ionic equation.

First move. Balance the molecular equation. Split only aqueous strong electrolytes, cancel unchanged ions, and check atoms and charge on the net equation.

Worked case. AgNO₃(aq)+NaCl(aq)→AgCl(s)+NaNO₃(aq) reduces to Ag⁺(aq)+Cl⁻(aq)→AgCl(s).

Try this before revealing the answer

Give the net ionic equation for BaCl₂(aq)+Na₂SO₄(aq)→BaSO₄(s)+2NaCl(aq).

Reveal answer and reasoning

Ba²⁺(aq)+SO₄²⁻(aq)→BaSO₄(s). Sodium and chloride are spectators.

If stuck. Do not split a solid, liquid, gas, or weak molecular acid into free ions.

Predict precipitation with solubility

Question cue. Two soluble salts are mixed and a solid may form.

Meaning. Dissolved ions can exchange partners, but a double replacement predicts an observable precipitate only if one product has low solubility under the stated conditions.

First move. Write both ion sets, exchange partners without changing ion charge, apply solubility rules, then write a balanced molecular and net ionic equation.

Worked case. Mixing aqueous Pb(NO₃)₂ and KI gives solid PbI₂ and aqueous KNO₃: Pb²⁺+2I⁻→PbI₂(s).

Try this before revealing the answer

Will AgNO₃(aq) plus NaCl(aq) form a solid? Name it.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Yes. AgCl is poorly soluble and precipitates; NaNO₃ remains aqueous.

If stuck. A product formula is not evidence that it precipitates. Check its solubility.

OPTIONAL SHORT VIDEOSolubility Rules · 00:28 ↗

Track electrons with oxidation numbers

Question cue. A reaction asks what is oxidized, reduced, or transferred.

Meaning. Oxidation raises an oxidation number; reduction lowers it. The oxidizing agent accepts electrons and is itself reduced.

First move. Assign oxidation numbers before identifying agents. Check that electron loss and gain balance in the finished equation.

Worked case. In Zn+Cu²⁺→Zn²⁺+Cu, Zn rises from 0 to +2 and loses two electrons. Cu falls from +2 to 0 and gains them.

Try this before revealing the answer

For 2Mg+O₂→2MgO, which species is the reducing agent?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Mg is the reducing agent because its oxidation number rises from 0 to +2 as it supplies electrons to oxygen.

If stuck. Oxygen is not always −2, notably in peroxides; use the compound context.

Balance a redox equation in acidic solution

Question cue. Elements and charge must be balanced in an aqueous redox reaction.

Meaning. Half reactions separate electron loss from gain. Water balances oxygen, H⁺ balances hydrogen in acid, and electrons balance charge.

First move. Split oxidation and reduction. Balance non H/O atoms, add H₂O for O and H⁺ for H, then electrons. Multiply half reactions to cancel electrons and check all atoms and net charge.

Worked case. MnO₄⁻+8H⁺+5e⁻→Mn²⁺+4H₂O has equal Mn, O, H, and charge (left +2; right +2).

Try this before revealing the answer

How many electrons are required to reduce Cr₂O₇²⁻ to 2Cr³⁺ in acid?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Six electrons. Each Cr goes from +6 to +3 and gains three electrons; two Cr atoms gain six.

If stuck. Balancing atoms without balancing net charge leaves an invalid ionic reaction.

UNIT 04

Gases and heat

Choose a gas model with its conditions

Question cue. Pressure, volume, temperature, and moles are related.

Meaning. An ideal gas relates P, V, n, and absolute T. It is most reliable when particles are dilute and intermolecular effects are small.

First move. Convert temperature to kelvin, choose consistent pressure and volume units, and use an R value that matches them. State which quantities stay fixed if using a two-state law.

Worked case. At 1.00 atm and 273 K, 1.00 mol ideal gas occupies about 22.4 L with R=0.08206 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Try this before revealing the answer

At fixed n and T, pressure doubles. What happens to volume?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Volume halves because PV=nRT remains constant for the stated model.

If stuck. Never insert Celsius directly into a gas-law temperature ratio.

OPTIONAL SHORT VIDEOIdeal Gas Law Formula · 01:15 ↗

Separate total gas pressure into components

Question cue. A mixture has partial pressures or mole fractions.

Meaning. For an ideal-gas mixture, each component contributes a pressure proportional to its mole fraction at common T and V. Collected gas over water includes water-vapor pressure.

First move. Find total gas moles and each mole fraction. Multiply by total pressure, or subtract vapor pressure when isolating a dry gas.

Worked case. A mixture with 1 mol N₂ and 3 mol O₂ at 2.0 atm has x(N₂)=1/4 and P(N₂)=0.50 atm.

Try this before revealing the answer

Gas is collected over water at total pressure 760 mmHg; water vapor is 24 mmHg. What is the dry-gas pressure?

Reveal answer and reasoning

736 mmHg, because the total is dry gas plus water vapor.

If stuck. A partial pressure is not the same as the pressure that component would have at a different volume.

Read gas trends from particle motion

Question cue. A question compares gas speed, effusion, or temperature.

Meaning. At equal temperature, gases share mean translational kinetic energy, but lighter molecules have larger root-mean-square speed. Effusion rate has an approximate inverse square-root molar-mass relation.

First move. Compare absolute temperatures for kinetic energy. For rms speed, use molar mass in kg/mol with SI R. For two gases at equal T, compare speeds or effusion rates using molar-mass square roots.

Worked case. At the same temperature, helium (M≈4 g/mol) has about sqrt(40/4)=3.16 times the rms speed of argon (M≈40 g/mol).

Try this before revealing the answer

At equal T, which effuses faster: H₂ or O₂, and by what approximate factor?

Reveal answer and reasoning

H₂; the idealized rate ratio is sqrt(32/2)=4.

If stuck. Equal temperature does not imply equal molecular speed; it implies equal average translational kinetic energy.

Use temperature change to find exchanged heat

Question cue. A substance or calorimeter warms or cools.

Meaning. Heat flows because of a temperature difference. A sample with positive ΔT absorbs heat; a reaction that warms its surroundings releases heat to them.

First move. Identify the system and surroundings, use mass and specific heat for the measured body, then invert the sign for the reacting system when assuming negligible losses.

Worked case. 100 g water warming by 5.0 °C absorbs about 100×4.184×5.0=2092 J. The reaction that caused it releases approximately 2.09 kJ under the simple calorimeter model.

Try this before revealing the answer

50 g water cools by 2.0 °C. Is q for that water positive or negative?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Negative: q=50×4.184×(−2.0)≈−418 J. It released heat.

If stuck. A temperature change is not itself a heat quantity; heat also depends on mass and heat capacity.

Reverse and add thermochemical steps

Question cue. A target reaction is assembled from known reactions.

Meaning. Enthalpy is a state function. Reverse a reaction and its ΔH sign changes; multiply coefficients and ΔH scales by the same factor.

First move. Write the target equation. Reverse, scale, and add supplied equations so unwanted species cancel. Add the adjusted enthalpy changes only after the chemical equations match.

Worked case. If A→B has ΔH=−40 kJ and B→C has ΔH=+15 kJ, then A→C has ΔH=−25 kJ.

Try this before revealing the answer

If 2A→2B has ΔH=+60 kJ, what is ΔH for B→A?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Divide by two to obtain A→B at +30 kJ, then reverse: B→A is −30 kJ.

If stuck. Adding enthalpies without first aligning full equations can hide wrong coefficients.

UNIT 05

Electrons, bonds, and phases

Connect wavelength to photon energy

Question cue. Light has a wavelength, frequency, or photon energy.

Meaning. Light frequency and wavelength are inversely related at a fixed propagation speed; each photon carries energy proportional to frequency. Atomic spectra reflect discrete energy differences.

First move. Convert wavelength to meters. Use ν=c/λ and E=hν for one photon. Shorter wavelength means higher photon energy.

Worked case. A 500 nm photon has energy (6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s)(3.00×10⁸ m/s)/(500×10⁻⁹ m)≈3.98×10⁻¹⁹ J.

Try this before revealing the answer

Which has greater photon energy, 400 nm or 800 nm light, and by what factor?

Reveal answer and reasoning

The 400 nm photon has twice the frequency and twice the energy.

If stuck. Light intensity changes photon count; it does not change the energy of a photon at fixed frequency.

Fill orbitals before predicting behavior

Question cue. An electron configuration or unpaired-electron count is needed.

Meaning. Subshell capacities follow the number of orbitals: s holds 2, p 6, d 10, f 14 electrons. Hund filling maximizes unpaired electrons before pairing within equal-energy orbitals.

First move. Count electrons from atomic number, adjust for ion charge, fill orbitals in energy order, and remove electrons from the highest principal shell first for common transition-metal cations.

Worked case. Neutral Fe is [Ar]4s²3d⁶; Fe²⁺ loses the two 4s electrons and is [Ar]3d⁶.

Try this before revealing the answer

Write the condensed configuration of Mg²⁺.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Neutral Mg is [Ne]3s²; losing two 3s electrons gives [Ne].

If stuck. Writing a transition-metal cation by removing 3d electrons before 4s often gives the wrong configuration.

Explain a periodic trend with attraction

Question cue. The question ranks atom size or ionization energy.

Meaning. Across a period, increasing effective nuclear charge often pulls valence electrons inward. Down a group, an added shell usually increases atomic radius. Ionization energy generally moves opposite atomic radius.

First move. Identify period and group first. Use shell number for a vertical comparison and nuclear attraction or shielding for a horizontal comparison, then look for known exceptions when precision matters.

Worked case. Across period 3, Na is larger than Cl. The stronger effective attraction in Cl helps hold its valence electrons more tightly.

Try this before revealing the answer

Which neutral atom is larger, K or Na? Which usually has the lower first ionization energy?

Reveal answer and reasoning

K is larger because it has an additional occupied shell and generally has the lower first ionization energy.

If stuck. Do not apply a simple across-period trend to compare an atom with its ion without accounting for electron gain or loss.

Build a Lewis structure that accounts for charge

Question cue. Connectivity, lone pairs, or formal charge are requested.

Meaning. A Lewis structure tracks valence electrons in bonds and lone pairs. Formal charge is a bookkeeping tool for comparing plausible structures, not a measured ionic charge at every atom.

First move. Count total valence electrons including ion charge. Connect atoms, fill outer octets when applicable, place remaining electrons centrally, and use multiple bonds if needed. Calculate formal charges and check total.

Worked case. For CO₂, O=C=O uses 16 valence electrons, gives octets, and has zero formal charge on all atoms.

Try this before revealing the answer

How many valence electrons does NO₃⁻ have before drawing bonds?

Reveal answer and reasoning

N contributes 5, three O contribute 18, and the −1 charge adds 1: total 24 electrons.

If stuck. An octet alone does not prove the best structure; check formal charge, atom placement, and valid exceptions.

Turn electron domains into molecular shape

Question cue. A Lewis structure must predict a three-dimensional shape.

Meaning. VSEPR uses the number of electron domains around the central atom. Single, double, and triple bonds each count as one domain; lone pairs affect the molecular shape and angle.

First move. Draw Lewis first, count bonding and lone-pair domains, name the electron geometry, then describe the positions of atoms alone. Use symmetry to test whether bond dipoles cancel.

Worked case. H₂O has four electron domains around O, two bonding and two lone pairs. Its electron geometry is tetrahedral, molecular shape is bent, and it is polar.

Try this before revealing the answer

What is the molecular shape of NH₃ and why is it polar?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Four domains with one lone pair give a trigonal-pyramidal shape. Its N–H bond dipoles do not cancel.

If stuck. Electron geometry and molecular geometry differ whenever a central lone pair replaces an atom.

OPTIONAL SHORT VIDEOMolecular Geometry with Four Electron Groups · 01:57 ↗

Choose the dominant intermolecular interaction

Question cue. Boiling point, condensation, or miscibility is compared.

Meaning. All particles exhibit dispersion. Polar molecules can have dipole interactions; molecules with H bonded to N, O, or F can form hydrogen bonds with suitable partners. These attractions are between particles, not the covalent bonds inside them.

First move. Identify molecular polarity and H-bond donors and acceptors. Compare size and surface area as well as interaction type, then connect stronger attractions to higher boiling point when molecules are otherwise comparable.

Worked case. H₂O boils much higher than H₂S because water forms an extensive hydrogen-bond network, while H₂S does not make comparably strong hydrogen bonds.

Try this before revealing the answer

Between CH₄ and NH₃, which has the higher boiling point and what interaction matters?

Reveal answer and reasoning

NH₃ has the higher boiling point; N–H molecules can hydrogen-bond, while CH₄ relies mainly on dispersion.

If stuck. A polar covalent bond does not automatically make the whole molecule polar; molecular symmetry matters.

Read heating and phase changes

Question cue. A heating curve has slopes and plateaus.

Meaning. On a sloped segment, added energy changes temperature. On a plateau during a first-order phase change at constant pressure, energy changes phase while temperature stays approximately constant.

First move. Mark initial and final phase. Add mcΔT for temperature intervals and nΔH for phase intervals. Keep fusion and vaporization enthalpies distinct.

Worked case. Melting 2.0 mol ice at its melting temperature takes q=2.0ΔHfus without raising its temperature during the melt.

Try this before revealing the answer

A liquid at its boiling point absorbs heat and fully vaporizes. Does temperature rise during this idealized phase step?

Reveal answer and reasoning

No. Under constant pressure during the phase change, energy breaks intermolecular attractions and temperature remains at the boiling point.

If stuck. A flat heating-curve segment is not zero energy input.

UNIT 06

Solutions, rates, and equilibrium

Choose a concentration scale

Question cue. The problem specifies solution volume, solvent mass, or a mole fraction.

Meaning. Molarity depends on final solution volume and therefore temperature. Molality uses kilograms of solvent; mole fraction is moles of one component divided by all component moles.

First move. Circle the denominator named in the question. Convert mass to moles and volume to liters only where the chosen scale requires them.

Worked case. Dissolving 0.50 mol solute in 2.0 kg solvent gives 0.25 mol/kg molality. Final solution volume was not supplied, so molarity cannot yet be calculated.

Try this before revealing the answer

A solution has 0.20 mol solute and 0.80 mol solvent. What is solute mole fraction?

Reveal answer and reasoning

0.20/(0.20+0.80)=0.20.

If stuck. Molality uses solvent mass; using the mass of the entire solution changes the result.

Count dissolved particles in a colligative effect

Question cue. Freezing or boiling point shifts after adding a solute.

Meaning. Ideal colligative changes depend on the concentration of dissolved particles. An electrolyte may produce several ions per formula unit, summarized approximately by the van ’t Hoff factor i.

First move. Determine the solvent constant, molality, and realistic particle factor. Compare the result with the pure-solvent transition temperature in the correct direction.

Worked case. For an ideal nonelectrolyte at 1.0 m in water, freezing-point depression is about 1.86 °C, so the solution freezes near −1.86 °C.

Try this before revealing the answer

Which ideally makes a larger freezing-point depression at equal molality: glucose or NaCl?

Reveal answer and reasoning

NaCl, because it ideally produces about two dissolved ions per formula unit, whereas glucose remains one molecule.

If stuck. Real electrolytes may have i below the simple ion count because of ion pairing and nonideal behavior.

OPTIONAL SHORT VIDEOBoiling Point Elevation · 03:05 ↗

Infer a rate law from measured changes

Question cue. An initial-rate table varies reactant concentrations.

Meaning. Reaction orders are empirical exponents determined by experiment unless an elementary step is established. Overall balanced coefficients usually do not determine the rate law.

First move. Compare trials with only one changed concentration. Divide rates to infer that exponent, repeat for other reactants, then use one trial to calculate k and its units.

Worked case. If doubling [A] doubles rate with [B] fixed, A is first order. If doubling [B] quadruples rate with [A] fixed, B is second order: rate=k[A][B]².

Try this before revealing the answer

Doubling [A] changes rate by a factor of four while other concentrations stay fixed. What is the order in A?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Second order, because 2^m=4 gives m=2.

If stuck. Do not copy exponents from stoichiometric coefficients for a multistep reaction.

OPTIONAL SHORT VIDEORate Law Fundamentals · 01:52 ↗

Separate rate from energy barrier

Question cue. Temperature or catalyst changes reaction speed.

Meaning. Increasing temperature makes a larger fraction of collisions energetic enough to cross an activation barrier. A catalyst supplies an alternative path with a lower barrier but does not change the overall reaction enthalpy or equilibrium constant.

First move. For a temperature comparison, use the Arrhenius relationship and absolute T. For a catalyst question, compare barriers and distinguish a faster arrival at equilibrium from a changed equilibrium composition.

Worked case. A catalyst lowers both forward and reverse kinetic barriers. Equilibrium is reached sooner, but K at the same temperature is unchanged.

Try this before revealing the answer

Can a catalyst turn an unfavorable equilibrium constant into a favorable one at fixed temperature?

Reveal answer and reasoning

No. It changes rates, not ΔG° or K; changing temperature may alter K.

If stuck. A faster reaction need not be more thermodynamically favorable.

Compare current composition with equilibrium

Question cue. A reversible reaction gives concentrations before equilibrium.

Meaning. An equilibrium constant compares equilibrium activities in a specified reaction direction. A reaction quotient Q uses the same expression with the current composition.

First move. Write the balanced equation and its K expression, omitting pure solids and liquids. Compute Q; Q<K favors forward change, Q>K favors reverse change, and Q=K is equilibrium.

Worked case. For H₂+I₂⇌2HI with K=4, current [HI]=1, [H₂]=1, [I₂]=1 gives Q=1, so the net change initially proceeds toward HI.

Try this before revealing the answer

If Q=9 for a reaction with K=3, which net direction is favored?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Reverse: products are relatively too abundant compared with equilibrium.

If stuck. K says where equilibrium lies, not how fast it will be reached.

Solve an equilibrium change with one variable

Question cue. Initial amounts and an equilibrium constant are given.

Meaning. An ICE table converts the reaction stoichiometry into consistent changes in concentration or pressure. A small-x approximation needs a numerical check, not an assumption.

First move. Write initial, change, and equilibrium rows for every gas or aqueous species. Substitute the expressions into K, solve a physically meaningful root, and check nonnegative concentrations and any approximation.

Worked case. For A⇌B with initial [A]=1.00 M and K=[B]/[A]=4, let x form B. Then x/(1−x)=4, so x=0.80 M and [A]=0.20 M.

Try this before revealing the answer

For A⇌B starting with 2.0 M A and K=1, what are the equilibrium concentrations?

Reveal answer and reasoning

x/(2.0−x)=1 gives x=1.0 M. Thus [A]=[B]=1.0 M.

If stuck. An algebraic root that makes a concentration negative is chemically impossible.

Predict a shift without claiming a new rate law

Question cue. A system at equilibrium is stressed by concentration, pressure, or temperature.

Meaning. A disturbance changes Q immediately; the system then moves until Q equals the appropriate K. Only a temperature change generally changes K for a given reaction.

First move. Write the reaction and decide what the stress changes first. Use Q relative to K for concentration or pressure. Treat heat as reactant or product for temperature changes.

Worked case. For N₂+3H₂⇌2NH₃, adding H₂ lowers Q and favors product formation. Compressing an ideal-gas mixture favors the side with fewer gas moles.

Try this before revealing the answer

At equilibrium, an inert gas is added at fixed volume and temperature. Must the equilibrium composition shift?

Reveal answer and reasoning

No. The reactive gases retain their partial pressures at fixed volume and T, so Q and K do not change.

If stuck. “More pressure favors fewer moles” needs the compression condition; adding inert gas at fixed volume is different.

UNIT 07

Acids, buffers, and aqueous equilibria

Identify acid base partners and pH

Question cue. A proton-transfer equation or [H₃O⁺] is given.

Meaning. A Brønsted acid donates H⁺; a Brønsted base accepts it. Conjugate partners differ by one proton. pH is logarithmic, so a tenfold [H₃O⁺] increase lowers pH by one.

First move. Mark the proton transferred and pair each acid with the species it becomes. For a strong monoprotic acid under ordinary dilute conditions, use its analytical concentration as [H₃O⁺] before taking −log₁₀.

Worked case. At 25 °C, 0.010 M strong HCl gives [H₃O⁺]≈0.010 M and pH≈2.00.

Try this before revealing the answer

At 25 °C, what is the pH of an idealized 1.0×10⁻³ M strong monoprotic acid?

Reveal answer and reasoning

pH=3.00 because [H₃O⁺]≈1.0×10⁻³ M.

If stuck. Do not treat a weak acid concentration as if it dissociates fully.

Solve a weak acid with an equilibrium expression

Question cue. The acid concentration and Ka are given.

Meaning. A weak acid dissociates only partly. Its conjugate base and hydronium appear in equal amounts for a simple monoprotic acid initially alone in water.

First move. Set x=[H₃O⁺] formed. Use Ka=x²/(C−x), solve exactly or approximate if x/C is small, then verify the approximation and take pH.

Worked case. For 0.100 M HA with Ka=1.0×10⁻⁵, x≈sqrt(KaC)=1.0×10⁻³ M, 1% dissociation; pH≈3.00.

Try this before revealing the answer

For 0.010 M HA with Ka=1.0×10⁻⁶, estimate [H₃O⁺] and pH.

Reveal answer and reasoning

x≈sqrt(10⁻⁸)=1.0×10⁻⁴ M, about 1% of C, so pH≈4.00.

If stuck. The square-root shortcut is not justified when dissociation is a substantial fraction of initial acid.

Choose the buffer state before the equation

Question cue. A weak acid and conjugate base are both present.

Meaning. A buffer resists modest pH changes because acid consumes added base and conjugate base consumes added acid. Henderson–Hasselbalch follows from Ka and is useful only while both partners are present at meaningful amounts.

First move. If strong acid or base was added, perform its stoichiometric neutralization first. Then use the remaining weak-acid and base amounts in their ratio; check that neither is exhausted.

Worked case. With equal HA and A⁻ amounts, pH=pKa. Doubling the A⁻/HA ratio raises pH by log₁₀2≈0.301.

Try this before revealing the answer

A buffer has pKa=4.75 and [A⁻]/[HA]=10. What is its approximate pH?

Reveal answer and reasoning

pH=4.75+log₁₀10=5.75, assuming both species remain and activity effects are neglected.

If stuck. Henderson–Hasselbalch is not a shortcut for strong-acid excess after the buffer has been consumed.

Read a titration by its chemical region

Question cue. A titration curve or volume of titrant is given.

Meaning. Before equivalence, a weak-acid/strong-base titration contains both HA and A⁻. At half-equivalence pH≈pKa; at equivalence the conjugate base makes the solution basic. A strong-acid/strong-base equivalence is near pH 7 at 25 °C.

First move. Locate the initial, buffer, half-equivalence, equivalence, or excess-titrant region before choosing a pH method. Use mole balances to locate equivalence.

Worked case. 20.0 mL of 0.100 M HCl contains 0.00200 mol acid; a 0.200 M NaOH solution reaches 1:1 equivalence after 10.0 mL.

Try this before revealing the answer

At half-equivalence of a weak monoprotic acid titrated with strong base, how does pH compare with pKa?

Reveal answer and reasoning

They are approximately equal because the neutralization produces equal HA and A⁻ amounts.

If stuck. Equivalence and pH 7 coincide only for a strong-acid/strong-base titration under ideal standard conditions.

Use Ksp and Qsp for precipitation

Question cue. A sparingly soluble salt meets dissolved ions.

Meaning. Ksp describes an equilibrium with the solid omitted from the expression. Qsp is calculated from current ion concentrations; Qsp>Ksp favors precipitation until equilibrium is restored.

First move. Write the dissolution equation first, then the ion-product expression with stoichiometric exponents. Include the dilution that occurs when solutions mix.

Worked case. For MX₂(s)⇌M²⁺+2X⁻, pure-water solubility s gives Ksp=s(2s)²=4s³.

Try this before revealing the answer

If Ksp=4.0×10⁻¹² for ideal MX₂ in pure water, estimate s.

Reveal answer and reasoning

4s³=4.0×10⁻¹², so s³=1.0×10⁻¹² and s=1.0×10⁻⁴ M.

If stuck. Comparing two salts by Ksp alone can misrank solubility when their dissociation stoichiometries differ.

Recognize a complex-ion side equilibrium

Question cue. A metal ion binds ligands and apparent solubility changes.

Meaning. Ligand binding lowers free-metal-ion concentration. It can pull a dissolution equilibrium forward, so a salt may dissolve more in the presence of a suitable ligand.

First move. Write the metal–ligand formation reaction and its Kf separately from the salt’s Ksp. Use mass balances if a numerical total concentration is required.

Worked case. Ag⁺ binds two NH₃ molecules to form [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺. Reducing free [Ag⁺] can promote dissolution of an Ag-containing solid.

Try this before revealing the answer

If a ligand strongly removes free M²⁺ from a solution saturated with MX₂, which way does MX₂ dissolution shift?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Toward more dissolved ions, provided the complex forms and other conditions permit it.

If stuck. A measured total dissolved metal concentration is not the same as free-metal-ion concentration in Ksp.

UNIT 08

Free energy, electricity, and extensions

Test spontaneity under stated conditions

Question cue. A reaction has enthalpy, entropy, temperature, or equilibrium data.

Meaning. Gibbs free energy combines energy and entropy at constant T and P. A negative ΔG favors forward change under current conditions; ΔG° refers to standard states and connects to K.

First move. Use kelvin and matching energy units. Distinguish ΔG from ΔG°. A reaction with positive ΔG° can still proceed forward in a mixture with sufficiently small Q.

Worked case. If ΔH=+40 kJ/mol and ΔS=+0.100 kJ mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, then at 500 K ΔG≈40−50=−10 kJ/mol.

Try this before revealing the answer

For ΔH=−20 kJ/mol and ΔS=−0.050 kJ mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, is ΔG negative at 300 K?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Yes: ΔG=−20−300(−0.050)=−5 kJ/mol.

If stuck. A favorable ΔG does not show that a reaction is fast.

Assign cell electrodes before calculating voltage

Question cue. Two standard reduction potentials form a galvanic cell.

Meaning. Oxidation occurs at the anode, reduction at the cathode. Electrons flow through the external wire from anode to cathode. Tabulated potentials are reductions, so subtract the anode reduction potential.

First move. Choose the more positive reduction potential as the cathode for a spontaneous standard galvanic cell. Write and balance half reactions, then calculate E°cell; never multiply a potential to balance electrons.

Worked case. Zn²⁺/Zn is −0.76 V and Cu²⁺/Cu is +0.34 V. Zn oxidizes, Cu²⁺ reduces, and E°cell=0.34−(−0.76)=+1.10 V.

Try this before revealing the answer

Cathode reduction is +0.50 V and anode reduction is −0.20 V. Find E°cell.

Reveal answer and reasoning

+0.70 V. Subtract the listed anode reduction potential: 0.50−(−0.20).

If stuck. Changing half-reaction coefficients does not multiply electrode potentials.

Connect nonstandard composition to cell voltage

Question cue. A cell operates away from standard concentrations.

Meaning. Cell voltage depends on reaction quotient. A product-heavy composition reduces a positive cell potential; at equilibrium the cell voltage for that reaction is zero.

First move. Balance the overall electron transfer to get n and Q. Use the Nernst equation with kelvin and consistent R/F units, then check the sign of ln Q.

Worked case. For a two-electron cell at 298 K, Q=10 gives a voltage about 0.0296 V below E° because (0.0592/2)log₁₀10=0.0296 V.

Try this before revealing the answer

At 298 K, a one-electron cell has Q=0.1. Is E greater or less than E°?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Greater. log₁₀(0.1)=−1, so subtracting the negative term raises E by about 0.0592 V.

If stuck. Use Q for the balanced overall cell reaction, not a concentration ratio invented from half reactions.

Convert electric charge into chemical product

Question cue. Current and time determine electrolysis amount.

Meaning. Charge passed is current times time. Faraday’s constant converts charge to moles of electrons; the balanced half reaction converts electrons to product moles.

First move. Compute Q in coulombs, divide by F≈96485 C/mol e⁻, then use the electron-to-product coefficient. Apply current efficiency only if specified.

Worked case. Cu²⁺+2e⁻→Cu. Passing 1930 C ideally gives 1930/96485≈0.0200 mol e⁻ and 0.0100 mol Cu, about 0.635 g.

Try this before revealing the answer

How many moles of Ag form ideally from Ag⁺+e⁻→Ag after 9648.5 C passes?

Reveal answer and reasoning

0.100 mol e⁻ passes and the ratio is 1:1, so 0.100 mol Ag forms.

If stuck. Moles of electrons are not automatically moles of metal; read the half-reaction ratio.

Read radioactive change as a nuclear balance

Question cue. A nuclide decays or a half-life is supplied.

Meaning. Nuclear equations conserve mass number A and atomic number Z. Half-life describes the time for the expected number of undecayed nuclei in a large sample to halve.

First move. For a decay equation, balance A and Z separately. For a half-life problem, count elapsed half-lives or use the exponential form.

Worked case. An 80 mg sample with a 5 day half-life has 40 mg after 5 days and 20 mg after 10 days.

Try this before revealing the answer

What fraction remains after three half-lives?

Reveal answer and reasoning

(1/2)³=1/8, or 12.5% of the original radioactive nuclei.

If stuck. Half-life does not mean that every nucleus decays at the same instant.

Recognize a functional group before naming details

Question cue. Two organic structures share a formula but differ in behavior.

Meaning. Connectivity and functional groups often predict broad chemical behavior more reliably than a molecular formula alone. Hydroxyl, carbonyl, carboxyl, and amine groups have different bonding patterns.

First move. Count C and heteroatoms, then inspect bonds and group placement. Determine whether the structures are constitutional isomers or merely different drawings of the same connectivity.

Worked case. CH₃CH₂OH and CH₃OCH₃ both have C₂H₆O, but ethanol is an alcohol and dimethyl ether is an ether.

Try this before revealing the answer

Are CH₃CH₂CH₂OH and CH₃CHOHCH₃ identical molecules?

Reveal answer and reasoning

No. They are constitutional isomers: the OH attaches to a different carbon.

If stuck. A molecular formula does not uniquely identify a structure or its properties.

Balance charge in a coordination complex

Question cue. A metal ion is surrounded by charged or neutral ligands.

Meaning. Coordination number counts donor atoms bound to the metal; oxidation state follows total complex charge. A bidentate ligand counts two donor atoms, even if it is one ligand molecule.

First move. Write each ligand charge and donor count. Solve the metal oxidation state from the overall charge, then sum donor atoms for coordination number.

Worked case. In [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺, NH₃ is neutral: Cu is +2 and four monodentate ligands give coordination number four.

Try this before revealing the answer

For [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ with each CN⁻ monodentate, find Fe oxidation state and coordination number.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Six CN⁻ contribute −6. Fe must be +2 to give −4 overall, and six donors give coordination number six.

If stuck. The ligand count is not always the coordination number when a ligand has multiple donor atoms.

Mixed practice

  1. Amount. How many O atoms are in 0.40 mol CO₂?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    0.80 mol O atoms, about 4.8×10²³ atoms.

  2. Formula. What neutral compound forms from Al³⁺ and Cl⁻?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    AlCl₃: one Al³⁺ balances three Cl⁻.

  3. Reaction. Balance Al+O₂→Al₂O₃.
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    4Al+3O₂→2Al₂O₃.

  4. Limit. For N₂+3H₂→2NH₃, 1 mol N₂ and 1 mol H₂ are mixed. Which limits?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    H₂ limits; 1 mol H₂ produces 2/3 mol NH₃.

  5. Solution. How many moles are in 25.0 mL of 0.200 M NaCl?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    0.0250 L×0.200 mol/L=0.00500 mol NaCl.

  6. Ions. What is the net ionic equation when AgNO₃(aq) meets NaCl(aq)?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Ag⁺(aq)+Cl⁻(aq)→AgCl(s).

  7. Gas. At fixed n and T, an ideal gas volume doubles. What happens to P?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Pressure halves because PV remains fixed.

  8. Heat. Water warms by 3 °C. What sign is q for the water?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Positive. The water absorbed heat.

  9. Light. At fixed propagation speed, wavelength halves. What happens to photon energy?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Energy doubles because E=hc/λ.

  10. Shape. Is a linear CO₂ molecule polar because each C=O bond is polar?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    No. Its two opposed bond dipoles cancel.

  11. Rate. Doubling [A] triples a measured initial rate with other concentrations fixed. Is its order necessarily 1?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    No. Order m satisfies 2^m=3, so m≈1.585 for those data; inspect experimental uncertainty and model.

  12. Equilibrium. Q=0.2 while K=5. Which direction is favored?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Forward, since Q<K.

  13. Acid. Estimate pH of 0.0010 M strong monoprotic acid at 25 °C.
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    About 3.00.

  14. Buffer. In an ideal HA/A⁻ buffer, [A⁻]=[HA]. What is pH?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Approximately pKa.

  15. Solubility. For MX₂(s)⇌M²⁺+2X⁻, express Ksp in pure water using solubility s.
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    Ksp=s(2s)²=4s³.

  16. Cell. Cathode E°red=+0.80 V, anode E°red=−0.40 V. Find E°cell.
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    +1.20 V.

  17. Decay. What fraction remains after two half-lives?
    Reveal answer and reasoning

    One quarter.
