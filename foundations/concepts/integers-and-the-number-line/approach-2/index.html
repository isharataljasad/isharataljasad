What this route covers

This is the practice treatment. One log sheet is set up below and every family reads from it, so the decisions stay connected to a quantity that matters rather than to bare symbols. Commit to an answer before opening each reply. The replies name the misreading behind the tempting wrong answer, and on this topic that misreading is nearly always the same one wearing different clothes.

If a ruling surprises you, the curriculum reference states it in one line, and the written guide explains it at length.

The one log sheet used throughout

A vaccine store must be held at or below -20 °C. Four readings were logged during one week.

ReadingTemperature
Monday-22 °C
Tuesday-18 °C
Wednesday-25 °C
Thursday-19.5 °C

The convention is stated once and held: above freezing is positive, below freezing is negative.

Family 1 · Membership: which readings are integers?

Decide: which of the four readings are integers, and which is not?

Name the one that fails and say what disqualifies it.

The one that is not, and why the question is not pedantry

Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday are integers. Thursday's -19.5 is not: it lies between -19 and -20, and the integers contain nothing between consecutive whole numbers.

This matters here rather than being a definition drill. -19.5 sits between two readings that fall on opposite sides of the limit, so any procedure that silently rounds it to an integer will change whether the store passes or fails. Rounding to -20 passes; rounding to -19 fails.

The error worth naming: treating "not an integer" as a technicality. The value is perfectly real; it is the set that excludes it, and the set was chosen for counting, not measuring.

Family 2 · Order: which readings are within limit?

Decide: is Tuesday's `-18 °C` within the limit of at most `-20 °C`?

Answer yes or no, and justify by position rather than by digits.

Then decide: order all four readings from coldest to warmest

The judgement, and the reasoning that inverts it

Tuesday is not within limit. -18 lies to the right of -20 on the line, so it is warmer, and the requirement was to stay at or below -20.

The tempting answer is yes, reached by noticing that 18 is a smaller digit than 20 and concluding that -18 is the smaller temperature. That compares the digits and ignores the side of zero. Among negatives the larger digit is the smaller number, because it lies further left.

Coldest to warmest: -25, -22, -19.5, -18. Only Monday and Wednesday meet the requirement.

The error worth naming: reading "below" as "smaller digit". Below means further left, and for negatives that is the opposite of a smaller digit. Here it decides whether a batch of vaccine is reported as safe.

Family 3 · Opposites and the double sign

Decide: what is the opposite of `-22`?

Then decide: evaluate `-(-22)`, and say whether it agrees

Why the two answers must agree

The opposite of -22 is 22: the same distance from zero, on the other side.

-(-22) = 22 as well. Writing a minus sign in front of a number is an instruction meaning "the opposite of", so the expression asks for the opposite of negative twenty-two. Applying the instruction twice returns you to where you started.

The two agree because they are the same request written two ways, not because two negatives happen to cancel by a rule you memorised.

A check that confirms it: a number and its opposite sum to zero, and -22 + 22 = 0.

The error worth naming: reading -(-22) as a typing slip and deleting one sign. Each sign is doing a job, and dropping one changes the request.

Family 4 · Absolute value does not order anything

Decide: compare `|-25|` with `|-18|`. Then compare `-25` with `-18`.

State both results, then say why they point opposite ways.

Then decide: does `|a| = |b|` allow you to conclude `a = b`?

What the bars keep and what they throw away

|-25| = 25 and |-18| = 18, so |-25| > |-18|. But -25 < -18, because -25 lies further left.

The two comparisons disagree because they are about different things. Absolute value is the distance from zero, which has no direction; order is about which side and how far. The bars deliberately discard the side, and the side is exactly what an order comparison needs.

So no: |a| = |b| does not give a = b. It gives a = b or a = -b. |-18| = |18| while -18 ≠ 18.

Applied to the log: Wednesday has the largest absolute value and is the coldest reading; Tuesday has a smaller absolute value and is the warmest. Absolute value happened to rank the negatives in reverse here — and it would rank a positive reading wrongly against both.

The error worth naming: using "how big the number looks" as a stand-in for order. It is a distance, and distances do not know which way you walked.

What to be able to do before moving on

  1. Decide whether a measured value belongs to the integers, and say why the distinction can change a judgement.
  2. Compare two negatives by position, and state a "below a limit" ruling that contradicts the digits.
  3. Give an opposite, evaluate a double sign, and check the pair sums to zero.
  4. Compute an absolute value, and say why it cannot be used to order two integers.

Check yourself

Commit to an answer before checking. A wrong answer opens a different problem, not the same one again. Everything here is written out above as well, so the page works without scripting.

A vaccine store must be held at or below -20 °C. Tuesday logged -18 °C. Is that within limit?

Choose one

-18 lies to the right of -20 on the line, so it is warmer. "At or below" needs a position further left, such as -20 or -25.

A different problem, to test the repair

Of the readings -22, -18 and -25, enter the coldest.

Worked answer

-25 lies furthest left, so it is coldest, although 25 is the largest digit.

Enter the smaller of -25 and -18.

-25 lies further left on the number line, so it is the smaller number.

A different problem, to test the repair

Now enter the value of |-25| - |-18|.

Worked answer

25 - 18 = 7. Both bars report a distance from zero, and a distance is never negative.

Evaluate -(-22) and explain what the outer negative sign does.

A minus sign in front means "the opposite of", so this asks for the opposite of negative twenty-two.

A different problem, to test the repair

A number and its opposite always sum to the same value. Enter -22 + 22.

Worked answer

Zero, which is exactly what "same distance from zero, other side" means.
