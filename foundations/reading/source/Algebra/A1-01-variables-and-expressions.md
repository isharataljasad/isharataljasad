# Variables and expressions: reading the structure of a sentence

**Algebra 1, episode 01 | Early reading edition**

## What this lesson will settle

The challenge is not merely knowing that a letter stands for a number. It is preserving the **order and grouping** of a verbal instruction when you turn it into algebra. A correct expression is a compact record of the operations in the sentence.

## 1. The parts of an algebraic expression

In `5x² + 2x - 3`, `x` is a variable; `5x²`, `2x`, and `-3` are terms; `5` and `2` are coefficients of the variable terms. The exponent in `x²` applies to `x`, not to the coefficient: `5x²` means `5 × x × x`. An expression has no equals sign. An equation, such as `5x² + 2x - 3 = 0`, asks whether two expressions have the same value for some input.

Juxtaposition usually means multiplication: `7x` is `7 × x`, and `(a+b)(c+d)` multiplies two complete sums. We avoid the letter-shaped multiplication sign in algebra because it can be mistaken for the variable `x`.

## 2. Translate the outer operation first

The words “sum,” “difference,” “product,” and “quotient” identify operations, but they do not by themselves identify the full pieces being combined. Find the **outermost operation**, then bracket its parts.

| Phrase | Structure to notice | Expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seven less than twice `y` | Start with twice `y`; subtract seven | `2y - 7` |
| Four times the sum of `a` and `b` | Multiply a whole sum | `4(a+b)` |
| The square of `x` times the cube of `z` | Multiply two powers | `x²z³` |
| The sum of `3a` and `8`, divided by `2` | Divide the whole sum | `(3a+8)/2` |

“Seven less than `2y`” is not `7 - 2y`. The phrase names a reduction **from** `2y`. Test a concrete value: if `y = 10`, seven less than twenty is thirteen, and `2(10)-7 = 13`.

## 3. Nested phrases need visible grouping

Consider: “the quotient of the sum of three times `p` and six, and the difference of four times `q` and five.” The quotient is the outer operation. Its numerator is `3p + 6`; its denominator is `4q - 5`. The expression is `(3p + 6)/(4q - 5)`. Parentheses protect both groups. It is defined only when `4q - 5 ≠ 0`, so `q ≠ 5/4`.

Now compare “the difference of `4q` and (five less than `c`).” “Five less than `c`” is `c - 5`, and the outer difference is `4q - (c - 5)`, which simplifies to `4q - c + 5`. Removing the parentheses before respecting the subtraction would change the meaning. This is the kind of hidden step a spoken explanation can leave too quickly.

## 4. Verify by reading backward

After writing symbols, read them back literally. `(u + 2)/3` says “the sum of `u` and two, divided by three”; `u + 2/3` says “`u` plus two thirds.” Substitute an easy number such as `u = 1` to see that they differ: the first is `1`, the second is `5/3`. This small test catches many translation mistakes before an equation is solved.

## Coverage before questions

You have met variable, coefficient, term, exponent, implicit multiplication, expression versus equation, outer versus inner operations, phrase order, parentheses, denominator restrictions, and backward checking. The examples used new values and wording so the method must transfer, not be memorized.

## Five question fingerprints

1. **Structure:** In `6t³ - 4t + 9`, identify the variable, the coefficient of the cubic term, and the constant term.
2. **Order:** Translate “nine less than the product of four and `x`.” Explain why `9 - 4x` is wrong.
3. **Grouping:** Translate “twice the difference of `m` and five, divided by the sum of `n` and one.” State when the expression is undefined.
4. **Reverse:** Write in words what `3(a + b²)` means. Which part is squared?
5. **Error:** A student translates “the square of the sum of `r` and two” as `r² + 2`. Correct it and show with `r = 1` that the two expressions differ.

## Answers with reasons

1. The variable is `t`; the cubic coefficient is `6`; the constant is `9`.
2. `4x - 9`. “Nine less than” tells us to subtract nine from the product `4x`.
3. `2(m - 5)/(n + 1)`. The denominator cannot be zero, so `n ≠ -1`.
4. “Three times the sum of `a` and the square of `b`.” Only `b` is squared.
5. `(r + 2)²`. At `r = 1`, it is `9`, while `r² + 2` is `3`. The square applies to the whole sum.
