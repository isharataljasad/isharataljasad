PHY 101 · TOPIC 07

Circular motion

Recognise that changing direction requires acceleration.

Book →Pearson →Educator →

The idea

In uniform circular motion, speed is constant but velocity changes direction. Inward acceleration is v²/r. The required net inward force is supplied by actual forces such as tension, friction or gravity; it is not an additional force.

A first example

For speed 4 m/s and radius 2 m, inward acceleration is 16/2=8 m/s².

Engineering connection. Rotating equipment provides context for distinguishing speed, angular motion and inward acceleration.

What this explanation develops

  • Centripetal acceleration and centripetal force are calculated from velocity, radius, and mass.
  • Identify the real forces whose radial resultant provides the centripetal force.
  • Relate period to speed on a circular path.

Definitions & meaning

Uniform circular motion
Motion on a circular path at constant speed. Velocity still changes because its direction changes, so acceleration is nonzero and points toward the centre.
Centripetal acceleration
The inward acceleration for uniform circular motion has magnitude a_c=v²/r. It is perpendicular to velocity. At fixed radius, doubling speed quadruples the acceleration.
Centripetal force
The resultant force toward the center: F_c = m v²/r. It is not a new kind of force and is not added to the free-body diagram; Rather, it is the role played by a real force such as tension, friction, gravity, or surface force.
Period (T)
The time required for a complete cycle. The distance in one revolution is the circumference of the circle 2πr, so the speed is v = 2πr/T.
Centrifugal force
Centrifugal force is an inertial force used in a rotating frame. It is not an additional interaction force in an inertial-frame free-body diagram. In this lesson, diagrams use an inertial frame and include only the real forces providing inward acceleration.

Formulas & conditions

Centripetal acceleration

a_c = v² / r

towards the center; And the speed is squared.

Centripetal force

F_c = m v² / r

A role played by real power, not additional power.

Speed of the cycle

v = 2 π r / T

Circumference of the circle divided by the cycle time.

Maximum cornering speed

v_max = √(μ_s g r) on a horizontal curve

Independent of mass, because mass is at both ends.

How is an object accelerating when its speed is constant?

An apparent contradiction that confuses many people: “constant speed” and “accelerating” in the same sentence.

  1. Refer to the definition: Acceleration is a change in velocity, not its magnitude.

    A vector has magnitude and direction, and a change in either of them is a change in it. Everyday language means speed alone, and hence the confusion.

  2. Visualize the velocity arrow at two close points on the circle: their length is the same and their direction is different.

    The difference between them is not zero, the difference is Δv, which is what the acceleration measures.

  3. Subtract the two arrows: the difference points inside the circle, i.e. towards the center.

    This is why the acceleration is called centripetal; Direction is a geometric result, not a convention.

  4. Since acceleration is perpendicular to velocity, the work of the centripetal force is zero.

    The kinetic energy does not change, so the speed remains constant. The contradiction disappears completely: the acceleration rotates the vector and does not lengthen it. This is the same result that I saw in the energy lesson for work.

Worked examples

Draw the body at the moment you are studying, and determine where the center is. Then make the positive direction toward the center, and collect the real forces on this axis. Then type ΣF = mv²/r and solve. Do not add an arrow in the name of centripetal force, nor add centrifugal force; The first is the result of addition, and the second does not exist in this context.

Example 1 · Centripetal acceleration

A body moving on a circle of radius 25 m with a constant speed 10 m/s., what is its centripetal acceleration?

  1. Substitute in a_c = v²/r = (10)²/25 = 100/25.

    Speed enters squared; Entering it without a square is the most common error here.

  2. Output 4.0 m/s² toward the center.

    Mentioning direction is part of the answer: acceleration here does not change the magnitude but rather the direction.

Result: a_c = 4.0 m/s² toward the centre

Example 2 · Maximum speed on a horizontal curve

A car turns on a horizontal curve with radius 50 m, and coefficient of static friction 0.40. What is the maximum speed before sliding? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. The only force towards the center is static friction, the maximum being μ_s N = μ_s mg.

    The surface is horizontal, so N = mg. is static friction, not kinetic, because the tire rolls and does not slide sideways.

  2. Equal: μ_s m g = m v²/r.

    The left end is the most that friction can provide, and the right is what movement requires. Exceeding the speed makes what is required greater than what is available and it slips.

  3. Delete m from both sides : v² = μ_s g r = 0.40 × 9.8 × 50 = 196.

    The mass disappears, so the maximum cornering speed is the same for the truck and the small car on the same surface. This is a result that surprises many.

  4. Take the root: v = 14 m/s.

    The output of 196 is v² and its unit is m²/s²; The question is about speed.

Result: v_max = 14 m/s

Further worked applications

The first type: Centripetal acceleration

Square the speed and divide by the radius.

A body rotating on a circle of radius 25 m at a constant speed 10 m/s., what is its centripetal acceleration?

a_c = v²/r = 100/25 = 4.0 m/s² toward the center.

Result: 4 m/s²

The second type: Central force

Enter the mass and divide by the radius.

A car of mass 1200 kg turns with speed 20 m/s on a curve of radius 50 m. How much centripetal force is needed?

‎F_c = m v²/r = 1200 × 400 / 50 = 9600 N‎، It is provided by static friction between the tires and the road.

Result: 9600 N

The third type is the speed of the time course

Use the circumference of the circle, not the radius.

A body makes a complete revolution on a circle of radius 2.0 m at 4.0 s. What is its speed?

‎v = 2πr/T = 2π(2.0)/4.0 = 12.57/4.0 = 3.14 m/s‎.

Result: 3.14 m/s

Fourth mode · Maximum cornering speed

Friction equals the demand and takes the root.

Horizontal curve with radius 50 m and coefficient of static friction 0.40. What is the maximum cornering speed? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

μ_s m g = m v²/r, the mass is deleted and v² = μ_s g r = 0.40 × 9.8 × 50 = 196 remains, And v = 14 m/s.

Result: 14 m/s

The fifth type is the resultant force with constant speed

You realize that the stability of the amount does not mean the absence of the outcome.

A body of mass 2.0 kg is rotating with a constant speed of 4.0 m/s on a circle of radius 8.0 m. What is the magnitude of the resultant forces acting on it?

Constant magnitude does not mean lack of acceleration: the direction changes. The resultant is m v²/r = 2.0 × 16 / 8.0 = 4.0 N toward the center.

Result: 4 N

Details that matter

Because you are not being pushed out at all; Your body tends to continue in a straight line by its own inertia, The car turns under you. You collide with the door, and the door pushes you **inside**, This is the only real horizontal force over you. The feeling of being pushed out is real, but its interpretation is insufficiency, not force.

The name describes the direction, not the source, as we say “vertical force.” Its benefit is that it combines different positions under one equation: Whatever the source of the force, if it points to the center, its magnitude is mv²/r. The mistake is to think that the name is an independent type and draw an additional arrow.

Because gravity at that moment points to the center of the circle, so it plays the role of a central force. The water does "fall", but it falls along a curved path that matches the path of the bucket. If the speed slows below a certain limit, gravity becomes greater than necessary for the bending, and the water leaves the bucket.

Useful background

Resultant force and free body diagram

Draw the body with only the real forces on it: weight, surface force, friction, and tension. Then add them towards the center and equal the sum to mv²/r. Do not draw an arrow named “central force” next to it; It is the result of summation and has no element.

Acceleration is a change in velocity

Acceleration is a = Δv/Δt and velocity is a vector, so its change includes a change in direction. A body rotating at a constant speed is accelerating at every moment because its direction is constantly changing. This does not contradict the stability of the magnitude, because the acceleration is perpendicular to the speed and does not increase or decrease it.

Scope of this explanation
  • Irregular circular motion (change in speed) is not in this lesson.
  • Rotation of rigid bodies, torque and rotational inertia are out of range.
  • Oblique curves (banking) are not counted here.
  • Movement in rotating frames and imaginary forces are mentioned for correction and are not calculated.
