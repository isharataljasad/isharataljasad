PHY 101 · TOPIC 07

Circular motion

Recognise that changing direction requires acceleration.

TryLearnApply
TRY FIRST

Start with a short challenge

Find inward acceleration at v=6 m/s and r=3 m.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

In uniform circular motion, speed is constant but velocity changes direction. Inward acceleration is v²/r. The required net inward force is supplied by actual forces such as tension, friction or gravity; it is not an additional force.

Worked example

For speed 4 m/s and radius 2 m, inward acceleration is 16/2=8 m/s².

Engineering connection Rotating equipment provides context for distinguishing speed, angular motion and inward acceleration.

Circular motion and inward force

Physical idea

Constant speed around a circle still means acceleration because velocity direction changes. The net radial force points toward the center; centripetal force is a role played by real forces, not an extra force to draw.

First move

Choose inward as positive and name the real forces with radial components. Write their signed radial sum as mv²/r. Use tangential equations separately if speed changes.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg mass moves at 3 m/s in a circle of radius 1.5 m. Required inward net force is 2·9/1.5=12 N. If a string supplies it, tension is 12 N only when other radial forces are absent.

Check yourself

A 1 kg object moves at 6 m/s around a 2 m circle. Find inward acceleration and net radial force.

Reveal answer and reasoning

ac=36/2=18 m/s² and Fr=1·18=18 N inward.

Common error. Do not add a separate “centripetal force” arrow to real tension, gravity, or friction arrows.

Angular motion and torque

Physical idea

Angular displacement and angular speed describe how a rigid body turns. Torque measures how strongly a force tends to rotate it, depending on force magnitude, lever arm, and the angle between them.

First move

Use radians in v=rω. For torque, measure r from the chosen pivot to the point where force acts, then use only the component perpendicular to r. Assign positive or negative torque by rotation sense.

Worked reasoning

A 10 N force perpendicular to a 0.30 m wrench produces torque 3.0 N·m. If the same force acts along the wrench, sinφ=0 and torque about the bolt is zero.

Check yourself

A wheel has radius 0.20 m and angular speed 15 rad/s. Find rim speed.

Reveal answer and reasoning

v=rω=0.20·15=3.0 m/s.

Common error. Force alone does not determine torque; the perpendicular lever arm matters.

Circular motion and inward force

Meaning

An object can have constant speed and still accelerate because its velocity direction changes. Centripetal force is the name for the net inward component of real forces.

First move

Draw the circle center, choose inward positive, and identify which real forces have inward components. Use speed at the point of interest, not a force “outward” added to the free-body diagram.

Worked reasoning

At 10 m/s on a radius 5 m circle, centripetal acceleration is 20 m/s². A 2 kg object needs 40 N net inward force.

Your turn

A 1 kg object moves at 6 m/s around a 3 m radius. Find net inward force.

Reveal answer and reasoning

F=mv²/r=1·36/3=12 N inward.

If it went wrong. A circular path does not imply an extra physical “centripetal force” in addition to tension, friction, gravity, or the normal force.

Flat and banked curves

Meaning

A flat curve needs static friction to provide inward force. An ideal frictionless bank instead uses the inward component of the normal force.

First move

Draw the force diagram before selecting a formula. The flat-road expression describes the threshold of slip; the bank expression assumes no friction.

Worked reasoning

On a flat curve with μs=0.5, r=20 m, and g=10 m/s², the maximum speed before slipping is √(0.5·20·10)=10 m/s.

Your turn

With μs=0.4, r=25 m, and g=10, find the flat-curve speed limit.

Reveal answer and reasoning

vmax=√(0.4·25·10)=10 m/s.

If it went wrong. The two formulas describe different physical assumptions; a banked curve with friction needs a new force balance.

Circular motion and inward force

When you see

A trajectory bends, even when speed stays constant.

Choose this model

Particle with radial acceleration; identify the real force that supplies net inward force.

Principle

Centripetal acceleration is a direction of net acceleration, not an additional force. Tangential acceleration changes speed; radial acceleration changes direction.

First move

Point the radial axis toward the center. Resolve gravity, tension, friction, and normal force along it.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg mass at 4 m/s on a 2 m radius needs 16 N net inward force.

Try it

At twice the speed and the same radius and mass, how does required inward force change?

Reveal answer and reasoning

It becomes four times larger because mv²/r depends on speed squared.

Repair the error. A constant speed on a curved path is accelerated motion.

Angular kinematics

When you see

A wheel turns and angular acceleration is given.

Choose this model

Rigid body rotating about a fixed axis.

Principle

Angular position, velocity, and acceleration parallel linear kinematics only when angular acceleration is constant. Points on a rigid wheel share ω but have different linear speeds.

First move

Work in radians, choose positive rotation, and use r to connect angular and tangential quantities.

Worked reasoning

Starting at 2 rad/s with α=3 rad/s² for 4 s, a wheel reaches 14 rad/s and turns through 32 rad.

Try it

At ω=6 rad/s, what is tangential speed at r=0.50 m?

Reveal answer and reasoning

vt=rω=3 m/s. A point at twice the radius would have twice this speed.

Repair the error. Do not put degrees directly into equations derived using radians.
