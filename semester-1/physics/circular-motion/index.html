Circular motion

Recognise that changing direction requires acceleration.

LearnCheckReferenceImageStep by stepPracticeApply

Bayt Al-Fuad

Before you start

What will you be able to do after this lesson?

  • Centripetal acceleration and centripetal force are calculated from velocity, radius, and mass.
  • Identify the real forces whose radial resultant provides the centripetal force.
  • Relate period to speed on a circular path.

What this lesson does not cover

  • Irregular circular motion (change in speed) is not in this lesson.
  • Rotation of rigid bodies, torque and rotational inertia are out of range.
  • Oblique curves (banking) are not counted here.
  • Movement in rotating frames and imaginary forces are mentioned for correction and are not calculated.

We mentioned them so that you do not think that you have completed the whole topic.

Prerequisites

  • Resultant force and free body diagram

    You need it because: The centripetal force is not a new force added, but rather the resultant of the real forces in the direction of the center.

    Draw the body with only the real forces on it: weight, surface force, friction, and tension. Then add them towards the center and equal the sum to mv²/r. Do not draw an arrow named “central force” next to it; It is the result of summation and has no element.

    Study Newton's laws

  • Acceleration is a change in velocity

    You need it because: The whole idea is based on the fact that a change in direction is an acceleration, even if the amount is fixed.

    Acceleration is a = Δv/Δt and velocity is a vector, so its change includes a change in direction. A body rotating at a constant speed is accelerating at every moment because its direction is constantly changing. This does not contradict the stability of the magnitude, because the acceleration is perpendicular to the speed and does not increase or decrease it.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

In uniform circular motion, speed is constant but velocity changes direction. Inward acceleration is v²/r. The required net inward force is supplied by actual forces such as tension, friction or gravity; it is not an additional force.

Worked example

For speed 4 m/s and radius 2 m, inward acceleration is 16/2=8 m/s².

Engineering connection Rotating equipment provides context for distinguishing speed, angular motion and inward acceleration.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

Uniform circular motion
Motion on a circular path at constant speed. Velocity still changes because its direction changes, so acceleration is nonzero and points toward the centre.
Centripetal acceleration
The inward acceleration for uniform circular motion has magnitude a_c=v²/r. It is perpendicular to velocity. At fixed radius, doubling speed quadruples the acceleration.
Centripetal force
The resultant force toward the center: F_c = m v²/r. It is not a new kind of force and is not added to the free-body diagram; Rather, it is the role played by a real force such as tension, friction, gravity, or surface force.
Period (T)
The time required for a complete cycle. The distance in one revolution is the circumference of the circle 2πr, so the speed is v = 2πr/T.
Centrifugal force
Centrifugal force is an inertial force used in a rotating frame. It is not an additional interaction force in an inertial-frame free-body diagram. In this lesson, diagrams use an inertial frame and include only the real forces providing inward acceleration.

The relationships you will use

  • a_c = v² / rCentripetal accelerationtowards the center; And the speed is squared.

How is an object accelerating when its speed is constant?

An apparent contradiction that confuses many people: “constant speed” and “accelerating” in the same sentence.

  1. Refer to the definition: Acceleration is a change in **velocity**, not its magnitude.Why: A vector has magnitude and direction, and a change in either of them is a change in it. Everyday language means speed alone, and hence the confusion.
  2. Visualize the velocity arrow at two close points on the circle: their length is the same and their direction is different.Why: The difference between them is not zero, the difference is Δv, which is what the acceleration measures.
  3. Subtract the two arrows: the difference points inside the circle, i.e. towards the center.Why: This is why the acceleration is called centripetal; Direction is a geometric result, not a convention.
  4. Since acceleration is perpendicular to velocity, the work of the centripetal force is zero.Why: The kinetic energy does not change, so the speed remains constant. The contradiction disappears completely: the acceleration rotates the vector and does not lengthen it. This is the same result that I saw in the energy lesson for work.

Approach 2 · Visual exploration

Velocity is rotating, and acceleration is pointing to the center

Description of the drawing for those who do not see it: A dashed circle with a dot in the middle representing the center. There are three dots around it. From each point, two arrows emerge: a green one tangent to the circle, representing speed, and a red one pointing toward the center, representing acceleration. The direction of the green arrow varies with position, while the red indicates the center in the three positions. Under the drawing is a line that says that the velocity is tangential and changes, and the acceleration is always toward the center.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

Draw the body at the moment you are studying, and determine where the center is. Then make the positive direction toward the center, and collect the real forces on this axis. Then type ΣF = mv²/r and solve. Do not add an arrow in the name of centripetal force, nor add centrifugal force; The first is the result of addition, and the second does not exist in this context.

Example 1 · Centripetal acceleration

A body moving on a circle of radius 25 m with a constant speed 10 m/s., what is its centripetal acceleration?

  1. Substitute in a_c = v²/r = (10)²/25 = 100/25.Why: Speed enters squared; Entering it without a square is the most common error here.
  2. Output 4.0 m/s² toward the center.Why: Mentioning direction is part of the answer: acceleration here does not change the magnitude but rather the direction.

Output: a_c = 4.0 m/s² toward the centre

Example 2 · Maximum speed on a horizontal curve

A car turns on a horizontal curve with radius 50 m, and coefficient of static friction 0.40. What is the maximum speed before sliding? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. The only force towards the center is static friction, the maximum being μ_s N = μ_s mg.Why: The surface is horizontal, so N = mg. is static friction, not kinetic, because the tire rolls and does not slide sideways.
  2. Equal: μ_s m g = m v²/r.Why: The left end is the most that friction can provide, and the right is what movement requires. Exceeding the speed makes what is required greater than what is available and it slips.
  3. Delete m from both sides : v² = μ_s g r = 0.40 × 9.8 × 50 = 196.Why: The mass disappears, so the maximum cornering speed is the same for the truck and the small car on the same surface. This is a result that surprises many.
  4. Take the root: v = 14 m/s.Why: The output of 196 is v² and its unit is m²/s²; The question is about speed.

Output: v_max = 14 m/s

Questions often left unexplained

Why am I pushed out into the corner if there is no repulsive force?

Because you are not being pushed out at all; Your body tends to continue in a straight line by its own inertia, The car turns under you. You collide with the door, and the door pushes you **inside**, This is the only real horizontal force over you. The feeling of being pushed out is real, but its interpretation is insufficiency, not force.

If central power is just a role, why does it have a special name?

The name describes the direction, not the source, as we say “vertical force.” Its benefit is that it combines different positions under one equation: Whatever the source of the force, if it points to the center, its magnitude is mv²/r. The mistake is to think that the name is an independent type and draw an additional arrow.

How does the water stay in the upside down bucket on top of the spin?

Because gravity at that moment points to the center of the circle, so it plays the role of a central force. The water does "fall", but it falls along a curved path that matches the path of the bucket. If the speed slows below a certain limit, gravity becomes greater than necessary for the bending, and the water leaves the bucket.

Circular motion and inward force

Physical idea

Constant speed around a circle still means acceleration because velocity direction changes. The net radial force points toward the center; centripetal force is a role played by real forces, not an extra force to draw.

First move

Choose inward as positive and name the real forces with radial components. Write their signed radial sum as mv²/r. Use tangential equations separately if speed changes.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg mass moves at 3 m/s in a circle of radius 1.5 m. Required inward net force is 2·9/1.5=12 N. If a string supplies it, tension is 12 N only when other radial forces are absent.

Check yourself

A 1 kg object moves at 6 m/s around a 2 m circle. Find inward acceleration and net radial force.

Reveal answer and reasoning

ac=36/2=18 m/s² and Fr=1·18=18 N inward.

Common error. Do not add a separate “centripetal force” arrow to real tension, gravity, or friction arrows.

Angular motion and torque

Physical idea

Angular displacement and angular speed describe how a rigid body turns. Torque measures how strongly a force tends to rotate it, depending on force magnitude, lever arm, and the angle between them.

First move

Use radians in v=rω. For torque, measure r from the chosen pivot to the point where force acts, then use only the component perpendicular to r. Assign positive or negative torque by rotation sense.

Worked reasoning

A 10 N force perpendicular to a 0.30 m wrench produces torque 3.0 N·m. If the same force acts along the wrench, sinφ=0 and torque about the bolt is zero.

Check yourself

A wheel has radius 0.20 m and angular speed 15 rad/s. Find rim speed.

Reveal answer and reasoning

v=rω=0.20·15=3.0 m/s.

Common error. Force alone does not determine torque; the perpendicular lever arm matters.

Circular motion and inward force

Meaning

An object can have constant speed and still accelerate because its velocity direction changes. Centripetal force is the name for the net inward component of real forces.

First move

Draw the circle center, choose inward positive, and identify which real forces have inward components. Use speed at the point of interest, not a force “outward” added to the free-body diagram.

Worked reasoning

At 10 m/s on a radius 5 m circle, centripetal acceleration is 20 m/s². A 2 kg object needs 40 N net inward force.

Your turn

A 1 kg object moves at 6 m/s around a 3 m radius. Find net inward force.

Reveal answer and reasoning

F=mv²/r=1·36/3=12 N inward.

If it went wrong. A circular path does not imply an extra physical “centripetal force” in addition to tension, friction, gravity, or the normal force.

Flat and banked curves

Meaning

A flat curve needs static friction to provide inward force. An ideal frictionless bank instead uses the inward component of the normal force.

First move

Draw the force diagram before selecting a formula. The flat-road expression describes the threshold of slip; the bank expression assumes no friction.

Worked reasoning

On a flat curve with μs=0.5, r=20 m, and g=10 m/s², the maximum speed before slipping is √(0.5·20·10)=10 m/s.

Your turn

With μs=0.4, r=25 m, and g=10, find the flat-curve speed limit.

Reveal answer and reasoning

vmax=√(0.4·25·10)=10 m/s.

If it went wrong. The two formulas describe different physical assumptions; a banked curve with friction needs a new force balance.

Circular motion and inward force

When you see

A trajectory bends, even when speed stays constant.

Choose this model

Particle with radial acceleration; identify the real force that supplies net inward force.

Principle

Centripetal acceleration is a direction of net acceleration, not an additional force. Tangential acceleration changes speed; radial acceleration changes direction.

First move

Point the radial axis toward the center. Resolve gravity, tension, friction, and normal force along it.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg mass at 4 m/s on a 2 m radius needs 16 N net inward force.

Try it

At twice the speed and the same radius and mass, how does required inward force change?

Reveal answer and reasoning

It becomes four times larger because mv²/r depends on speed squared.

Repair the error. A constant speed on a curved path is accelerated motion.

Angular kinematics

When you see

A wheel turns and angular acceleration is given.

Choose this model

Rigid body rotating about a fixed axis.

Principle

Angular position, velocity, and acceleration parallel linear kinematics only when angular acceleration is constant. Points on a rigid wheel share ω but have different linear speeds.

First move

Work in radians, choose positive rotation, and use r to connect angular and tangential quantities.

Worked reasoning

Starting at 2 rad/s with α=3 rad/s² for 4 s, a wheel reaches 14 rad/s and turns through 32 rad.

Try it

At ω=6 rad/s, what is tangential speed at r=0.50 m?

Reveal answer and reasoning

vt=rω=3 m/s. A point at twice the radius would have twice this speed.

Repair the error. Do not put degrees directly into equations derived using radians.

Question types

Each style examines something different. These selected patterns cover the lesson objectives, It is not all that can be asked about the topic.

The first type: Centripetal acceleration

Square the speed and divide by the radius.

A body rotating on a circle of radius 25 m at a constant speed 10 m/s., what is its centripetal acceleration?

a_c = v²/r = 100/25 = 4.0 m/s² toward the center.

The second type: Central force

Enter the mass and divide by the radius.

A car of mass 1200 kg turns with speed 20 m/s on a curve of radius 50 m. How much centripetal force is needed?

‎F_c = m v²/r = 1200 × 400 / 50 = 9600 N‎، It is provided by static friction between the tires and the road.

The third type is the speed of the time course

Use the circumference of the circle, not the radius.

A body makes a complete revolution on a circle of radius 2.0 m at 4.0 s. What is its speed?

‎v = 2πr/T = 2π(2.0)/4.0 = 12.57/4.0 = 3.14 m/s‎.

Fourth mode · Maximum cornering speed

Friction equals the demand and takes the root.

Horizontal curve with radius 50 m and coefficient of static friction 0.40. What is the maximum cornering speed? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

μ_s m g = m v²/r, the mass is deleted and v² = μ_s g r = 0.40 × 9.8 × 50 = 196 remains, And v = 14 m/s.

The fifth type is the resultant force with constant speed

You realize that the stability of the amount does not mean the absence of the outcome.

A body of mass 2.0 kg is rotating with a constant speed of 4.0 m/s on a circle of radius 8.0 m. What is the magnitude of the resultant forces acting on it?

Constant magnitude does not mean lack of acceleration: the direction changes. The resultant is m v²/r = 2.0 × 16 / 8.0 = 4.0 N toward the center.
