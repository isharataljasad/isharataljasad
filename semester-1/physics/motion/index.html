PHY 101 · TOPIC 02

Vectors and motion

Read direction and motion graphs before choosing an equation.

Book →Pearson →Educator →

The idea

Velocity has direction; speed does not. Position-time slope is velocity, and velocity-time slope is acceleration. Constant-acceleration equations apply only over intervals where that assumption is justified.

A first example

Starting from rest with constant acceleration 2 m/s² for 3 s gives v=6 m/s and displacement 9 m.

Engineering connection. A conveyor or moving sample can be described by position, velocity and acceleration without confusing the quantities.

What this explanation develops

  • Distinguish distance from displacement, and speed from velocity.
  • You read the position curve and the velocity curve: slope, area, and what they indicate.
  • You choose the equation of motion with constant acceleration from the data and the unknown.
  • It solves the motion of a projectile with two components and explains the independence of horizontal and vertical motion.

Definitions & meaning

A vector is a quantity that has both magnitude and direction. We resolve the velocity into vx=v cosθ and vy=v sinθ when θ is measured from the horizontal axis. Without air resistance, ax=0 and ay=−g: the horizontals are constant and the verticals change, but time itself connects them. At the top of vy=0, neither vx nor the gravitational wheel disappear.

For a projectile that starts and ends at the same altitude, the flight time is 2v sinθ/g and the range is v² sin(2θ)/g. If the two altitudes are different, solve the equation y first to choose the positive physical time, then calculate x=vx t; Do not use the special term form. In relative motion, we collect the vectors within Galileo's description: the speed of the boat relative to the ground is equal to its speed relative to the water plus the speed of the water relative to the ground.

Displacement
Displacement is the change in position, Δx=x_f−x_i. It is a vector and depends only on the starting and ending positions. Returning to the starting position gives zero displacement even when the distance travelled is large.
Distance
Distance is the nonnegative length of the path travelled. It is at least the magnitude of displacement. In straight-line motion with no reversal, distance equals displacement magnitude.
Velocity
Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time: v_avg=Δx/Δt. Instantaneous velocity is dx/dt, the slope of a position–time graph. In one dimension its sign indicates direction relative to the chosen positive axis.
Speed
Speed is the magnitude of instantaneous velocity and is nonnegative. Average speed is total distance divided by elapsed time. A completed round trip can have positive average speed but zero average velocity.
Acceleration
Average acceleration is Δv/Δt; instantaneous acceleration is dv/dt, measured in m/s². Acceleration can change the magnitude or direction of velocity. In one dimension, opposite signs of velocity and acceleration mean the object is slowing down.

Why does the area under the velocity curve equal the displacement?

A rule that is often memorized for no reason, although its reason is seen in two lines.

  1. Take a motion with constant speed v during the duration t: displacement Δx = v t.

    This is the simple case we're building on, which is the definition of speed upside down.

  2. Draw v against time: a horizontal line emerges, and the area below it is a rectangle with length t and height v.

    The area of the rectangle is v × t, which is literally the same expression we used to calculate the displacement.

  3. If the speed changes, divide the time into short periods in which the speed is almost constant.

    Each interval gives a small rectangle, and the sum of the rectangles is the sum of the displacements.

  4. Add the rectangles and reduce their width: the sum equals the area under the curve.

    The rule is not a convention but a result: area is a way to add v × Δt over all periods. This is why it is valid for any shape of the curve, not just the straight line.

Practical method: Write the five quantities v₀, v, a, t, and Δx, and mark the knowns. And the unknown is required. There remains one quantity that is neither known nor required, and it is what tells you the equation: Choose the equation that does not contain it. This saves you from solving a system of two equations in most questions.

Worked examples

Determine the positive direction first and write it on the paper, so all signs after it are measured to it. Then take stock of the five quantities and mark what is known and what is required. Then choose the equation that excludes the remaining quantity. It is not replaced before the units are unified; The equation does not alert you to mixing minutes with seconds.

Example: 1 · Stopping distance

A car with speed 20 m/s brakes with acceleration −4.0 m/s² to a stop. How far do you travel?

  1. : v₀ = 20, v = 0, a = −4.0, Δx are required, and t is neither known nor required.

    The missing quantity is time, which determines the equation without experimentation.

  2. Choose v² = v₀² + 2aΔx because it alone does not contain t.

    Choosing an equation without the missing quantity will avoid solving two equations together.

  3. Replace : 0 = 400 + 2(−4.0)Δx, which is 8Δx = 400.

    The final velocity is zero because the car stopped; This information is extracted from words, not from numbers.

  4. Solve: Δx = 50 m.

    The resultant is positive even though the acceleration is negative, and this is true: The car moves forward while its acceleration opposes its movement.

Result: Δx = 50 m

Example 2 · reading area under the velocity curve

A body starts from rest and its speed increases linearly until it reaches 12 m/s after 6.0 s. What is its displacement during this period?

  1. v with t: Draw a line from (0, 0) to (6.0, 12), and the shape is a triangle underneath.

    The speed changes linearly, so the shape is a triangle, not a rectangle, and the difference between them is a factor of half.

  2. Calculate the area : ½ × 6.0 × 12 = 36 m.

    The area under the velocity curve is the displacement, as in the derivation above.

  3. Verify with the formula : a = 12/6.0 = 2.0 m/s² and x = ½(2.0)(6.0)² = 36 m.

    The two paths agree, and this is no coincidence: the equation ½at² is the area of the triangle itself written in symbols.

Result: Δx = 36 m

Horizontal extrusion

An object is thrown horizontally at a speed of 6 m/s from a height of 20 m. Use g=10.

  1. 0=20−5t²، t=2 s

    The initial vertical velocity is zero.

  2. x=6×2=12 m

    Horizontal velocity is constant.

  3. vy=−10×2=−20 m/s

    The body faces down upon arrival.

Result: t=2 s; range=12 m

Further worked applications

The first type is an offset, not a space

You subtract the return from the outgoing instead of adding them together.

A person walks 300 m east and then 100 m returns west. What is its displacement in metres?

Make East positive: Δx = +300 − 100 = +200 m. The distance traveled is 400 m, which is another quantity.

Result: 200 m

The second type is final speed

It adds to the initial speed instead of neglecting it.

An object with initial velocity 5.0 m/s and acceleration 3.0 m/s². What is its speed after 4.0 s?

‎v = v₀ + at = 5.0 + (3.0)(4.0) = 5.0 + 12 = 17 m/s‎.

Result: 17 m/s

The third type: Distance without time

Choose the equation that does not contain the missing quantity.

A car with speed 30 m/s brakes with acceleration −5.0 m/s² to a stop. What is the stopping distance in metres?

0 = (30)² + 2(−5.0)Δx, any 10Δx = 900, then Δx = 90 m.

Result: 90 m

Fourth type: Area under the velocity curve

Distinguish the triangle from the rectangle, that is, do not forget the half factor.

A body starts from rest and its speed increases linearly up to 16 m/s through 5.0 s. What is its displacement during this period in metres?

The figure under the velocity curve is a triangle with base 5.0 s and height 16 m/s. The area is ½ × 5.0 × 16 = 40 m.

Result: 40 m

Fifth pattern · Displacement from rest

You use the ½at² limit and do not mix it with the speed limit.

A body starts from rest with constant acceleration 2.0 m/s². How much distance does he cover in meters during 5.0 s?

‎x = v₀t + ½at² = 0 + ½(2.0)(25) = 25 m‎.

Result: 25 m

When the movement is in a straight line and in one direction, there is no return. Any reversal in direction makes the distance greater than the amount of displacement. Because the distance adds all the positive lengths, while the displacement subtracts the return from the outgoing.

Useful background

Consistency of units before substitution

Unite all quantities before substituting: time in seconds, distance in metres, and speed in m/s. The 72 km/h = 20 m/s is divided by 3.6. Write down the units in each step to catch the error before it costs you arithmetic.
