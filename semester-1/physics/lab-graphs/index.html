THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Label axes with quantities and units. A straight-line slope is a ratio of changes, with units from the axes. Repeated measurements reveal scatter; they do not by themselves remove calibration error. Record observations separately from explanations.

Worked example

On a position-time graph, points (1 s, 2 m) and (3 s, 8 m) give average slope (8−2)/(3−1)=3 m/s.

Engineering connection A reproducible laboratory record states what was measured, how it was measured and the model used to interpret it.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

Every measurement is limited by accuracy and repeatability. Record the reading, its unit, and the known instrument limit; Estimating half the smallest scale of an analog instrument is a basic practical rule, while digital requires the stated accuracy of the instrument. A regular error may move all the readings together and not go away once they are repeated. Repetition detects random dispersion but does not prove the validity of the titration.

For data (xi,yi), the least squares slope gives Σ(xi−x̄)(yi−ȳ)/Σ(xi−x̄)² and the intercept ȳ−mx̄, if this is a fitted line model and the uncertainty of x is small compared to its effect in y. I plot the residuals: a regular curvature in them indicates that the line does not describe the data even if its fit appears good. Don't just draw the line between the first point and the last point.

Plot uncertainty bars when data are available. In small independent error approximations, the relative uncertainty of y=x² is approximately twice that of x; To add two independent quantities, you add the uncertainty variances, not the values ​​of the two measurements. These are hypotheses and not a recipe for every error. An experiment report states the objective, method, data, drawing, calculations, limitations, and conclusion; Don't copy perfect results and call them measurements.

Independent and dependent variables
Plot the independent variable on the horizontal axis and the dependent variable on the vertical axis. For a nonzero straight-line slope, swapping axes takes its reciprocal and changes its units. The relationship and the experimental design determine what each axis means.
Line of best fit
A fitted line represents the trend of scattered measurements. It need not pass through an individual data point or through the origin. Use widely separated points on the fitted line to estimate its slope. A formal fit should state its method and any uncertainty assumptions.
Linearisation
Choose a function for the axis so that the relationship becomes straight, such as plotting T² instead of T. Its benefit is that the slope is then a single number with a meaning, while the slope of the curve changes from one point to another.
Extrapolation
Estimating a value outside the range of measurements. It is always weaker than internal reading, because the relationship may break outside of what you have experienced. A spring is linear up to a certain limit after which it deforms, and the internal data does not tell you that.
Intercept
The vertical intercept is the dependent-variable value predicted when the independent variable is zero. It can represent a physical initial value or suggest a systematic offset. Interpret it using the model and the measured range.

Why do we plot instead of calculating the slope directly from the curve?

It may be said: Why don't we take the slope of the curve and finish? The answer is that the slope is not a single number.

  1. Draw the time of the pendulum T with the length L:, producing a curve that increases and then flattens.Why: Because the relationship is T ∝ √L not T ∝ L, and the root makes this curvature.
  2. Try taking the slope of the curve: you'll get a different value at each position.Why: The curve does not have a single slope, and a single number cannot be attributed to it. Any number you get will depend on the two points you chose, and this is not a measurement.
  3. Square both sides of T = 2π√(L/g) and you get T² = (4π²/g) L.Why: The relationship became y = m x with two new variables : T² and L.
  4. Draw T² with L: coming out with a line of slope 4π²/g, so g = 4π²/slope.Why: The slope is now a single, meaningful number, and from it we extract a physical constant. Straightness is not a property of the data, but rather the result of our choice of the two axes.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

Before drawing: Write down the theoretically expected relationship, then arrange it in the form y = m x + c Determine what to place on each axis. After drawing: Draw the alignment line, then take two far apart points from it, then calculate the slope in its unit. Then translate the slope into the quantity required. In the description: say what the data supports within its scope, and do not generalize beyond it.

Example 1 · mile and its meaning

Representing the displacement with time, and on the matching line there are two points : (2.0 s, 4.0 m) and (6.0 s, 20.0 m). What quantity does the slope represent and what is its value?

  1. Calculate: (20.0 − 4.0)/(6.0 − 2.0) = 16.0/4.0 = 4.0.Why: The difference in the vertical over the difference in the horizontal, in this order and not the other way around.
  2. Module m/s.Why: Vertical axis unit on horizontal unit; It is what is called quantity.
  3. So the slope speed value is 4.0 m/s.Why: A non-zero intersection with the vertical axis means that the body did not start from the origin. This is additional information, not an error.

Output: slope = 4.0 m/s (velocity)

Questions often left unexplained

Is the reconciliation line forced to pass through the origin?

No, unless physics dictates it. Representing force with elongation must go through the origin because zero elongation means zero force. If it does not pass, this reveals a systematic error, such as an incorrect zero on the scale. Forcing him to go through the original despite the data hides the error and does not correct it.

How many data points are enough?

Two points define a line but do not prove a line. Sin is a claim about the relationship, and proving it requires sufficient points distributed over the entire period. Five to seven points is a reasonable guideline in a first-year laboratory, with repeated measurement at each point.

What is wrong with saying “the relationship is linear” after one experiment?

It generalizes over an unmeasured extent. Honest wording: “The relationship is linear in the range measured from such and such to that.” The spring is linear until the limit of elasticity and then stops, and the resistance is constant until it heats up. And what you do not measure, do not describe.

Measurements, units, and model limits

Physical idea

A number is a physical answer only when its units and meaning are clear. Dimensional analysis checks whether an equation can be sensible; it cannot prove that the physics model is correct.

First move

Before calculating, identify the system, draw a simple representation, convert to SI units, and state the approximation. Compare dimensions on both sides of an equation and round the final result to a precision justified by the data.

Worked reasoning

A car moves at 72 km/h. Dividing by 3.6 gives 20 m/s. In 5 s at constant speed it travels 100 m. The calculation would fail if the car were accelerating and 72 km/h were only its starting speed.

Check yourself

A runner covers 150 m in 25 s. Find average speed and check its units.

Reveal answer and reasoning

150/25 = 6.0 m/s. Distance divided by time has dimension L/T.

Common error. A mathematically consistent result can still use the wrong model; write the condition that makes the equation valid.

Position, velocity, acceleration, and motion graphs

Physical idea

Displacement is final minus initial position and can be negative. Speed uses distance traveled; velocity uses displacement. On a position-time graph slope is velocity. On a velocity-time graph slope is acceleration and signed area is displacement.

First move

Read axis labels first. For a straight segment, calculate rise over run. For area under v(t), split the region into familiar shapes and preserve area below the time axis as negative.

Worked reasoning

Velocity rises linearly from 0 to 8 m/s in 4 s, then stays at 8 m/s for 2 s. Displacement is triangle 16 m plus rectangle 16 m, totaling 32 m.

Check yourself

A body moves at +3 m/s for 4 s, then −2 m/s for 3 s. Find displacement and total distance.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Displacement is 12−6=6 m. Distance is 12+6=18 m.

Common error. Area under a velocity graph is displacement, not always distance; use absolute areas for distance.

Significant figures and dimensional checks

Meaning

Units can reject a wrong equation: terms being added must share dimensions. Report precision supported by measurements, but retain extra digits during intermediate calculations.

First move

Check dimensions before arithmetic. For multiplication or division, use the fewest significant figures in the inputs; for addition or subtraction, use the least precise decimal place.

Worked reasoning

A length 2.0 m divided by 3.00 s is 0.67 m/s to two significant figures. Adding 12.3 m and 0.456 m gives 12.8 m to one decimal place.

Your turn

A formula claims distance equals speed plus acceleration times time. Is it dimensionally valid?

Reveal answer and reasoning

No. Both terms on the right have units of speed, not distance.

If it went wrong. Dimensional agreement is necessary but does not prove that the physical model is valid.

Read motion graphs before equations

Meaning

A position-time slope gives velocity. A velocity-time slope gives acceleration, while signed area under a velocity-time graph gives displacement.

First move

Read both axis labels and units first. For a segment use rise over run. For area split into rectangles or triangles, preserving the sign below the time axis.

Worked reasoning

Velocity rises uniformly from 0 to 6 m/s during 4 s. The triangular area is (1/2)·4·6=12 m of displacement.

Your turn

Velocity is −2 m/s for 3 s. Find displacement and distance traveled.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Signed area gives −6 m displacement; distance is 6 m.

If it went wrong. The area under a position-time curve does not generally represent displacement. Use its slope instead.

Measurement and dimensional reasoning

When you see

A quantity is reported in unfamiliar units or a proposed equation looks suspicious.

Choose this model

Quantity with dimensions; idealized particle or system only after the units are clear.

Principle

An equation can add only quantities of the same dimension. A unit check can reject an equation but cannot establish that the physical model is true. Keep extra digits until the final reported answer.

First move

Cancel units explicitly, estimate the expected scale, and report precision supported by the given data.

Worked reasoning

72 km/h is 72/3.6 = 20 m/s. At constant speed for 5 s, the distance is 100 m.

Try it

Convert 54 km/h to m/s, then find its constant-speed travel in 4 s.

Reveal answer and reasoning

15 m/s and 60 m. The conversion lowers the numerical speed because one metre per second is a larger unit than one kilometre per hour.

Repair the error. A dimensionally valid equation can still describe the wrong situation; state the physical condition before substitution.

Motion graphs and rates

When you see

The question gives position, velocity, or acceleration as a graph or function of time.

Choose this model

Particle; choose a graph slope for a rate and a signed area for accumulation.

Principle

The slope of a position graph is velocity. The slope of a velocity graph is acceleration. Signed area under velocity gives displacement; absolute area gives distance.

First move

Mark the time interval and axis units. For a piecewise graph, calculate each signed area before adding.

Worked reasoning

A velocity of +4 m/s for 3 s, then -2 m/s for 2 s, gives displacement 12-4 = 8 m and distance 12+4 = 16 m.

Try it

Velocity is -3 m/s for 4 s. Find displacement and distance.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Displacement is -12 m; distance is 12 m. The sign records direction, not the amount of ground covered.

Repair the error. The area under a position-time graph is not generally a displacement.
