PHY 101 · TOPIC 08

Laboratory graphs and evidence

Describe a measured relationship without overstating it.

Book →Pearson →Educator →

The idea

Label axes with quantities and units. A straight-line slope is a ratio of changes, with units from the axes. Repeated measurements reveal scatter; they do not by themselves remove calibration error. Record observations separately from explanations.

A first example

On a position-time graph, points (1 s, 2 m) and (3 s, 8 m) give average slope (8−2)/(3−1)=3 m/s.

Engineering connection. A reproducible laboratory record states what was measured, how it was measured and the model used to interpret it.

What this explanation develops

  • You choose the two axes that make the relationship linear before you calculate any slope.
  • You calculate the slope from two points on the alignment line, and read its unit and physical meaning.
  • It differentiates between a reading within the measurement range and an extrapolation outside it, and does not claim more than what the data supports.
  • It estimates uncertainty and uses the slope of the best line while checking units and bounds.

Definitions & meaning

Every measurement is limited by accuracy and repeatability. Record the reading, its unit, and the known instrument limit; Estimating half the smallest scale of an analog instrument is a basic practical rule, while digital requires the stated accuracy of the instrument. A regular error may move all the readings together and not go away once they are repeated. Repetition detects random dispersion but does not prove the validity of the titration.

For data (xi,yi), the least squares slope gives Σ(xi−x̄)(yi−ȳ)/Σ(xi−x̄)² and the intercept ȳ−mx̄, if this is a fitted line model and the uncertainty of x is small compared to its effect in y. I plot the residuals: a regular curvature in them indicates that the line does not describe the data even if its fit appears good. Don't just draw the line between the first point and the last point.

Plot uncertainty bars when data are available. In small independent error approximations, the relative uncertainty of y=x² is approximately twice that of x; To add two independent quantities, you add the uncertainty variances, not the values ​​of the two measurements. These are hypotheses and not a recipe for every error. An experiment report states the objective, method, data, drawing, calculations, limitations, and conclusion; Don't copy perfect results and call them measurements.

Independent and dependent variables
Plot the independent variable on the horizontal axis and the dependent variable on the vertical axis. For a nonzero straight-line slope, swapping axes takes its reciprocal and changes its units. The relationship and the experimental design determine what each axis means.
Line of best fit
A fitted line represents the trend of scattered measurements. It need not pass through an individual data point or through the origin. Use widely separated points on the fitted line to estimate its slope. A formal fit should state its method and any uncertainty assumptions.
Linearisation
Choose a function for the axis so that the relationship becomes straight, such as plotting T² instead of T. Its benefit is that the slope is then a single number with a meaning, while the slope of the curve changes from one point to another.
Extrapolation
Estimating a value outside the range of measurements. It is always weaker than internal reading, because the relationship may break outside of what you have experienced. A spring is linear up to a certain limit after which it deforms, and the internal data does not tell you that.
Intercept
The vertical intercept is the dependent-variable value predicted when the independent variable is zero. It can represent a physical initial value or suggest a systematic offset. Interpret it using the model and the measured range.

Why do we plot instead of calculating the slope directly from the curve?

It may be said: Why don't we take the slope of the curve and finish? The answer is that the slope is not a single number.

  1. Draw the time of the pendulum T with the length L:, producing a curve that increases and then flattens.

    Because the relationship is T ∝ √L not T ∝ L, and the root makes this curvature.

  2. Try taking the slope of the curve: you'll get a different value at each position.

    The curve does not have a single slope, and a single number cannot be attributed to it. Any number you get will depend on the two points you chose, and this is not a measurement.

  3. Square both sides of T = 2π√(L/g) and you get T² = (4π²/g) L.

    The relationship became y = m x with two new variables : T² and L.

  4. Draw T² with L: coming out with a line of slope 4π²/g, so g = 4π²/slope.

    The slope is now a single, meaningful number, and from it we extract a physical constant. Straightness is not a property of the data, but rather the result of our choice of the two axes.

Worked examples

Before drawing: Write down the theoretically expected relationship, then arrange it in the form y = m x + c Determine what to place on each axis. After drawing: Draw the alignment line, then take two far apart points from it, then calculate the slope in its unit. Then translate the slope into the quantity required. In the description: say what the data supports within its scope, and do not generalize beyond it.

  1. Calculate: (20.0 − 4.0)/(6.0 − 2.0) = 16.0/4.0 = 4.0.

    The difference in the vertical over the difference in the horizontal, in this order and not the other way around.

Result: slope = 4.0 m/s (velocity)

Useful background

Calculate the slope from two points

slope = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). Take two points **on the matching line**, not two data points, and space them as far apart as possible to reduce the reading effect. The unit of slope is the unit of the vertical axis divided by the unit of the horizontal, which reveals its physical meaning.

Reading complex units

The slope represents displacement with time and its unit is m/s, so it is velocity. The slope of the representation of force with elongation and its unit is N/m, so it is a spring constant. Write the unit before you name the quantity, not after you name it.
