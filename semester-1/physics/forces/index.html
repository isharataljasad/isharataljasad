Newton laws and force diagrams

Define the object and sum external forces on it.

TRY FIRST

Start with a short challenge

A net force of 12 N acts on a 3 kg object. Find its acceleration.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Draw all actual external forces on the chosen object. Choose axes and write a signed force balance. The net force equals mass times acceleration; motion alone does not imply a net force.

Worked example

A 5 kg object with a net horizontal force of 15 N accelerates at 3 m/s². Weight and normal force may cancel vertically without cancelling horizontal force.

Engineering connection A diagram keeps drive, support and resistance forces distinct when modelling moving equipment.

Newton laws and free body diagrams

Physical idea

An object accelerates when the vector sum of external forces is nonzero. A free body diagram contains forces acting on one chosen object. Newton third-law partners act on different objects and cannot cancel on one diagram.

First move

Isolate the body, draw only real external forces, choose axes, and write a force equation for each axis. If acceleration is zero, the sum is zero; a moving object can still have zero acceleration.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg block on a horizontal frictionless surface is pulled right by 6 N. Its weight and normal force cancel vertically, while horizontal acceleration is 6/2=3 m/s².

Check yourself

A 4 kg object has 18 N right and 6 N left. Find its acceleration.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Net force is 12 N right, so a=12/4=3 m/s² right.

Common error. Do not put the force exerted by the object on its surroundings on the object’s own free body diagram.

Connected objects and tension

Physical idea

Objects joined by a taut, light string over an ideal pulley share the same acceleration magnitude. The string tension is the same on both sides only under the ideal string and pulley model.

First move

Draw a separate free body diagram for each mass. Pick one consistent positive direction along the string for the whole system. Write both force equations, then eliminate tension to solve acceleration.

Worked reasoning

For m1=2 kg and m2=3 kg, with g=10, the ideal Atwood acceleration is (3−2)10/5=2 m/s², with m2 downward. From T−m1g=m1a, tension is 24 N.

Check yourself

For 1 kg and 3 kg masses on an ideal pulley, find acceleration using g=10.

Reveal answer and reasoning

a=(3−1)10/(1+3)=5 m/s²; the 3 kg mass moves down.

Common error. Do not set tension equal to the heavier weight when the system accelerates.

Newton laws and the system boundary

Meaning

Acceleration is caused by the net external force on the chosen object or system. Zero net force means constant velocity, which may be nonzero.

First move

Name the body under study. List only forces acting on that body, pick axes, then write a separate signed force equation for each axis.

Worked reasoning

An 18 N force acts right on a 4 kg cart while 6 N acts left. Net force is 12 N right, so acceleration is 3 m/s² right.

Your turn

A 5 kg object has 25 N right and 10 N left. Find acceleration.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Net force 15 N right gives 3 m/s² right.

If it went wrong. The equal-and-opposite reaction force acts on another body, so it does not cancel a force on this body.

Free-body diagrams and normal force

Meaning

A free-body diagram is an inventory of real external forces on one isolated body. Weight acts downward; a surface normal acts perpendicular to contact, not always upward.

First move

Replace the body with a dot or box. Draw weight, contact forces, tension, and applied forces from their actual sources. Resolve tilted forces before writing equations.

Worked reasoning

A 5 kg mass hangs at rest from a light string. With g=10 m/s², tension is 50 N because vertical acceleration is zero.

Your turn

A 4 kg box rests on a horizontal floor with no other vertical force. What normal force acts, using g=10?

Reveal answer and reasoning

N−mg=0, so N=40 N upward.

If it went wrong. N=mg is a result of this particular vertical balance, not a definition valid for all surfaces.

Equilibrium with forces in two dimensions

Meaning

Static equilibrium and constant-velocity motion both require zero acceleration. In two dimensions, horizontal and vertical force components must each balance.

First move

Resolve every angled force, then solve two component equations. Check whether the resulting magnitudes can be drawn in the stated directions.

Worked reasoning

A point has forces 3 N right and 4 N up. A third force of 5 N down-left, with components (−3,−4) N, makes equilibrium.

Your turn

For 5 N right and 12 N up, what single force balances them?

Reveal answer and reasoning

A force (−5,−12) N, magnitude 13 N, directed down-left.

If it went wrong. Balancing magnitudes alone misses the direction required by each component equation.

Tension and connected bodies

Meaning

A taut massless string over an ideal pulley gives the connected bodies equal acceleration magnitudes. Tension is common only under the ideal-string, ideal-pulley model.

First move

Draw one diagram per mass, choose consistent positive directions along the string, and write both Newton equations before eliminating tension.

Worked reasoning

For hanging masses 2 kg and 3 kg with g=10, acceleration is (3−2)10/5=2 m/s²; the 3 kg mass moves down and the string tension is 24 N.

Your turn

For 1 kg and 4 kg masses with g=10, find acceleration and tension.

Reveal answer and reasoning

a=(4−1)10/5=6 m/s². For the 1 kg side, T−10=1·6, so T=16 N.

If it went wrong. The tension is not the heavier weight while the system accelerates.

Free-body diagrams and Newton laws

When you see

The target is an acceleration, contact force, or equilibrium condition.

Choose this model

One isolated body with only real external forces; particle under net force.

Principle

A net force changes velocity. Zero net force means zero acceleration, not necessarily zero velocity. Third-law partners act on different bodies.

First move

Choose one body, draw gravity and each contact or applied force, then resolve the sum on chosen axes.

Worked reasoning

A 4 kg cart has 18 N right and 6 N left. Its net force is 12 N right and its acceleration is 3 m/s² right.

Try it

A 5 kg object has 25 N east and 10 N west. Find its acceleration.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Net force is 15 N east, so acceleration is 3 m/s² east.

Repair the error. Do not place an equal-and-opposite third-law partner on the same free-body diagram.
