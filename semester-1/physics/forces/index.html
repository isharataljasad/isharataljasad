Newton laws and force diagrams

Define the object and sum external forces on it.

Before you start

What will you be able to do after this lesson?

  • You first identify the body and then draw the external forces on it alone in a free-body diagram.
  • ΣF = ma is applied to each axis, and calculates acceleration, force, or mass.
  • Distinguish a third-law pair, which acts on two different bodies, from balanced forces acting on one body.

What this lesson does not cover

  • Friction and the inclined plane are the topic of the next lesson; Here we assume surfaces are smooth unless otherwise stated.
  • Moments and rotational equilibrium are not covered in this lesson.
  • Accelerated systems as frames of reference (imaginary forces) are out of scope.
  • Air resistance is neglected in all of the examples in this lesson.

We mentioned them so that you do not think that you have completed the whole topic.

Prerequisites

  • Movement with constant acceleration

    You need it because: Newton's second law gives you acceleration, then you need equations of motion to arrive at speed or distance.

    v = v₀ + at, x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at² and v² = v₀² + 2aΔx. The issue of forces often ends with acceleration, and the second half remains pure motion.

    Study vectors and motion

  • Decompose a vector into two components

    You need it because: Inclined force does not enter ΣF = ma as is; Its vehicle enters on each axle.

    The force of F makes an angle of θ with the horizontal: its horizontal component F cos θ and vertical F sin θ. The sum of the squares of the two components is equal to the square of the magnitude, and this is a quick check of the validity of the factoring. Note that cos accompanies the axis from which the angle is measured.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Draw all actual external forces on the chosen object. Choose axes and write a signed force balance. The net force equals mass times acceleration; motion alone does not imply a net force.

Worked example

A 5 kg object with a net horizontal force of 15 N accelerates at 3 m/s². Weight and normal force may cancel vertically without cancelling horizontal force.

Engineering connection A diagram keeps drive, support and resistance forces distinct when modelling moving equipment.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

Free-body diagram
A drawing that isolates a single body and shows the external forces acting on it alone. The forces with which it affects others are not depicted, nor are the internal forces between its parts. The first step is not drawing, but choosing the body: changing the choice changes the whole plan.
Net force
The vector sum of the external forces: ΣF. It is the only one that enters Newton's second law; Balanced forces are not neglected in the drawing But it cancels some of them out in total.
Weight
The force of gravity of the Earth on the body : W = mg, in newtons. It is fundamentally different from mass: mass is the amount of matter in kilograms and does not change with location. Weight is a force that changes with g.
Normal force
A force exerted by a surface on an object perpendicular to the surface. It is not always equal to weight; It is equal to it in a special case: A horizontal surface with no vertical acceleration or other vertical force.
Newton third law
If A acts on B with a force, B acts on A with an equal and opposite force. The crucial difference: the two forces affect two different bodies, They are never combined in one free body diagram nor do they cancel each other out.

The relationships you will use

  • ΣF = m aNewton II: ΣFx = max and ΣFy = may. are applied to each axis independently
  • W = m gWeightForce in newtons, not mass in kilograms.
  • N = m(g + a) In an elevator accelerating upwardNormal force in an elevatorIt becomes m(g − a) on downward acceleration, and zero in free fall.
  • F_AB = −F_BAAction and reactionOn two different bodies; That's why they don't prevent movement.

Why doesn't normal force always equal weight?

N = mg is preserved as a law, which is not a law but the result of a special case. Let's see where it comes from and when it drops.

  1. Draw a body on a stationary horizontal surface: the vertical forces are N up and W down.Why: The chart limits what enters the equation; What is not drawn will not appear in the total.
  2. Apply Newton's second vertically: N − W = m a_y.Why: This is the general equation. Everything after it is a branch of it according to the value of a_y.
  3. If the object is at rest or moving at a constant speed, then a_y = 0 results in N = W.Why: Here the preserved rule originated. Its condition is a_y = 0, which is a condition that is forgotten and the rule is unjustly generalized.
  4. If the body accelerates vertically, such as an elevator ascending with an acceleration of a, then N = m(g + a) > W.Why: Equality was broken because its condition was broken. In free fall, a = g becomes N = 0, and this is apparent weightlessness: Gravity did not disappear, but the surface force did.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

Start with a written sentence: “The object I am studying is this.” Then draw on it the external forces alone: always the weight, and the force of the surface if it is in contact. The force of the thread if it is tied, and any applied force are mentioned. Then choose a positive direction for each axis, and write ΣF = ma for each axis individually. Do not mix the forces of two bodies in one equation; Every body has its own diagram and equation.

Example 1 · Acceleration on a smooth surface

A box of mass 5.0 kg on a smooth horizontal surface is pushed by a horizontal force 20 N. What is its acceleration?

  1. The body is the box. Forces on it: weight down, surface force up, and horizontal push.Why: Identifying the body first prevents the introduction of forces that affect the surface or the driving hand.
  2. Vertical: No acceleration, N = W and N = W do not enter the horizontal calculation.Why: The two axes are independent; Balanced vertical forces do not affect horizontal acceleration.
  3. Horizontally : ΣFx = 20 N alone because the surface is smooth, a = 20/5.0 = 4.0 m/s².Why: Smoothness means no friction, so there is no horizontal force other than pushing.

Output: a = 4.0 m/s²

Example 2 · Surface force in an elevator

A person with mass 60 kg stands in an elevator accelerating upward by 2.0 m/s². What is the force of the ground on it? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. The body is the person. The forces on it are : N up from the floor, and W = mg down.Why: The elevator is not the object studied, its forces are not depicted here.
  2. Make UP positive and write : N − mg = ma.Why: The upward acceleration is positive by our choice, so the right side is positive, so N will be greater than the weight.
  3. Solve: N = m(g + a) = 60 × (9.8 + 2.0) = 60 × 11.8 = 708 N.Why: The weight is 588 N, the force is 708 N, and the difference between 120 N is exactly ma. This is what the rider feels as extra weight when starting the climb.

Output: N = 708 N

Questions often left unexplained

If every action has an equal and opposite reaction, why does anything move at all?

Because the two forces affect two different bodies. You push the box and it accelerates due to your force on it, and the box pushes you and you are affected by its force. The two forces do not come together on one body and are canceled out. As for the two forces that cancel each other, they are on the same body, such as weight and surface force, and they are not an action-reaction pair.

Does “weightlessness” on the space station mean zero gravity?

No. The gravity there is close to 90% than its value on the surface. But the station and everything in it are in continuous free fall around the Earth, so the acceleration is the same for all. There is no contact force between the pioneer and the station ground. The null is N, not W, as in the fourth row of the table.

When do I need a chart for each body instead of just one?

When you are asked about an internal force between two bodies, such as the tension of the thread connecting them. If you are asked about the acceleration of the entire system, one diagram of the total mass is sufficient. Because internal forces cancel each other out. As for tension, it does not appear unless you isolate one of the two bodies.

Newton laws and free body diagrams

Physical idea

An object accelerates when the vector sum of external forces is nonzero. A free body diagram contains forces acting on one chosen object. Newton third-law partners act on different objects and cannot cancel on one diagram.

First move

Isolate the body, draw only real external forces, choose axes, and write a force equation for each axis. If acceleration is zero, the sum is zero; a moving object can still have zero acceleration.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg block on a horizontal frictionless surface is pulled right by 6 N. Its weight and normal force cancel vertically, while horizontal acceleration is 6/2=3 m/s².

Check yourself

A 4 kg object has 18 N right and 6 N left. Find its acceleration.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Net force is 12 N right, so a=12/4=3 m/s² right.

Common error. Do not put the force exerted by the object on its surroundings on the object’s own free body diagram.

Connected objects and tension

Physical idea

Objects joined by a taut, light string over an ideal pulley share the same acceleration magnitude. The string tension is the same on both sides only under the ideal string and pulley model.

First move

Draw a separate free body diagram for each mass. Pick one consistent positive direction along the string for the whole system. Write both force equations, then eliminate tension to solve acceleration.

Worked reasoning

For m1=2 kg and m2=3 kg, with g=10, the ideal Atwood acceleration is (3−2)10/5=2 m/s², with m2 downward. From T−m1g=m1a, tension is 24 N.

Check yourself

For 1 kg and 3 kg masses on an ideal pulley, find acceleration using g=10.

Reveal answer and reasoning

a=(3−1)10/(1+3)=5 m/s²; the 3 kg mass moves down.

Common error. Do not set tension equal to the heavier weight when the system accelerates.

Newton laws and the system boundary

Meaning

Acceleration is caused by the net external force on the chosen object or system. Zero net force means constant velocity, which may be nonzero.

First move

Name the body under study. List only forces acting on that body, pick axes, then write a separate signed force equation for each axis.

Worked reasoning

An 18 N force acts right on a 4 kg cart while 6 N acts left. Net force is 12 N right, so acceleration is 3 m/s² right.

Your turn

A 5 kg object has 25 N right and 10 N left. Find acceleration.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Net force 15 N right gives 3 m/s² right.

If it went wrong. The equal-and-opposite reaction force acts on another body, so it does not cancel a force on this body.

Free-body diagrams and normal force

Meaning

A free-body diagram is an inventory of real external forces on one isolated body. Weight acts downward; a surface normal acts perpendicular to contact, not always upward.

First move

Replace the body with a dot or box. Draw weight, contact forces, tension, and applied forces from their actual sources. Resolve tilted forces before writing equations.

Worked reasoning

A 5 kg mass hangs at rest from a light string. With g=10 m/s², tension is 50 N because vertical acceleration is zero.

Your turn

A 4 kg box rests on a horizontal floor with no other vertical force. What normal force acts, using g=10?

Reveal answer and reasoning

N−mg=0, so N=40 N upward.

If it went wrong. N=mg is a result of this particular vertical balance, not a definition valid for all surfaces.

Equilibrium with forces in two dimensions

Meaning

Static equilibrium and constant-velocity motion both require zero acceleration. In two dimensions, horizontal and vertical force components must each balance.

First move

Resolve every angled force, then solve two component equations. Check whether the resulting magnitudes can be drawn in the stated directions.

Worked reasoning

A point has forces 3 N right and 4 N up. A third force of 5 N down-left, with components (−3,−4) N, makes equilibrium.

Your turn

For 5 N right and 12 N up, what single force balances them?

Reveal answer and reasoning

A force (−5,−12) N, magnitude 13 N, directed down-left.

If it went wrong. Balancing magnitudes alone misses the direction required by each component equation.

Tension and connected bodies

Meaning

A taut massless string over an ideal pulley gives the connected bodies equal acceleration magnitudes. Tension is common only under the ideal-string, ideal-pulley model.

First move

Draw one diagram per mass, choose consistent positive directions along the string, and write both Newton equations before eliminating tension.

Worked reasoning

For hanging masses 2 kg and 3 kg with g=10, acceleration is (3−2)10/5=2 m/s²; the 3 kg mass moves down and the string tension is 24 N.

Your turn

For 1 kg and 4 kg masses with g=10, find acceleration and tension.

Reveal answer and reasoning

a=(4−1)10/5=6 m/s². For the 1 kg side, T−10=1·6, so T=16 N.

If it went wrong. The tension is not the heavier weight while the system accelerates.

Free-body diagrams and Newton laws

When you see

The target is an acceleration, contact force, or equilibrium condition.

Choose this model

One isolated body with only real external forces; particle under net force.

Principle

A net force changes velocity. Zero net force means zero acceleration, not necessarily zero velocity. Third-law partners act on different bodies.

First move

Choose one body, draw gravity and each contact or applied force, then resolve the sum on chosen axes.

Worked reasoning

A 4 kg cart has 18 N right and 6 N left. Its net force is 12 N right and its acceleration is 3 m/s² right.

Try it

A 5 kg object has 25 N east and 10 N west. Find its acceleration.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Net force is 15 N east, so acceleration is 3 m/s² east.

Repair the error. Do not place an equal-and-opposite third-law partner on the same free-body diagram.

Practice

Question types

Each style examines something different. These selected patterns cover the lesson objectives, It is not all that can be asked about the topic.

The first type is acceleration of force

You divide the force by the mass in the right direction.

A net force 12 N acts on a body of mass 3.0 kg. What is its acceleration?

‎a = ΣF/m = 12/3.0 = 4.0 m/s²‎.

The second type is weight versus mass

Differentiate between kilograms and newtons.

What is the weight of an object of mass 8.0 kg on the surface of the Earth? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

‎W = mg = 8.0 × 9.8 = 78.4 N‎.

The third type is surface force with vertical acceleration

Draw the chart and do not generalize N = mg.

A person with mass 70 kg in an elevator is accelerating upward by 3.0 m/s². What is the force of the ground on it? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

N − mg = ma, N = m(g + a) = 70 × 12.8 = 896 N.

The fourth type is the result of two opposing forces

It is subtracted before it is divided, and does not stop at the result.

Two horizontal forces on a body of mass 2.0 kg: 15 N to the right and 9.0 N to the left. How much is it accelerating?

The resultant 15 − 9.0 = 6.0 N to the right, then a = 6.0/2.0 = 3.0 m/s².

Type 5: Reaction

You realize that the two forces are on two bodies, so they are not canceled or changed by mass.

Your hand pushes a box of mass 4.0 kg with a force of 10 N along a smooth surface. How much force does the box exert on your hand?

By Newton's third law, the two forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction: 10 N. It does not depend on mass or acceleration. However, the box accelerates because the force of your hand affects it, and its force affects you.
