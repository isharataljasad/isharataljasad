PHY 101 · TOPIC 03

Newton laws and force diagrams

Define the object and sum external forces on it.

Book →Pearson →Educator →

The idea

Draw all actual external forces on the chosen object. Choose axes and write a signed force balance. The net force equals mass times acceleration; motion alone does not imply a net force.

A first example

A 5 kg object with a net horizontal force of 15 N accelerates at 3 m/s². Weight and normal force may cancel vertically without cancelling horizontal force.

Engineering connection. A diagram keeps drive, support and resistance forces distinct when modelling moving equipment.

What this explanation develops

  • You first identify the body and then draw the external forces on it alone in a free-body diagram.
  • ΣF = ma is applied to each axis, and calculates acceleration, force, or mass.
  • Distinguish a third-law pair, which acts on two different bodies, from balanced forces acting on one body.

Definitions & meaning

Free-body diagram
A drawing that isolates a single body and shows the external forces acting on it alone. The forces with which it affects others are not depicted, nor are the internal forces between its parts. The first step is not drawing, but choosing the body: changing the choice changes the whole plan.
Net force
The vector sum of the external forces: ΣF. It is the only one that enters Newton's second law; Balanced forces are not neglected in the drawing But it cancels some of them out in total.
Weight
The force of gravity of the Earth on the body : W = mg, in newtons. It is fundamentally different from mass: mass is the amount of matter in kilograms and does not change with location. Weight is a force that changes with g.
Normal force
A force exerted by a surface on an object perpendicular to the surface. It is not always equal to weight; It is equal to it in a special case: A horizontal surface with no vertical acceleration or other vertical force.
Newton third law
If A acts on B with a force, B acts on A with an equal and opposite force. The crucial difference: the two forces affect two different bodies, They are never combined in one free body diagram nor do they cancel each other out.

Formulas & conditions

Newton II

ΣF = m a

: ΣFx = max and ΣFy = may. are applied to each axis independently

Weight

W = m g

Force in newtons, not mass in kilograms.

Normal force in an elevator

N = m(g + a) In an elevator accelerating upward

It becomes m(g − a) on downward acceleration, and zero in free fall.

Action and reaction

F_AB = −F_BA

On two different bodies; That's why they don't prevent movement.

Why doesn't normal force always equal weight?

N = mg is preserved as a law, which is not a law but the result of a special case. Let's see where it comes from and when it drops.

  1. Draw a body on a stationary horizontal surface: the vertical forces are N up and W down.

    The chart limits what enters the equation; What is not drawn will not appear in the total.

  2. Apply Newton's second vertically: N − W = m a_y.

    This is the general equation. Everything after it is a branch of it according to the value of a_y.

  3. If the object is at rest or moving at a constant speed, then a_y = 0 results in N = W.

    Here the preserved rule originated. Its condition is a_y = 0, which is a condition that is forgotten and the rule is unjustly generalized.

  4. If the body accelerates vertically, such as an elevator ascending with an acceleration of a, then N = m(g + a) > W.

    Equality was broken because its condition was broken. In free fall, a = g becomes N = 0, and this is apparent weightlessness: Gravity did not disappear, but the surface force did.

See the concept

Free-body diagram: one body, its forces alone

An isolated rectangular box in the center of the drawing. A green arrow emerges from the top, its symbol is N, which is the surface force. Below it is a red downward arrow whose symbol is W and equals the mass multiplied by the gravitational acceleration. On its right side is a horizontal golden arrow, symbol F. Under the drawing is a line reminding us that each arrow has a force on this body alone.

Worked examples

Start with a written sentence: “The object I am studying is this.” Then draw on it the external forces alone: always the weight, and the force of the surface if it is in contact. The force of the thread if it is tied, and any applied force are mentioned. Then choose a positive direction for each axis, and write ΣF = ma for each axis individually. Do not mix the forces of two bodies in one equation; Every body has its own diagram and equation.

Example 1 · Acceleration on a smooth surface

A box of mass 5.0 kg on a smooth horizontal surface is pushed by a horizontal force 20 N. What is its acceleration?

  1. The body is the box. Forces on it: weight down, surface force up, and horizontal push.

    Identifying the body first prevents the introduction of forces that affect the surface or the driving hand.

  2. Vertical: No acceleration, N = W and N = W do not enter the horizontal calculation.

    The two axes are independent; Balanced vertical forces do not affect horizontal acceleration.

  3. Horizontally : ΣFx = 20 N alone because the surface is smooth, a = 20/5.0 = 4.0 m/s².

    Smoothness means no friction, so there is no horizontal force other than pushing.

Result: a = 4.0 m/s²

Example 2 · Surface force in an elevator

A person with mass 60 kg stands in an elevator accelerating upward by 2.0 m/s². What is the force of the ground on it? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. The body is the person. The forces on it are : N up from the floor, and W = mg down.

    The elevator is not the object studied, its forces are not depicted here.

  2. Make UP positive and write : N − mg = ma.

    The upward acceleration is positive by our choice, so the right side is positive, so N will be greater than the weight.

  3. Solve: N = m(g + a) = 60 × (9.8 + 2.0) = 60 × 11.8 = 708 N.

    The weight is 588 N, the force is 708 N, and the difference between 120 N is exactly ma. This is what the rider feels as extra weight when starting the climb.

Result: N = 708 N

Further worked applications

The first type is acceleration of force

You divide the force by the mass in the right direction.

A net force 12 N acts on a body of mass 3.0 kg. What is its acceleration?

‎a = ΣF/m = 12/3.0 = 4.0 m/s²‎.

Result: 4 m/s²

The second type is weight versus mass

Differentiate between kilograms and newtons.

What is the weight of an object of mass 8.0 kg on the surface of the Earth? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

‎W = mg = 8.0 × 9.8 = 78.4 N‎.

Result: 78.4 N

The third type is surface force with vertical acceleration

Draw the chart and do not generalize N = mg.

A person with mass 70 kg in an elevator is accelerating upward by 3.0 m/s². What is the force of the ground on it? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

N − mg = ma, N = m(g + a) = 70 × 12.8 = 896 N.

Result: 896 N

The fourth type is the result of two opposing forces

It is subtracted before it is divided, and does not stop at the result.

Two horizontal forces on a body of mass 2.0 kg: 15 N to the right and 9.0 N to the left. How much is it accelerating?

The resultant 15 − 9.0 = 6.0 N to the right, then a = 6.0/2.0 = 3.0 m/s².

Result: 3 m/s²

Type 5: Reaction

You realize that the two forces are on two bodies, so they are not canceled or changed by mass.

Your hand pushes a box of mass 4.0 kg with a force of 10 N along a smooth surface. How much force does the box exert on your hand?

By Newton's third law, the two forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction: 10 N. It does not depend on mass or acceleration. However, the box accelerates because the force of your hand affects it, and its force affects you.

Result: 10 N

Useful background

Movement with constant acceleration

v = v₀ + at, x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at² and v² = v₀² + 2aΔx. The issue of forces often ends with acceleration, and the second half remains pure motion.

Decompose a vector into two components

The force of F makes an angle of θ with the horizontal: its horizontal component F cos θ and vertical F sin θ. The sum of the squares of the two components is equal to the square of the magnitude, and this is a quick check of the validity of the factoring. Note that cos accompanies the axis from which the angle is measured.
