Work energy and power

Use an energy balance with a defined system.

Before you start

What will you be able to do after this lesson?

  • You calculate the work from the force, the displacement, and the angle between them, and read its sign.
  • Apply conservation of mechanical energy when non-conservative forces do no net work on the chosen system.
  • Power is calculated from work and time, and is distinguished from work itself.
  • Spring energy and frictional work are calculated within the energy budget.

What this lesson does not cover

  • Damped oscillations and relativistic energy are beyond this lesson.
  • We calculate mechanical energy within specific forces and models; Air resistance is negligible unless stated.

We mentioned them so that you do not think that you have completed the whole topic.

Prerequisites

  • Force and free body diagram

    You need it because: Work is calculated for each force separately, so whoever does not take stock of the forces does not know which ones did work.

    Draw the body alone with the external forces on it: weight, surface force, and any acting force. Then calculate the occupancy of each one. Note that the normal surface force does not do work in the horizontal movement because it is perpendicular to the displacement.

    Study Newton's laws

  • Sine and cosine of common angles

    You need it because: The angle between force and displacement enters the calculation via cos θ, and its error changes the entire result.

    cos 0° = 1, cos 60° = 0.5, cos 90° = 0, cos 180° = −1. Both sides noticed that the force in the direction of motion does the greatest possible work. The perpendicular to it does nothing, and the opposite to it does negative work.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Work transfers energy through force and displacement. For a constant force, W=Fd cos θ. Net work changes kinetic energy. Power is the rate of energy transfer, not an amount of energy.

Worked example

A force of 10 N acting along 3 m does 30 J of work. Delivering 120 J over 4 s corresponds to an average power of 30 W.

Engineering connection Power and efficiency language is useful for understanding pumps and motors, while the actual model still needs correct system boundaries.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

The ideal elastic force F=−kx opposes the displacement from equilibrium, and the spring energy U=½kx² is measured from the undeformed length. The half factor appears because the force changes from zero to kx; The triangular area under the force versus elongation curve gives the work needed to tighten the spring.

The presence of friction does not mean that the total energy disappears. If we choose mechanical energy only, the change in K+U is equal to the work of the non-conservative forces within the model. Constant kinetic friction work opposite sliding Wf=−fk d. The potential energy does not add up and work the same conservative force again on the budget.

Work
For a constant force and displacement, W=Fd cos θ, measured in joules. Work is positive when the force has a component along displacement, negative when opposed, and zero when perpendicular. If the object does not move, mechanical work on it is zero, even though holding it can require metabolic effort.
Kinetic energy
Translational kinetic energy is K=½mv², measured in joules. It is nonnegative and equals zero at rest. Doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy for the same mass.
Gravitational potential energy
PE = m g h where h is the height above a reference level of your choice. Its absolute value is arbitrary because the reference is arbitrary, but the difference between two situations does not change as the reference changes. The difference alone is what enters the calculations.
Conservative force
A conservative force does work that depends only on the starting and ending positions. It has an associated potential energy with W=−ΔU. Gravity is an example. Frictional work generally depends on the path and cannot be represented by such a position-only potential.
Power
Average power is work divided by elapsed time, P_avg=W/Δt, in watts (J/s). Instantaneous power is dW/dt. Two machines can do the same work while having different average powers if they take different times.

The relationships you will use

  • W = F d cos θWorkThe angle between force and displacement, not necessarily between force and the horizontal.
  • KE = ½ m v²Kinetic energyThe speed is squared, and the half is unforgettable.
  • PE = m g hPotential energyIt's the difference that matters, not the absolute value.
  • W_net = ΔKEWork and energy theoremTotal work equals the change in kinetic energy, not the energy itself.
  • KE + PE = constant (No non-conservative forces)Conservation of mechanical energyIts condition is clear: no friction, no resistance, and no external force doing work.
  • P = W / tCapacityIts unit is the watt, and the unit of work is the joule. Don't confuse them.
  • Uspring=½kx²; Δ(K+U)=WnonconservativeElastic energy and external workUnits N/m and m give J.

Why does the force perpendicular to the motion do no work?

A seemingly strange result: a moon has been orbiting the Earth for millions of years and no work is being done on it. Let's see why.

  1. Refer to the definition : W = F d cos θ, the angle between force and displacement.Why: Work does not measure force alone or displacement alone, but rather what the force has in common in the direction of displacement.
  2. Put θ = 90°: into cos 90° = 0, the work is zero no matter how big F and d are.Why: The vehicle in the direction of movement is non-existent, as the force does not push the body forward or restrain it.
  3. Apply to uniform circular motion: force to the center, velocity on the tangent, and the angle between them 90°.Why: The work is zero at every moment, and therefore the kinetic energy does not change, and therefore the amount of speed remains constant.
  4. Notice what was not said: speed is constant in magnitude, not constant in direction. The body is accelerating despite the lack of work.Why: This is the source of confusion: absence of work means constant speed. It does not mean a lack of force or a lack of acceleration. Force changes direction without changing magnitude.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

First define the system, then ask: Is a non-conservative force acting? If the answer is no, use energy conservation: write the total energy in the two cases and equate them, You will save yourself the hassle of calculating forces and acceleration. If the answer is yes, calculate the work of that force and include it in the budget. Choose a reference level for elevation and write it down; Any level is fine as long as you stick to it in both cases.

Example 1 · Work an oblique force

The distance bag 4.0 m is pulled by the force of 50 N in a rope making 60° with the horizontal. What work does this force do?

  1. The angle between the force and the displacement is 60°, and the displacement is horizontal.Why: The angle in law is between force and displacement, not between force and any other direction; Here it happened that the displacement was horizontal and coincided with the angle of the rope.
  2. Replace : W = 50 × 4.0 × cos 60° = 50 × 4.0 × 0.5.Why: The cosine captures the horizontal component alone; The vertical component does not do work because the displacement is horizontal.
  3. Output 100 J.Why: It is half what it would be if the rope were horizontal, because only half of the force acts in the direction of movement.

Output: W = 100 J

Example 2 · speed of energy conservation

A body falls from rest from a height 5.0 m. What is its speed before reaching the ground? Neglect air resistance and take the g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. There are no non-conservative forces (we neglected resistance), so mechanical energy is conserved.Why: Check the condition first; If resistance were mentioned, the method would be invalid and its work would have to be calculated.
  2. Make the ground a reference: at the top PE = mgh and KE = 0, at the bottom PE = 0 and KE = ½mv².Why: Choosing the reference at the ground eliminates a complete term and simplifies the equation, and it is a free choice.
  3. Equal : mgh = ½mv², and remove m from both sides, so v = √(2gh).Why: Eliminating mass is not an accidental simplification but a consequence: the speed of free fall does not depend on mass.
  4. Replace : v = √(2 × 9.8 × 5.0) = √98 = 9.9 m/s.Why: Don't forget the root; The output of 98 is v² and its module m²/s² is not m/s.

Output: v ≈ 9.9 m/s

A spring pushes a mass

k=200 N/m, x=.10 m and.5 kg mass on smooth surface.

  1. U=½×200×.10²=1 JWhy: Energy reference when the spring is relaxed.
  2. ½mv²=1Why: Elastic energy is transformed into motion.
  3. v=√(2/.5)=2 m/sWhy: We take the positive magnitude of the velocity.

Output: 2 m/s

Questions often left unexplained

If the choice of reference level were free, wouldn't potential energy be meaningless?

The absolute value is really meaningless, the difference is what is meaningful. Physics never asks about PE alone, but about ΔPE, and this difference is the same no matter how you choose the reference. The only condition is that you adhere to the same reference in both cases.

Can kinetic energy be negative?

Not at all, because the mass is positive and the velocity is squared. The **change** in kinetic energy is negative when the body slows down. As for potential energy, it may be negative if you choose a reference higher than the body, and this is acceptable and there is no problem with it.

Why do we use energy at all if the equations of motion are sufficient?

Because energy is a scalar quantity and not a vector, it does not require factoring into components or tracking of trends. It solves problems in which the path is curved or the force is variable, which are problems that equations cannot solve Constant acceleration. An example is sliding down a curved slope: energy gives speed in one line.

Work, energy, and power

Physical idea

Work transfers energy when a force acts through displacement. Only the force component along motion contributes work. Power measures the rate of energy transfer, not the amount of energy itself.

First move

Identify the force and displacement direction. For a constant force use W=Fd cosθ. Compare net work with change in kinetic energy and keep joules and watts distinct.

Worked reasoning

A 20 N force acts 3 m at 60° to motion. Work is 20·3·cos60°=30 J. If that work occurs in 2 s, average power is 15 W.

Check yourself

A 4 kg object speeds up from 2 to 4 m/s. Find net work.

Reveal answer and reasoning

ΔK=0.5·4·(4²−2²)=24 J.

Common error. A force perpendicular to displacement can change direction without doing work.

Conservation of energy and losses

Physical idea

Total energy is conserved. Mechanical energy K+U remains constant only when nonconservative work is zero. Gravitational U near Earth is mgh; spring energy is ½kx² relative to the unstretched state.

First move

Choose system and reference height. Mark starting and ending states, then list stored energies and external or dissipative work. If friction acts, its work is typically negative on mechanical energy.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg object drops 5 m with g=10 and no drag. It loses 100 J of gravitational energy and gains 100 J of kinetic energy. If 20 J is dissipated, final kinetic energy is 80 J.

Check yourself

A 1 kg object starts from rest 3 m above a reference level. With g=10 and no losses, find speed at the reference level.

Reveal answer and reasoning

mgh=½mv² gives v=√(2gh)=√60≈7.75 m/s.

Common error. “Energy conserved” never means kinetic energy alone is conserved.

Adapted Pearson material selected for this topic.

Work by a force

Meaning

Work transfers energy through a force component along displacement. Positive work aids motion, negative work opposes it, and a perpendicular force does zero work.

First move

Mark force and displacement arrows. Use the angle between them, not necessarily the incline angle. For variable force, take signed area under force versus displacement.

Worked reasoning

A 20 N force acts at 60° to a 3 m displacement. Its work is 20·3·cos60°=30 J.

Your turn

A 10 N force acts at 120° to a 5 m displacement. Find work.

Reveal answer and reasoning

W=10·5·cos120°=−25 J; this force does negative work.

If it went wrong. Force magnitude times distance misses the sign and the along-path component.

The work-energy checkpoint

Meaning

Net work changes kinetic energy. This is useful when the requested speeds and positions matter more than travel time.

First move

Define the initial and final states and include work by every force, with its sign. A constant-speed path has zero net work even if individual forces do work.

Worked reasoning

For a 2 kg body speeding from 3 to 5 m/s, the net work is (1/2)·2·(25−9)=16 J.

Your turn

How much net work accelerates a 1 kg body from rest to 6 m/s?

Reveal answer and reasoning

ΔK=(1/2)·1·36=18 J.

If it went wrong. The theorem uses net work, not just the work of one convenient force.

Potential energy and conservation

Meaning

Gravity and ideal springs can store potential energy. Mechanical energy is conserved only when nonconservative external work is zero for the chosen system.

First move

Choose a height zero and a system boundary. Write initial and final kinetic plus potential energies, then place friction or an applied push as nonconservative work.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg mass falls 5 m from rest without drag. With g=10, it loses 100 J gravitational potential energy and gains 100 J kinetic energy, reaching 10 m/s.

Your turn

A 1 kg object starts at rest 2 m above the ground without drag, with g=10. Find speed at ground.

Reveal answer and reasoning

mgh=(1/2)mv² gives v=√(2gh)=√40≈6.32 m/s.

If it went wrong. Energy conservation without a loss term fails when friction converts mechanical energy to thermal energy.

Power is a rate, not energy

Meaning

Power tells how quickly work is done or energy is transferred. One watt is one joule per second; the instantaneous dot-product form uses the along-motion force component.

First move

Decide whether average power over an interval or instantaneous power at one speed is requested. Keep the angle between force and velocity when using Fv.

Worked reasoning

Transferring 600 J in 20 s requires average power 30 W. A 10 N along-motion force at 3 m/s also delivers 30 W instantaneously.

Your turn

A machine transfers 900 J in 30 s. Find average power.

Reveal answer and reasoning

P=900/30=30 W.

If it went wrong. Do not label a joule value as watts; divide by time to obtain a rate.

Work by constant and changing forces

When you see

A force acts over a displacement, possibly with a changing magnitude.

Choose this model

System energy transfer by work; integrate only the force component along motion.

Principle

Work is positive, negative, or zero according to the angle between force and displacement. Net work changes kinetic energy.

First move

Choose a system. For constant force use Fd cosφ; for changing force take signed area under F versus displacement.

Worked reasoning

Stretching a spring from 0 to 0.20 m with k=100 N/m stores 0.5kx²=2 J, supplied by 2 J of external quasistatic work.

Try it

A net 12 N force acts along 3 m of motion. How much does kinetic energy change?

Reveal answer and reasoning

ΔK=Wnet=12(3)=36 J. Direction alignment is assumed.

Repair the error. A force perpendicular to instantaneous motion does zero instantaneous work despite changing direction.

System choice and energy conservation

When you see

A height, spring compression, or speed changes without a need to find elapsed time.

Choose this model

System energy accounting; isolated for energy only when transfer across its boundary is negligible.

Principle

Energy is conserved overall. Mechanical energy K+U is constant only when nonconservative transfer and other exchange are absent. Potential energy belongs to an interacting system.

First move

Draw initial and final states, choose the system boundary, list transfers, then solve symbolically before inserting numbers.

Worked reasoning

A body released from rest drops 5 m with negligible drag. With g=10, mgh=0.5mv² gives v=10 m/s, independent of mass.

Try it

A body falls 1.8 m from rest with g=10 and negligible drag. Find speed.

Reveal answer and reasoning

v=sqrt(2gh)=sqrt(36)=6 m/s. If drag matters, the mechanical-energy result overpredicts speed.

Repair the error. Do not call mechanical energy conserved when friction converts part of it to internal energy.

Power and efficiency

When you see

The question asks how fast energy is transferred or how much input is useful.

Choose this model

Rate of work or energy flow across the chosen system boundary.

Principle

Power is a rate, not an amount of energy. At constant speed the driving power balances dissipative energy transfer at that instant.

First move

Compute output and input over the same interval. Separate joules from watts and state whether force and velocity are aligned.

Worked reasoning

A motor exerts 100 N along a vehicle moving at 3 m/s, so the instantaneous mechanical power is 300 W.

Try it

A device receives 500 J and delivers 350 J of useful work. Find its efficiency.

Reveal answer and reasoning

η=350/500=0.70, or 70%. The remaining 150 J is transferred in other forms.

Repair the error. A force magnitude times speed is not the power if the force is oblique; use the dot product.

Practice

Question types

Each style examines something different. These selected patterns cover the lesson objectives, It is not all that can be asked about the topic.

The first type: Work an oblique force

You include the cosine and do not neglect it.

A force 50 N pulls a body at a distance 4.0 m, and between it and the displacement is an angle 60°. What is the work done in a joule?

‎W = F d cos θ = 50 × 4.0 × cos 60° = 50 × 4.0 × 0.5 = 100 J‎.

The second type: kinetic energy

Square the speed and multiply by half.

What is the kinetic energy of an object with mass 2.0 kg moving with speed 6.0 m/s?

‎KE = ½ m v² = ½ × 2.0 × 36 = 36 J‎.

The third type: Speed of energy conservation

Take root at the limit and don't stop at v².

A body falls from rest from a height 20 m. What is its speed before reaching the ground? Ignore resistance and take g = 9.8 m/s².

mgh = ½mv², v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.8 × 20) = √392 = 19.8 m/s.

Fourth type · Potential energy

You hit all three and don't drop g.

What is the gravitational potential energy of an object with mass 3.0 kg at a height of 2.0 m above the reference? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

‎PE = mgh = 3.0 × 9.8 × 2.0 = 58.8 J‎.

Fifth type · Ability

Divide work by time and distinguish watts from joules.

600 J work is done through 4.0 s. What is the power done?

‎P = W/t = 600/4.0 = 150 W‎.
