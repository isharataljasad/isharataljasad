THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Momentum is mv and impulse is the change in momentum. Momentum conservation applies to a system with negligible external impulse over the interval. Kinetic energy need not be conserved in a collision.

Worked example

A 2 kg body changes velocity from +3 to −1 m/s. Its momentum change is 2(−1−3)=−8 kg·m/s.

Engineering connection A force-time graph gives impulse by its signed area; the peak force alone does not describe the full effect.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

Momentum
p = m v, a vector quantity with unit kg·m/s. Its direction is the direction of speed, and its sign in linear motion follows your chosen positive direction. Note that it is linear in speed, unlike the kinetic energy that is squared in it.
Impulse
Impulse is the time integral of net force and equals the change in momentum, J=Δp. For constant force, J=FΔt; the same formula uses average force when force varies. Units are N·s or kg·m/s. For the same momentum change, a longer stopping time reduces average force.
Isolated system
A system with zero net external force has constant total momentum. During a brief collision, momentum conservation can be a useful approximation when external impulse is negligible compared with the internal momentum exchanges.
Perfectly inelastic collision
The bodies stick together and share a common final velocity. With negligible external impulse, total momentum is conserved. Kinetic energy is generally reduced as energy becomes internal energy, deformation or sound. For fixed initial momenta, sticking gives the greatest kinetic-energy loss among ordinary collisions without an added energy source.
Elastic collision
Both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. It is an ideal model that collisions of billiard balls and gaseous molecules approach. It is not fully achieved in large objects.

The relationships you will use

  • p = m vMomentumvector; Carry the sign with you to every line.
  • J = F Δt = ΔpThe theorem of impulse and momentumGreat power in a short time is equivalent to small power in a long time.
  • Σ p_before = Σ p_afterPreserving the momentumFor isolated system; It is valid in all types of collisions.
  • m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂) v_fAdhesive collisionThe right side has one mass and one speed.

Why is momentum conserved, and why is kinetic energy not conserved with it?

The two memorizations seem related, but they are not. The reason is clear from the source of each.

  1. During a collision, each body exerts a force on the other, and they are equal and opposite according to Newton's third law.Why: This is the starting point: conservation is not an independent principle but a consequence of the third law.
  2. They affect during the same period, so the two impulses F Δt are equal and opposite.Why: The contact time for the two bodies is necessarily the same, so equal forces necessitate equal thrusts.
  3. The push is a change in the amount of movement, so what one gains is lost by the other, and the total is fixed.Why: Preservation arises from the opposition, not from the nature of the collision. This is why it is true for both elastic and inflexible ones.
  4. As for kinetic energy, nothing similar protects it: Internal forces may deform the two objects, heat them and produce sound.Why: Total energy is always conserved, but **kinetic** is transformed into other forms. There is no law that prevents this transformation, while there is something that prevents the imbalance of the total momentum. Hence: momentum is always conserved, and kinetic energy is in elastic alone.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

Write the positive direction first. Then make a table with two columns: before and after, Put m v in each box with its mark. Then equate the sum of the two columns. Do not calculate kinetic energy, thinking that it is conserved. Check the collision type first. If the two bodies collide, the other side has one mass and one unknown speed.

Example 1 · coalescing with different sign

An object of mass 3.0 kg moving to the right with speed 4.0 m/s collides with an object of mass 2.0 kg He moves left at a speed of 5.0 m/s, and they collide. What is their speed after the collision?

  1. Make right positive : v₁ = +4.0 and v₂ = −5.0.Why: Writing the two signs explicitly before the calculation is the difference between the correct and incorrect answer here.
  2. Quantity of movement before : 3.0(+4.0) + 2.0(−5.0) = 12 − 10 = +2.0 kg·m/s.Why: Addition is vector addition, so negative is subtracted. If the amounts were added together, 22 would come out, which is an error of eleven times.
  3. After the collision one mass 5.0 kg has a speed of v: 5.0 v = +2.0.Why: Coalition means one speed, the unknown is one, and one equation is sufficient.
  4. So v = +0.40 m/s, that is, right.Why: The positive sign is part of the answer, not an adornment: it says that the confluent mass is moving to the right. Because the momentum of the first body was greater.

Output: v = +0.40 m/s (to the right)

Example 2 · Lost energy in docking

In the previous example, how much kinetic energy was lost?

  1. Before : ½(3.0)(4.0)² + ½(2.0)(5.0)² = 24 + 25 = 49 J.Why: Energy is scalar, not vector, so the velocity is squared and the sign disappears; That is why the two terms are added and not subtracted.
  2. After : ½(5.0)(0.40)² = 0.40 J.Why: The speed after docking is small, and squaring makes it even smaller.
  3. Missing 49 − 0.40 = 48.6 J.Why: It turns into heat, distortion, and sound. The quantum of motion remains conserved at the same time, and this is not a contradiction: the two quantities are governed by two different laws.

Output: ΔKE ≈ 48.6 J lost

Questions often left unexplained

How is momentum conserved and energy lost in the collision itself?

Because the two quantities measure two different things. Quantum of motion is a vector protected by the opposition of forces in the third law. Kinetic energy is standard, and nothing prevents it from being transformed into heat and deformation. Total energy is always conserved, and what is lost is its kinetic form, not the energy itself.

Why does an airbag reduce injury if the change in momentum is the same?

Because F Δt = Δp and its right end are already fixed: the rider will stop in both cases. Prolonging Δt inevitably makes F smaller. The pillow does not reduce the change in the amount of movement, but rather spreads it over a longer time, and force is what hurts.

Is momentum conserved if there is friction?

Not in the long run, because friction is an external force. But during the collision itself, the collision forces are orders of magnitude greater than the friction. Its time is very short, so the effect of friction is neglected and the system is treated as isolated at that moment. This is a justified approximation, not ignoring the force.

Impulse and momentum

Physical idea

Momentum is a vector. Impulse is the change in momentum and equals the signed area under a force-time graph. Extending stopping time reduces average force for a fixed momentum change.

First move

Choose a direction and calculate final momentum minus initial momentum. For variable force, use graph area or an integral. Average force is Δp/Δt over the same interval.

Worked reasoning

A 0.15 kg ball approaches at +20 m/s and rebounds at −10 m/s. Its momentum change is 0.15(−10−20)=−4.5 N·s. If contact lasts 0.03 s, average force on the ball is −150 N.

Check yourself

A 2 kg cart slows from +5 to +1 m/s in 0.5 s. Find impulse and average force.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Δp=2(1−5)=−8 N·s; Favg=−8/0.5=−16 N.

Common error. For a rebound, the velocity sign changes. Subtracting speed magnitudes loses that direction change.

Collisions and system boundaries

Physical idea

Momentum is conserved for an isolated system during a collision. Kinetic energy is conserved only for an elastic collision. Objects that stick together share one final velocity.

First move

Define both bodies as the system and mark velocity directions. Write momentum conservation first. Add kinetic-energy conservation only when the collision is stated or shown to be elastic.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg cart at +3 m/s sticks to a 1 kg cart initially at rest. Final speed is (2·3)/(2+1)=2 m/s. Kinetic energy drops from 9 J to 6 J, so the collision is inelastic.

Check yourself

A 1 kg cart at +4 m/s sticks to a 3 kg cart at rest. Find final velocity.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Total momentum is +4 kg·m/s and total mass is 4 kg, so vf=+1 m/s.

Common error. Mechanical energy need not remain mechanical in an inelastic collision, although total energy remains conserved.

Momentum and system direction

Meaning

Momentum is a vector. The total momentum of a system is a signed or component-wise sum, not the sum of all speed magnitudes.

First move

Choose an axis before multiplying mass and velocity. Add momenta with their signs; in two dimensions, resolve x and y separately.

Worked reasoning

A 2 kg cart moving east at 3 m/s has momentum +6 kg·m/s with east positive.

Your turn

A 0.5 kg ball moves west at 4 m/s. Find signed momentum with east positive.

Reveal answer and reasoning

p=0.5·(−4)=−2 kg·m/s, meaning 2 kg·m/s west.

If it went wrong. Kinetic energy cannot substitute for momentum: it is scalar and depends on speed squared.

Impulse and force-time area

Meaning

Impulse is momentum change and equals signed area under a force-time graph. For the same momentum change, longer contact time means smaller average force magnitude.

First move

Find final minus initial momentum with velocity signs. Divide by contact duration only after determining the signed impulse.

Worked reasoning

A 50 N constant force acts for 0.20 s, delivering 10 N·s. A 2 kg object initially at rest gains 5 m/s in the force direction.

Your turn

A 0.20 kg ball changes from +15 to −5 m/s in 0.04 s. Find average force.

Δp=0.20(−5−15)=−4 N·s; Favg=−4/0.04=−100 N.

Conservation of momentum

Meaning

The momentum of a chosen system stays constant when net external impulse during the event is negligible. Individual objects can change momentum while the system total remains fixed.

First move

Choose all interacting objects as the system, define a positive axis, and write signed initial and final momentum sums. Check whether an external impact invalidates isolation.

Worked reasoning

A 1 kg cart starts at +4 m/s and a 3 kg cart is still. If the first ends at −2 m/s after interaction, the second must move at +2 m/s because 4=−2+3·2.

Your turn

A 2 kg cart at +5 m/s hits a 3 kg cart at rest. If the first leaves at −1 m/s, find the second final velocity.

Reveal answer and reasoning

10=−2+3v, so v=4 m/s in the positive direction.

If it went wrong. Momentum conservation does not imply kinetic-energy conservation.

Collisions and energy bookkeeping

Meaning

In a completely inelastic collision, bodies stick and share a final velocity. Momentum can be conserved while kinetic energy decreases into deformation, sound, and heat.

First move

Write signed momentum first to obtain shared velocity. Then compare initial and final kinetic energies separately; never assume the latter is conserved.

Worked reasoning

A 1 kg cart at 4 m/s sticks to an identical cart at rest. Final speed is 2 m/s. Initial kinetic energy is 8 J; final kinetic energy is 4 J.

Your turn

A 2 kg cart at 3 m/s sticks to a 1 kg cart at rest. Find final speed and kinetic-energy loss.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Momentum gives v=2 m/s. Initial K=9 J and final K=6 J, so 3 J leaves the kinetic store.

If it went wrong. Using an elastic-collision kinetic-energy equation for bodies that stick contradicts the event description.

Momentum and impulse

When you see

A force acts briefly or an object rebounds.

Choose this model

Particle or system with momentum change caused by external impulse.

Principle

Impulse is the area under force versus time. It changes vector momentum. A short interaction can produce large average force without a large time interval.

First move

Use signed initial and final velocities. Compute Δp first, then divide by duration for average force if needed.

Worked reasoning

A 0.5 kg ball changes from +6 to -2 m/s. Its impulse is 0.5(-2-6)=-4 N·s.

Try it

If that reversal lasts 0.020 s, what is average force on the ball?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Favg=J/Δt=-4/0.020=-200 N. The negative sign gives direction.

Repair the error. A bounce reverses momentum direction; subtract signed values instead of subtracting speeds.

Collisions and center of mass

When you see

Bodies interact strongly with each other over a short time.

Choose this model

Multi-body system with negligible external impulse; kinetic energy requires its own test.

Principle

Internal collision forces cancel in system momentum accounting. Elastic collisions conserve kinetic energy too; bodies that stick generally do not.

First move

Draw before and after velocity arrows. Conserve x and y momentum separately, then test energy only if the collision type warrants it.

Worked reasoning

A 1 kg cart at +4 m/s sticks to a stationary 3 kg cart. Shared velocity is +1 m/s. Kinetic energy falls from 8 J to 2 J.

Try it

A 1 kg mass is at x=0 and a 3 kg mass at x=4 m. Find the center of mass.

Reveal answer and reasoning

xcm=(1·0+3·4)/4=3 m, closer to the heavier mass.

Repair the error. Momentum conservation is a system claim; it fails if external impulse during the event is substantial.

Practice

Question types

Each style examines something different. These selected patterns cover the lesson objectives, It is not all that can be asked about the topic.

The first type: Calculating momentum

Multiply the mass by the velocity in the correct unit.

What is the momentum of a car with mass 1500 kg traveling at speed 20 m/s?

‎p = m v = 1500 × 20 = 30000 kg·m/s‎.

The second type: docking with a stationary body

It is divided by the total mass, not by one of them.

An object of mass 2.0 kg moving with speed 6.0 m/s collides with a stationary object of mass 4.0 kg and they merge. What is their speed after the collision?

2.0(6.0) + 4.0(0) = (2.0 + 4.0) v, 12 = 6.0 v and v = 2.0 m/s.

The third type is a collision of two sign

Subtract, not add, when the two velocities are opposite.

An object 3.0 kg moving to the right with a speed of 4.0 m/s collides with an object 2.0 kg moving to the left with a speed of 5.0 m/s, They join. What is their speed after the collision? Make the right positive and write the sign.

‎3.0(+4.0) + 2.0(−5.0) = 12 − 10 = +2.0‎. Then 5.0 v = +2.0 then v = +0.40 m/s right.

Fourth style · impulse

The force is multiplied by the time and the unit of the resultant is known.

Force 200 N acts for duration 0.050 s. How much is the thrust?

J = F Δt = 200 × 0.050 = 10 N·s, which is equivalent to 10 kg·m/s in terms of momentum change.

Type 5 · Energy lost in fusion

It calculates energy before and after and does not assume conservation.

An object 2.0 kg with a speed of 6.0 m/s joins a stationary object 4.0 kg and they move together with a speed of 2.0 m/s. How many joules of kinetic energy were lost?

Before : ½(2.0)(6.0)² = 36 J. and after : ½(6.0)(2.0)² = 12 J. The missing 36 − 12 = 24 J turned into heat, distortion, and sound.
