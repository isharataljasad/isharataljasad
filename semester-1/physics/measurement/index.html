Prerequisites

  • Decimal forces and scientific formula

    You need it because: The prefixes are all forces of ten, so anyone who does not read 10⁻⁶ with confidence cannot convert between them.

    A positive exponent means multiplication: 10³ = 1000. and a negative exponent means division: 10⁻³ = 0.001. When the forces are multiplied, the exponents add : 10³ × 10⁻⁶ = 10⁻³. The scientific form writes the number as a number between 1 and 10 multiplied by a power of ten, such as 7 × 10⁻⁵.

  • Multiply and reduce fractions

    You need it because: The conversion operator is a fraction, and the conversion series is the multiplication of fractions, and deletion in it is the same as contraction of fractions.

    When multiplying fractions, the numerator is multiplied by the numerator and the denominator is multiplied by the denominator. Any factor appears in the numerator And the denominator is removed : (3 × 5)/(5 × 7) = 3/7. The units are treated exactly like these factors, km in the numerator and km in the denominator are eliminated.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

A physical equation must have compatible dimensions on both sides. Convert by multiplying ratios equal to one. A measurement has finite precision; retain guard digits during calculation and round the final result appropriately.

Worked example

A speed of 72 km/h equals 72×1000/3600=20 m/s. Distance divided by time has dimensions of speed.

Engineering connection A unit mismatch in a process calculation can overwhelm an otherwise correct model.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

In addition and subtraction, we keep the result to the lowest exact decimal place between the inputs, : 12.11+0.3=12.41, and it is written 12.4 because 0.3 does not specify the hundredth part. In multiplication and division, we use the least significant number of significant figures, not the number of decimal places. Defined conversion coefficients and integers are exact numbers that do not limit the accuracy of the result.

Memorize additional numbers during the steps and round off at the limit; Repeated approximation accumulates an artifact. Leading zeros are not significant figures, and zeros between non-zero numbers are. Writing 1.20×10³ shows three significant figures while 1200 alone may be ambiguous without measurement context.

SI base quantities
SI has seven base quantities. This course often uses length in metres (m), mass in kilograms (kg), and time in seconds (s). Derived units combine base units; for example, a newton is kg·m/s².
Conversion factor
A fraction whose numerator and denominator represent the same expression in two different units, so its value is one. Same as (1000 m)/(1 km), it is equal to 1 because 1000 m and 1 km are equal lengths. Because it is equal to one, multiplying by it does not change the amount, but rather changes the unit with which we express it.
Significant figure
Significant figures indicate the precision represented by a measured value. Nonzero digits, zeros between significant digits, and trailing zeros after a decimal point following a nonzero digit are significant. Leading zeros are not. Thus 0.00340 has three significant figures: 3, 4 and the final 0.
Dimension
A dimension describes the kind of physical quantity independently of the chosen unit: length L, mass M and time T. Velocity has dimension L/T whether expressed in m/s or km/h. Quantities added together must have the same dimensions.

The relationships you will use

  • 1 km = 10³ m , 1 cm = 10⁻² m , 1 μm = 10⁻⁶ mCommon prefixesThe prefix is a power of ten that is multiplied by the base unit, not a new unit.
  • [v] = L/T , [a] = L/T² , [F] = M·L/T²Dimensions of kinetic quantitiesIt is derived from the definition: speed is displacement divided by time, and acceleration is speed divided by time.
  • The number of significant figures in the product of multiplication or division = is the least in the dataMultiplication and division ruleThe result is no more accurate than the weakest measurement entered into it.

Why is it permissible to multiply by the conversion factor without changing the quantity?

Multiplication usually changes the amount, so how can we multiply and nothing changes? The answer is in the value of the factor itself.

  1. Depart from the known equality 1 h = 3600 s.Why: This is not a conversion rule but a fact about time: the two durations are the same, and the two expressions are different.
  2. Divide both sides by 1 h and you get 1 = (3600 s)/(1 h).Why: Dividing an amount by itself gives one, so the resulting fraction has a value of one, nothing more and nothing less.
  3. Multiply your quantity by this fraction, or by its reciprocal (1 h)/(3600 s).Why: Multiplying by one does not change the amount. The reciprocal also equals one, so you can choose which one.
  4. Choose an orientation that places the unit to be deleted opposite its current position.Why: Here the common error occurs: both factors are correct, and the direction alone is what eliminates or doubles the unit.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

Start by writing down what you have and what you want in their units. Then arrange the conversion factors so that each unit falls into place Undesirable, sometimes in the numerator and sometimes in the denominator. Do not calculate any number before you are sure that the units are The remainder is exactly what is required; The units detect an error in the arrangement before it costs you the calculation.

Example 1 · conversion speed

Convert 90 km/h to m/s.

  1. Write down the requirement: We want m in the numerator and s in the denominator, and we have km in the numerator and h in the denominator.Why: Setting the goal in units makes selecting factors a decision rather than a guess.
  2. To remove km from the numerator, multiply by (1000 m)/(1 km).Why: km is now in the numerator, so we put it in the denominator to cancel out, and m comes in its place.
  3. To remove h from the denominator, multiply by (1 h)/(3600 s).Why: h is in the denominator, so we put it in the numerator to cancel out, and s is inserted in the denominator.
  4. Calculate : 90 × 1000 ÷ 3600 = 25, and the remaining unit is m/s.Why: The calculation is the last step because the units have proven the correctness of the arrangement before it.

Output: 25 m/s

Example 2 · Significant numbers in division

Sample mass 12.5 g and volume 4.1 cm³. Calculate the density.

  1. Swear: 12.5 ÷ 4.1 = 3.0487..Why: We calculate first with all the numbers, then we round at the limit, not at every step.
  2. Count the significant figures: 12.5 has three, and 4.1 has two.Why: The rule looks at the weakest data, so the outcome is governed by the least.
  3. Round to two significant figures: 3.0 g/cm³.Why: Writing 3.0487 claims accuracy that the measurement did not give; The extra number is fabricated information.

Output: 3.0 g/cm³

Addition is not the same as multiplication

Combine 12.11 cm and 0.3 cm with precision measurement.

  1. Arithmetic sum12.41 cmWhy: We perform the operation without intermediate approximation.
  2. The lowest precision given is tenthsWhy: 0.3 Scaled to one place after the comma.
  3. We write 12.4 cmWhy: We round to the same number, not to a single significant figure.

Output: 12.4 cm

Questions often left unexplained

Why do we write 3.0 and not 3 when their values are equal?

Because they are not equal to the declared accuracy,. 3 has one significant digit, which means that the value is approximately between 2.5 and 3.5. 3.0 has two numbers, meaning it is between 2.95 and 3.05. The zero here is information, not embellishment.

How do I know the order of the conversion factors is wrong before I calculate?

Look at the remaining units. If km·h remains or a square unit appears that you did not request, the order is reversed. This is the benefit of writing units at each step: it detects the error before the calculation.

Does dimensional analysis prove the validity of the equation?

No. It reveals error and does not prove what is correct. An equation whose dimensions are consistent may be wrong in a numerical factor. Like x = v·t and x = 2v·t, their dimensions are the same and one of them is wrong. The difference in dimensions alone is sufficient to definitively rule that there is an error.

Measurements, units, and model limits

Physical idea

A number is a physical answer only when its units and meaning are clear. Dimensional analysis checks whether an equation can be sensible; it cannot prove that the physics model is correct.

First move

Before calculating, identify the system, draw a simple representation, convert to SI units, and state the approximation. Compare dimensions on both sides of an equation and round the final result to a precision justified by the data.

Worked reasoning

A car moves at 72 km/h. Dividing by 3.6 gives 20 m/s. In 5 s at constant speed it travels 100 m. The calculation would fail if the car were accelerating and 72 km/h were only its starting speed.

Check yourself

A runner covers 150 m in 25 s. Find average speed and check its units.

Reveal answer and reasoning

150/25 = 6.0 m/s. Distance divided by time has dimension L/T.

Common error. A mathematically consistent result can still use the wrong model; write the condition that makes the equation valid.

Units and conversion factors

Meaning

A measured answer needs a number and a unit. A conversion factor is a ratio equal to one, so multiplying by it changes units without changing the physical quantity.

First move

Write the starting unit, multiply by factors that cancel it, and check whether the new numerical size is plausible. Convert to one coherent unit system before substituting into an equation.

Worked reasoning

At 72 km/h, a car travels 72/3.6=20 m/s. In 6 s at constant speed it covers 120 m; the constant-speed condition matters.

Your turn

Convert 54 km/h to m/s. How far does it travel in 4 s at that constant speed?

Reveal answer and reasoning

54/3.6=15 m/s; distance=15·4=60 m.

If it went wrong. If the car accelerates, multiplying one reported speed by the full time need not give the distance.

Significant figures and dimensional checks

Meaning

Units can reject a wrong equation: terms being added must share dimensions. Report precision supported by measurements, but retain extra digits during intermediate calculations.

First move

Check dimensions before arithmetic. For multiplication or division, use the fewest significant figures in the inputs; for addition or subtraction, use the least precise decimal place.

Worked reasoning

A length 2.0 m divided by 3.00 s is 0.67 m/s to two significant figures. Adding 12.3 m and 0.456 m gives 12.8 m to one decimal place.

Your turn

A formula claims distance equals speed plus acceleration times time. Is it dimensionally valid?

Reveal answer and reasoning

No. Both terms on the right have units of speed, not distance.

If it went wrong. Dimensional agreement is necessary but does not prove that the physical model is valid.

Measurement and dimensional reasoning

When you see

A quantity is reported in unfamiliar units or a proposed equation looks suspicious.

Choose this model

Quantity with dimensions; idealized particle or system only after the units are clear.

Principle

An equation can add only quantities of the same dimension. A unit check can reject an equation but cannot establish that the physical model is true. Keep extra digits until the final reported answer.

First move

Cancel units explicitly, estimate the expected scale, and report precision supported by the given data.

Worked reasoning

72 km/h is 72/3.6 = 20 m/s. At constant speed for 5 s, the distance is 100 m.

Try it

Convert 54 km/h to m/s, then find its constant-speed travel in 4 s.

Reveal answer and reasoning

15 m/s and 60 m. The conversion lowers the numerical speed because one metre per second is a larger unit than one kilometre per hour.

Repair the error. A dimensionally valid equation can still describe the wrong situation; state the physical condition before substitution.

Question types

Each style examines something different. These selected patterns cover the lesson objectives, It is not all that can be asked about the topic.

The first type is a compound transformation

Arranging two factors in two different directions in one problem.

Convert 108 km/h to m/s.

108 × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s)., km and h are deleted, leaving m/s. And the account is 108 × 1000 ÷ 3600 = 30.

The second type: Significant numbers

Decide how many numbers you are allowed to write in the output.

Mass of 24.8 g and volume of 6.0 cm³. Calculate the density in whole significant figures, Write the result as a number.

24.8 ÷ 6.0 = 4.1333.. in 24.8 there are three significant figures and in 6.0 there are two significant figures, The result has two significant figures : 4.1 g/cm³.

The third type: Dimensional analysis

Decomposition of a complex unit into its basic units.

Pressure is force divided by area. If we express the unit pressure in terms of kg, m and s only, What is the exponent of the meter in this expression? Write the number with a sign.

The force is kg·m/s², the area is m²., and the pressure is kg·m/(s²·m²) = kg/(m·s²). The meter becomes in the denominator with one power, its exponent is −1.

Fourth style · Prefixes

The prefix is read with its integer modulus without confusion between milli and micro.

What is the thickness of 250 μm. plate in metres?

μ means 10⁻⁶, then 250 μm = 250 × 10⁻⁶ m = 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ m = 0.00025 m.

Fifth pattern: Consistency of units before substitution

Unite units before using any equation, not after.

An object moves at a constant speed 15 m/s for a period of 4.0 minutes. What is the distance traveled in metres?

The equation x = v·t requires the time in seconds because the speed is in m/s. 4.0 min = 240 s, the distance is 15 × 240 = 3600 m.
