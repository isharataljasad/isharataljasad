Friction and inclined surfaces

Choose the friction model and resolve forces.

Before you start

What will you be able to do after this lesson?

  • Differentiate between static and kinetic friction and choose the appropriate model for each situation.
  • The force of friction is calculated from the coefficient and the normal force on a horizontal surface.
  • Decompose the weight into two components on an inclined plane and calculate the correct normal force.

What this lesson does not cover

  • Rolling friction and fluid resistance are not covered in this lesson.
  • The dependence of the coefficient of friction on speed, temperature and surface condition is neglected here.
  • Pulley systems and interconnected bodies on ramps are outside the scope of this lesson.
  • Friction in circular motion is the topic of the circular motion lesson.

We mentioned them so that you do not think that you have completed the whole topic.

Prerequisites

  • Free body diagram

    You need it because: Friction depends on the normal force, and the normal force is known only from the vertical force diagram.

    Isolate the object and draw on it: the weight, the normal force from the surface, the friction, and any applied force. Then write ΣF = ma on two perpendicular axes. Choose the two axes parallel to the surface and perpendicular to it in slope problems, not horizontal and vertical.

    Study Newton's laws and free body diagram

  • The sine and cosine of an angle

    You need it because: The factoring of weight on the slope is based on them, and changing them is the most common mistake in this lesson.

    For the composite slope angle θ: parallel to the surface mg sin θ, and perpendicular to it mg cos θ. If your choice gives you a result that contradicts this at zero, then you have switched the two functions.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Static friction adjusts up to a maximum; kinetic friction is modelled as μkN while sliding. The normal force is not automatically mg. On a simple incline with no other normal force component, it is mg cos θ.

Worked example

A sliding object with N=50 N and μk=0.2 has a friction magnitude of 10 N opposite relative sliding.

Engineering connection Friction changes both the force balance and energy bookkeeping in motion models.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

Static friction
Static friction opposes the tendency of surfaces to slide relative to one another. Its magnitude adjusts as needed up to f_s,max=μ_sN. For the simple horizontal equilibrium examples, it balances the applied horizontal force until that limit is reached.
Kinetic friction
Kinetic friction opposes relative sliding. In the simplified dry-friction model its magnitude is f_k=μ_kN. The model neglects dependence on sliding speed and apparent contact area.
Coefficient of friction (μ)
A unitless number that describes the roughness of both surfaces together, not just one surface. Usually μ_s > μ_k, so more thrust is required to start the movement than to keep it going. Its value often ranges between 0.1 and 1.0.
Normal force on an incline
For a body on an incline with no other force component perpendicular to the surface and no perpendicular acceleration, N=mg cos θ. The downslope weight component is mg sin θ. The normal force decreases as the inclination increases; weight itself remains mg.
Critical angle
The angle at which sliding begins, is tan θ_c = μ_s. Notice that mass disappears from this relationship: The sliding initiation angle is the same for a heavy and light body of the same material.

The relationships you will use

  • f_s ≤ μ_s NStillnessInequality: Friction is equal to what is pushed up to the limit.
  • f_k = μ_k NKineticEquality: one value as long as the movement continues.
  • N = m g cos θVerticality on a slopeCosine with perpendicular, and sine with parallel.
  • mg sin θPropelling vehicleParallel to the surface, which is resisted by friction.
  • tan θ_c = μ_sSliding angleCompletely mass independent.

Why N = mg cos θ and why does the mass disappear from the sliding angle

Two rules are preserved, and one of them seems surprising: the slip angle does not change with mass.

  1. Choose two axes: one parallel to the surface and the other perpendicular to it, not horizontal and vertical.Why: With this choice, the acceleration of the body is on one axis only, so the two equations are simplified. Choosing the horizontal and vertical axes is correct, but it unnecessarily introduces two components of the acceleration.
  2. Analyze the weight: its component perpendicular to the surface mg cos θ and parallel to it mg sin θ.Why: The weight alone is tilted away from the two new axes; N and f are located on the two axes originally.
  3. On the vertical axis there is no acceleration, so N = mg cos θ.Why: The body does not sink into the surface or rise from it, so the sum of the forces vertically is zero. This is why N becomes smaller as the surface tilts, and with it the friction decreases.
  4. When you start sliding : mg sin θ = μ_s N = μ_s mg cos θ.Why: This is the moment of limit equilibrium: the impulse equals the maximum resistance to friction.
  5. Divide both sides by mg cos θ and you get tan θ_c = μ_s.Why: mass and g are omitted because they are on both ends. Heaviness increases momentum and increases friction in the same proportion, so neither is more likely. This is why a heavy and a light object slides at the same angle.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

First ask: Is the object at rest or in motion? If it is moving, then the friction is equal to μ_k N. If it is stationary, the friction is equal to the force acting on it, and it does not reach μ_s N except at the edge of sliding. Then on the slope: choose the two axes parallel to the surface and perpendicular to it, Analyze the weight alone, and calculate N from the vertical axis before calculating the friction.

Example 1 · Kinetic friction on a horizontal surface

A box with mass 10 kg slides on a horizontal surface, and the coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.30. What is the force of friction? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. On a horizontal surface with no other vertical forces: N = mg = 10 × 9.8 = 98 N.Why: The normal force is the input to friction, so it is always calculated first. It is equal to the weight here because the surface is horizontal and there is no vertical acceleration.
  2. The body is moving, so the model is : f_k = μ_k N = 0.30 × 98 = 29.4 N.Why: The existing movement decides the choice of model; If it had been silent, the formula would have been different.

Output: f_k = 29.4 N

Example 2 · The normal and compound force driving a slope

A body of mass 5.0 kg is on the slope of angle 30°. Calculate the normal and compound forces parallel to the surface. Take g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. Weight mg = 5.0 × 9.8 = 49 N, which is vertical.Why: The weight never tilts with the surface; The diagonal is the two axes on which we analyze it.
  2. Vertical rooftop vehicle : mg cos 30° = 49 × 0.866 = 42.4 N, then N = 42.4 N.Why: There is no acceleration perpendicular to the surface, the normal force balances this vehicle alone. Note that it weighs less than 49 N, and this is expected whenever the surface tilts.
  3. The component parallel to the surface: mg sin 30° = 49 × 0.5 = 24.5 N.Why: These are what push the body down the slope, and are resisted by friction. Check : 42.4² + 24.5² ≈ 49², the factoring is correct.

Output: N = 42.4 N, parallel component = 24.5 N

Questions often left unexplained

Why doesn't friction depend on the area of contact?

Because increasing the area distributes the same weight over a larger area, the pressure decreases by the same amount. Friction arises from real contact between microscopic protrusions, and its sum depends on the normal force Not on virtual space. This is in the simple model; Wide racing tires are against it for reasons outside this model.

How can static friction be variable and have a constant coefficient?

The parameter specifies the **maximum** not the actual value. Static friction is exactly equal to the force acting to keep the body at rest. It increases with it until it reaches μ_s N, where it becomes weak and begins to slide. It is a response, not an imposed value.

Can friction be in the direction of motion?

Yes, it is what moves the car in the first place. The tire pushes the ground back, and friction pushes the tire forward. The precise rule is that friction opposes **relative sliding** between the two surfaces. Not necessarily body movement.

Friction and inclined planes

Physical idea

Static friction adjusts up to a maximum. Kinetic friction has approximate magnitude μkN and opposes slipping. On a plane inclined at angle θ, gravity has a downhill component mg sinθ and a perpendicular component mg cosθ.

First move

Choose one axis along the plane. Determine the direction of actual or impending relative motion before drawing friction. Compute N from the perpendicular force balance if the object has no perpendicular acceleration.

Worked reasoning

A 5 kg block slides down a 30° incline with μk=0.20 and g=10 m/s². Downhill gravity is 25 N; N≈43.3 N and friction≈8.66 N uphill. Acceleration is (25−8.66)/5≈3.27 m/s² downhill.

Check yourself

A 2 kg block is at rest on a 30° incline with μs=0.8 and g=10. Is rest possible?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Downhill force is 10 N; N≈17.3 N, so maximum static friction≈13.9 N. Rest is possible and actual friction is 10 N uphill.

Common error. Static friction is not automatically μsN; that expression is only its limiting value.

Friction and incline decisions

Meaning

Static friction supplies only the amount needed up to a maximum. Kinetic friction approximately equals μkN and opposes relative sliding. On an incline, weight splits along and across the plane.

First move

Choose an axis along the plane. Predict actual or impending slip, draw friction opposite it, compute N from the perpendicular balance, then solve along the plane.

Worked reasoning

A 5 kg block slides down a 30° incline with μk=0.20 and g=10. Downhill gravity is 25 N, N≈43.3 N, friction≈8.66 N uphill, and acceleration≈3.27 m/s² downhill.

Your turn

A 2 kg block rests on a 30° incline with μs=0.8 and g=10. Is rest possible?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Downhill pull is 10 N; maximum static friction is 0.8·(20 cos30°)≈13.9 N. Rest is possible; actual friction is 10 N uphill.

If it went wrong. Do not automatically set static friction equal to μsN; that is only its limiting value.

Friction and inclined surfaces

When you see

A body touches a rough surface or moves along a slope.

Choose this model

One body with normal force and friction; decide rest versus sliding first.

Principle

Static friction adjusts up to a maximum; kinetic friction has approximate magnitude μkN while surfaces slide. The normal force is set by perpendicular force balance, not by a universal N=mg rule.

First move

Choose axes parallel and perpendicular to the slope. Use the impending-motion test before selecting a friction law.

Worked reasoning

For a 2 kg block sliding down a 30-degree slope with μk=0.20 and g=10, N≈17.3 N, downhill gravity is 10 N, friction is 3.46 N uphill, and a≈3.27 m/s² downhill.

Try it

A 3 kg block rests on a horizontal floor with μs=0.5 and g=10. Can static friction hold against a 12 N horizontal pull?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Yes. N=30 N and the maximum static friction is 15 N, but actual static friction is 12 N, opposite the pull.

Repair the error. Setting static friction equal to μsN in every stationary problem overstates it.

Practice

Question types

Each style examines something different. These selected patterns cover the lesson objectives, It is not all that can be asked about the topic.

The first type is kinetic friction on a horizontal surface

You calculate N first and then multiply by the modulus.

A box of mass 8.0 kg slides on a horizontal surface and the coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.25. What is the force of friction? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

N = mg = 8.0 × 9.8 = 78.4 N, f_k = 0.25 × 78.4 = 19.6 N.

Type 2: Normal force on a slope

Cosine is used with the vertical axis.

An object of mass 5.0 kg is on a slope of angle 30°. What is the normal force? Take g = 9.8 m/s² and cos 30° = 0.866.

‎N = mg cos θ = 5.0 × 9.8 × 0.866 = 42.4 N‎.

The third type is the propulsive vehicle

The sine is used with the parallel axis.

The body itself is 5.0 kg on the slope of 30°. What is the component of the weight parallel to the surface? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

mg sin θ = 5.0 × 9.8 × 0.5 = 24.5 N, which pushes the body down the slope.

Fourth type: Sliding angle

The parameter is related to the tangent of the angle.

The coefficient of static friction between an object and a surface is 1.0. At what angle of slope in degrees does sliding begin?

tan θ_c = μ_s = 1.0, and the angle with a tangent of one is 45°. Notice that the mass does not enter the calculation at all.

Type 5: Less force to initiate movement

The static coefficient is used at the sliding edge.

A box with mass 20 kg is at rest on a horizontal surface, and the coefficient of static friction is 0.50. What is the smallest horizontal force moving it? Take g = 9.8 m/s².

N = 20 × 9.8 = 196 N, and maximum static friction 0.50 × 196 = 98 N. Any force exceeding 98 N starts motion.
