Linear approximation

Use a nearby tangent model and recognise its limits.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Near x=a, approximate f(x) by f(a)+f′(a)(x−a). This local model is useful when a small change is easier to handle than a full recalculation. A large change may produce a poor approximation.

Worked example

Near x=4, √x≈2+(x−4)/4. At 4.04, the linear estimate is 2.01.

Engineering connection A sensitivity estimate describes the effect of a small input change; it is not a complete uncertainty analysis.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

It is best to choose the expansion point to meet two conditions: its proximity to the desired entrance, and the ease of calculating f and f′ there. To calculate √4.04 we choose a=4 not a=0; The root of 4 is easy and its derivative is limited. At zero, the root function does not have a final derivative that we use in this construction.

The symbol ≈ is necessary. Rounding is a way to get an interpretable estimate, not a license to write many numbers as correct. Increasing the distance from a may enlarge the error, and a highly curvilinear function may require a smaller domain. L can be compared to exact values ​​in a table to understand the local range.

In engineering measurement, dx may represent a small deviation or an approximate limit of error. If the slope is negative, the sign of the change is reversed, but the error term in the magnitude is used as the absolute value. The units remain consistent: the volume is derived with respect to the length in unit area, and multiplied by the length error gives the volume error.

Do not confuse absolute error with relative error. The error 0.1 cm is small for an object of length 100 cm but large for an object of length 0.2 cm. Do not use the relative error if the reference value is zero. Then the division is not defined and we need an absolute standard or some other standard.

Linearization
The function L(x)=f(a)+f′(a)(x−a) replaces the curve with a tangent near a. The two functions are not equal everywhere; They are equal in value and slope at a. Choosing a close to x and easy to calculate makes the approximation useful instead of making calculating the tangent more difficult than the original problem.
Differential
The quantity dy=f′(a)dx is the change of the tangent when the input changes by an amount dx., while Δy=f(a+dx)−f(a) is the actual change of the function. The difference between them appears due to the curvature of the curve. In a linear function they are exactly equal, and in a smooth function the difference is locally small.
Relative error
The ratio of the amount of error of a quantity to the amount of its reference value. We multiply by 100 to write a percentage. Estimating the error in the volume of a sphere from the error of the radius gives |dV|/V≈3|dr|/r. This is a local approximation and does not mean that large errors are multiplied by a factor of precisely 3.
Concavity and tangent estimate
When f″ is positive on the interval and the function curve lies above its tangent, the linear approximation is less than the actual value. When it is negative, the tangent lies above the curve. Knowing the sign of concavity gives the direction of the error, but it does not give its magnitude alone.

Why does a factor of three appear in the ball volume error?

We first differentiate and then divide by the reference volume.

  1. dV=4πr²drWhy: Power base.
  2. dV/V=(4πr²dr)/(4πr³/3)Why: We compare the change with the original value.
  3. dV/V=3dr/rWhy: Common factors are eliminated; The relationship concerns differentials.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

Select a, calculate f(a) and f′(a), select dx=x−a, then sum the original value and the approximate change.

Rounding a root

Estimate √4.04.

  1. a=4، f(a)=2Why: We select a near perfect square.
  2. f′(4)=1/(2√4)=1/4Why: root derivative.
  3. dx=0.04، L=2+(1/4)(0.04)=2.01Why: The change in x is not 4.04 but 0.04.

Output: ≈2.01

Ball volume sensitivity

r=10 cm, possible measurement error 0.02 cm. Estimate the percent relative error of the volume.

  1. |dr|/r=0.02/10=0.002Why: We convert the length error into a ratio.
  2. |dV|/V≈3(0.002)=0.006Why: The volume is proportional to the cube of the radius.
  3. 0.006×100=0.6%Why: Convert the fraction to a percentage.

Output: ≈0.6%

Questions often left unexplained

Why not choose a more distant point that is easy to calculate?

Ease of calculation is not enough. Tangent describes the behavior near the point of tangent, and error is affected by distance and curvature.

Linear approximation and differentials

Meaning

Near a known point, the tangent line gives a simple estimate for a smooth curve. The approximation improves as x approaches a.

Method

Choose a nearby a where f(a) and f′(a) are easy. Set Δx=x−a and estimate Δf≈f′(a)Δx. Check whether the estimate’s direction is sensible and remember that the neglected curvature creates error.

Worked reasoning

For √1.08, use f(x)=√x at a=1. Then f(1)=1 and f′(1)=1/2, so √1.08≈1+(1/2)(0.08)=1.04.

Check yourself

Estimate ∛8.12 using a=8.

Reveal answer and reasoning

For f(x)=x^(1/3), f(8)=2 and f′(8)=1/12. With Δx=0.12, the estimate is 2+0.12/12=2.01.

Common error. A tangent-line estimate is local; using a faraway a can make it poor.

Linear estimates and differentials

Idea

Near a known input, the tangent line provides a local estimate. The differential dy=f′(x)dx estimates output change, with error usually growing as the step grows.

First move

Choose a convenient anchor a close to the target, calculate f(a) and f′(a), then multiply the derivative by the small input change.

Worked reasoning

Estimate √(4.1) using f(x)=√x at a=4. Since f(4)=2, f′(4)=1/4, and Δx=0.1, the estimate is 2+0.025=2.025.

Your turn

Estimate (8.12)^(1/3) using a=8.

Reveal answer and reasoning

f(8)=2, f′(8)=1/(3·8^(2/3))=1/12, Δx=0.12, so the estimate is 2.01.

If it went wrong. Use the derivative at the anchor and the difference from that anchor, not the target itself as Δx.

Linear approximation and differentials

Principle

Near a known input, a smooth curve is approximately its tangent line. The differential dy=f′(a)dx estimates output change and gives a fast local error or measurement estimate.

Why and when

Choose a convenient a near the requested input, compute f(a) and f′(a), then multiply by the small displacement Δx. State that the result is an approximation.

Worked solution

Estimate √9.2. With f(x)=√x at a=9, f(9)=3, f′(9)=1/6, and Δx=0.2. The estimate is 3+0.2/6≈3.0333.

Try it

Estimate (8.12)^(1/3) using a=8.

Reveal answer and reasoning

f(8)=2, f′(8)=1/12, Δx=0.12, so the estimate is 2.01.

Common trap. Δx is target minus anchor, not the target input itself.
