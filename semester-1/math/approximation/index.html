Linear approximation

Use a nearby tangent model and recognise its limits.

TryLearnApply
TRY FIRST

Start with a short challenge

Use the linearisation of √x at 4 to estimate √4.08.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Near x=a, approximate f(x) by f(a)+f′(a)(x−a). This local model is useful when a small change is easier to handle than a full recalculation. A large change may produce a poor approximation.

Worked example

Near x=4, √x≈2+(x−4)/4. At 4.04, the linear estimate is 2.01.

Engineering connection A sensitivity estimate describes the effect of a small input change; it is not a complete uncertainty analysis.

Linear approximation and differentials

Meaning

Near a known point, the tangent line gives a simple estimate for a smooth curve. The approximation improves as x approaches a.

Method

Choose a nearby a where f(a) and f′(a) are easy. Set Δx=x−a and estimate Δf≈f′(a)Δx. Check whether the estimate’s direction is sensible and remember that the neglected curvature creates error.

Worked reasoning

For √1.08, use f(x)=√x at a=1. Then f(1)=1 and f′(1)=1/2, so √1.08≈1+(1/2)(0.08)=1.04.

Check yourself

Estimate ∛8.12 using a=8.

Reveal answer and reasoning

For f(x)=x^(1/3), f(8)=2 and f′(8)=1/12. With Δx=0.12, the estimate is 2+0.12/12=2.01.

Common error. A tangent-line estimate is local; using a faraway a can make it poor.

Linear estimates and differentials

Idea

Near a known input, the tangent line provides a local estimate. The differential dy=f′(x)dx estimates output change, with error usually growing as the step grows.

First move

Choose a convenient anchor a close to the target, calculate f(a) and f′(a), then multiply the derivative by the small input change.

Worked reasoning

Estimate √(4.1) using f(x)=√x at a=4. Since f(4)=2, f′(4)=1/4, and Δx=0.1, the estimate is 2+0.025=2.025.

Your turn

Estimate (8.12)^(1/3) using a=8.

Reveal answer and reasoning

f(8)=2, f′(8)=1/(3·8^(2/3))=1/12, Δx=0.12, so the estimate is 2.01.

If it went wrong. Use the derivative at the anchor and the difference from that anchor, not the target itself as Δx.

Linear approximation and differentials

Principle

Near a known input, a smooth curve is approximately its tangent line. The differential dy=f′(a)dx estimates output change and gives a fast local error or measurement estimate.

Why and when

Choose a convenient a near the requested input, compute f(a) and f′(a), then multiply by the small displacement Δx. State that the result is an approximation.

Worked solution

Estimate √9.2. With f(x)=√x at a=9, f(9)=3, f′(9)=1/6, and Δx=0.2. The estimate is 3+0.2/6≈3.0333.

Try it

Estimate (8.12)^(1/3) using a=8.

Reveal answer and reasoning

f(8)=2, f′(8)=1/12, Δx=0.12, so the estimate is 2.01.

Common trap. Δx is target minus anchor, not the target input itself.
