Limits

Find a limit before substituting into a broken expression.

Before you start

What will you be able to do after this lesson?

  • the limit is calculated by direct substitution when it is legitimate, and it is known when it is not.
  • The limit of a fraction in which the substitution gives the form 0/0 is calculated by factoring or multiplying the conjugate.
  • Differentiate between the value of a function at a point and its limit at that point.
  • Rule that the limit does not exist if the two side limits are different.
  • Calculate limits at infinity and trigonometric limits, explain bounds, and interpret the definition of a limit.

What this lesson does not cover

  • Interpret epsilon and delta using a linear example. General proofs in real analysis are beyond this lesson.
  • L'Hopital's rule requires additional conditions and derivation; It is not a substitute for the methods of this lesson.

We mentioned them so that you do not think that you have completed the whole topic.

Prerequisites

  • Difference of two squares factoring and triangular factoring

    You need it because: The first way to remove the indeterminate form 0/0 is factoring. If you are unable to analyze, stop the solution at the beginning.

    The difference between two squares : a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b). example : x² − 9 = (x − 3)(x + 3) because 9 = 3². The trinomial x² + bx + c is analyzed by looking for two numbers whose product is c and whose sum is b. Example: x² − 5x + 6 = (x − 2)(x − 3) because (−2)(−3) = 6 and (−2) + (−3) = −5.

  • cancellation in algebraic fractions, and when is it permissible?

    You need it because: After factored, you will delete a factor from the numerator and denominator, and this deletion is conditional on a condition that is easy to forget.

    It is permissible to reduce the common factor as long as it is not equal to zero. (x − 3)(x + 3) / (x − 3) = x + 3 True for all x except x = 3. The equality here is conditional, not absolute, which is why we write x ≠ 3 next to it. This is exactly what makes the cancellation legitimate inside the limit, as you will see in the derivation below.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

A limit describes nearby behaviour. A missing value at the point does not by itself prevent a limit. Factor or rationalise an indeterminate expression, cancel only for nearby allowed inputs, and then evaluate.

Worked example

For (x² − 9)/(x − 3), factor the numerator. For x ≠ 3 the expression is x + 3, so its limit as x approaches 3 is 6. The original expression is still undefined at 3.

Engineering connection A limiting model describes what a process approaches, even when an ideal endpoint is not directly measured.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

At infinity we compare the speed of growth of the boundaries. In breaking a polynomial, we divide the numerator and denominator by the highest power in the denominator. If the two degrees are equal, the ratio of the two main coefficients remains, and if the numerator is the lowest degree, the limit is zero. The highest degree in the numerator does not always give positive infinity: check the coefficient, sign, and point of approach. We do not replace the infinity symbol as if it were a number.

The base limit sin(x)/x=1 at x→0 assumes radians. Write sin(3x)/x=3 sin(3x)/(3x), the limit is 3., and by multiplying the conjugate, the limit of (1−cos x)/x² is one half. If a function is bounded between two functions with the same limit, it has that limit: From −x²≤x² sin(1/x)≤x² we deduce zero without the limit of sin(1/x) alone.

The divergence towards +∞ is not a true finished term. Near x=0, 1/x has two sides with different signs, while 1/x² is increasing infinitely from both sides. The terminus 1/x→0 at x→∞ gives a horizontal asymptote y=0; Another curve can intersect its asymptote, the line describing a distal behavior rather than a barrier.

Exact meaning: For every tolerance ε>0 around L we can choose a distance δ>0 around a, such that 0<|x−a|<δ ensures |f(x)−L|<ε. in f(x)=2x+1 at a=3, the error |f(x)−7|=2|x−3|; Choosing δ=ε/2 fulfills the condition. We do not need to know f(a) to set the adjacent values.

Limit
The limit of f(x) as x approaches a is L when f(x) approaches L as x approaches a from both sides, excluding x=a itself. We write lim(x→a) f(x)=L. The value f(a) may differ from L or may be undefined.
One-sided limit
A left-hand limit uses x<a; a right-hand limit uses x>a. A finite two-sided limit exists exactly when both one-sided limits exist and equal the same finite number.
Indeterminate form
The form 0/0 obtained by direct substitution is not a value. Different expressions with this form can have different limits. Simplify the expression or use another valid limit argument before deciding the result.
Direct substitution
Polynomials are continuous everywhere. A rational function is continuous wherever its denominator is nonzero. At such points its limit equals its value. Check the denominator and domain before substituting.

The relationships you will use

  • lim(x→a) f(x) = LBasic wordingIt reads: The closer we bring x to a, the closer f(x) is to L.
  • lim(x→a⁻) f = lim(x→a⁺) f ⟺ the limit is thereCondition of existenceThe difference between the two sides alone is sufficient to rule that there is no limit.
  • lim(x→a) p(x) = p(a) for polynomial pDirect substitutionPolynomials do not create surprises, and substitution is always legitimate.
  • lim(x→0) sin x/x = 1Basic trigonometric limitx in radians.
  • lim(x→∞) (2x²+1)/(3x²−x)=2/3Equal degreeWe divide by x².

Why is it permissible to delete the factor even though it is equal to zero at the point

This is the question that many explanations skip. Deletion seems like a clear violation, so let's see why it's legitimate.

  1. Start at (x² − 9)/(x − 3) and factor the numerator to (x − 3)(x + 3).Why: factoring reveals the common factor that creates zero in the denominator, so we know the source of the problem instead of confronting it.
  2. Note that the definition of limit speaks of x being close to 3 and x ≠ 3.Why: This is the crucial point: the limit never asks about the value of the function at 3, but rather about its behavior around it.
  3. As long as x ≠ 3, x − 3 ≠ 0, and division by a non-zero number are allowed, eliminate the operator.Why: Deletion is prohibited only at zero, and we have excluded zero by definition itself, so there is no objection left.
  4. The expression becomes x + 3 for every x ≠ 3, which is a polynomial, so substitute x = 3 to get 6.Why: The two functions match in every point that concerns the limit, so their limit is the same, and it is permissible to substitute it for the second.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

Always start with direct substitution. If a specific number comes out, you're done in one step. If the indeterminate form 0/0 comes out, this is not the end of the method, but rather its beginning: go to the factoring, If there is a square root, multiply by the conjugate. That's the whole decision tree in this lesson.

Example: 1 · is a fraction that gives 0/0, solved by factorization

Find lim(x→2) (x² − 4)/(x − 2).

  1. Substitute x = 2: for the numerator 4 − 4 = 0 and the denominator for 2 − 2 = 0, and the form is 0/0.Why: We start with substitution to find out whether we need additional work at all. Indeterminate form 0/0 tells us we need it.
  2. Factor the numerator : x² − 4 = (x − 2)(x + 2).Why: The appearance of 0 in both the numerator and denominator means that (x − 2) is a common factor, and the factoring shows it.
  3. Remove (x − 2) from the numerator and denominator, leaving x + 2 for every x ≠ 2.Why: the limit considers x close to 2 and not equal to it, so the factor is non-zero and the deletion is legitimate.
  4. Substitute x = 2 into x + 2 and you get 4.Why: x + 2 is a polynomial, and direct substitution is always legitimate.

Output: 4

Example: 2 · is a square root, solved by multiplying the conjugate

Find lim(x→0) (√(x + 4) − 2)/x.

  1. Substitute x = 0: for the numerator √4 − 2 = 0 and the denominator for 0, and the form is 0/0.Why: The first step remains the same no matter how much the form of the expression changes: try the substitution first.
  2. factoring is not useful here because of the root, so multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate (√(x + 4) + 2).Why: Multiplying the root by its conjugate removes the root via (a − b)(a + b) = a² − b², and multiplying by the same expression A numerator and a denominator do not change the value of the fraction.
  3. The numerator becomes (x + 4) − 4 = x, so the fraction becomes x / (x(√(x + 4) + 2)).Why: Here the common factor x that was hidden behind the root appeared; This is the purpose of multiplying by the conjugate.
  4. Remove x, leaving 1/(√(x + 4) + 2), then replace x = 0 and you get 1/(2 + 2) = 0.25.Why: Deletion is permissible because x ≠ 0 is inside the limit, and the denominator after substitution is not equal to zero, so substitution is permissible.

Output: 0.25

Confine oscillation

Calculate the limit of x² cos(1/x) at zero.

  1. −1≤cos(1/x)≤1Why: The fluctuation is limited between these two numbers.
  2. −x²≤x² cos(1/x)≤x²Why: We multiply by the non-negative x² so the inequalities are not reversed.
  3. The two outer limits are zeroWhy: The squeeze theorem gives zero for the middle function.

Output: 0

Questions often left unexplained

Why is it called "0/0 «" "Unassigned" instead of zero or one?

Because the same image leads to different results : lim(x→0) x/x = 1 and lim(x→0) x²/x = 0, The three lim(x→0) 5x/x = 5.s give 0/0 upon substitution and end up as 1, 0, and 5. The image does not determine the result, hence the name.

Is the limit always equal to the value of the function at the point?

No. They are equal when the function is continuous at that point, which is the topic of the next lesson. Here, I saw a function whose limit at 3 is equal to 6, while it has no value at 3 at all. The function may have a value that contradicts its limit, as in the third question of the training.

Is it enough to calculate the limit from one side?

It is not enough to judge existence. It is sufficient to rule non-existence if you calculate the two aspects and find them different. But if you count only one aspect, you have not examined half of the issue.

Investigating a limit

Meaning

A limit describes what outputs approach near an input, not necessarily the output at that input. Both one-sided approaches must agree for a finite two-sided limit.

Method

Use a graph to see approach direction and a table of nearby inputs on both sides to estimate. Then seek an algebraic argument: numerical evidence alone can mislead near a sharp feature. Separate a hole, jump, vertical asymptote, and oscillation.

Worked reasoning

For f(x)=(x²−4)/(x−2) with x≠2, nearby outputs approach 4 because f(x)=x+2 away from 2. The value f(2) remains undefined; the limit is 4.

Check yourself

If f(x)=−1 for x<0 and f(x)=2 for x≥0, what is lim as x→0 of f(x)?

Reveal answer and reasoning

The left limit is −1 and the right limit is 2; the two-sided limit does not exist.

Common error. A table with values only on one side cannot establish a two-sided limit.

Algebraic limit methods

Meaning

Direct substitution works when the expression remains continuous. An apparent 0/0 signals algebra to perform, not a numerical answer.

Method

Factor and cancel a common nonzero factor near the point; rationalize radicals; or divide by the dominant power for behavior at infinity. For a trigonometric limit, rewrite it to expose sin(u)/u and account for any scale factor. Use one-sided signs to analyze a denominator tending to zero without cancellation.

Worked reasoning

lim as x→3 of (x²−9)/(x−3) equals lim(x+3)=6 after factoring. In contrast, lim as x→∞ of (3x²−1)/(2x²+5)=3/2 after division by x².

Check yourself

Evaluate lim as x→0 of sin(5x)/x.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Rewrite as 5[sin(5x)/(5x)]; the inner ratio tends to 1, so the limit is 5.

Common error. Cancellation is legal for x near the target, not at the point where the original denominator is zero.

Approach from both sides

Idea

A limit is the value approached near c; it can differ from f(c). A two-sided finite limit exists only if both one-sided approaches agree. A hole changes the function value but need not change the limit.

First move

From a graph or piecewise rule, trace toward c from the left and right separately. State a two-sided limit only after comparing them.

Worked reasoning

Let f(x)=x+1 for x<2, and f(x)=x² for x≥2. The left limit at 2 is 3, the right limit is 4, and f(2)=4. Because 3≠4, the two-sided limit does not exist.

Your turn

Let g(x)=x² for x≠1 and g(1)=7. Find the limit as x approaches 1 and g(1).

Reveal answer and reasoning

The limit is 1, while g(1)=7. The isolated assigned value does not control nearby behavior.

If it went wrong. Substituting the point value for the limit confuses the output at c with the approach to c.

Algebraic limits and continuity

Idea

Direct substitution works when the expression is continuous at the point. An apparent 0/0 is a signal to simplify, not an answer. Continuity requires an existing limit, a defined point value, and equality between them.

First move

Substitute first. If 0/0 appears, factor, rationalize, or simplify on a punctured neighborhood. For a piecewise join, match the two side limits and the assigned value.

Worked reasoning

lim as x→3 of (x²−9)/(x−3) is not 0/0. For x≠3, factor to x+3; its limit is 6. Defining the missing value as 6 would make the function continuous at 3.

Your turn

Choose a so f(x)=ax+1 for x<2 and f(x)=x² for x≥2 is continuous at 2.

Reveal answer and reasoning

The right limit and point value are 4. Match 2a+1=4, so a=3/2.

If it went wrong. Matching only branch formulas is insufficient if the branch with equality assigns a different point value.

One-sided limits and failure to agree

Principle

A finite two-sided limit exists only if the left and right approaches settle at the same number. A point can have a different assigned value without changing that approach.

Why and when

Trace each side separately on a graph or in a piecewise rule. Distinguish a finite limit from unbounded behavior, a jump, and persistent oscillation.

Worked solution

Let f(x)=x+2 for x<1 and f(x)=x² for x≥1. The left limit is 3, the right limit is 1, and f(1)=1. Therefore the two-sided limit does not exist.

Try it

Let g(x)=x² for x≠2 and g(2)=9. Find the limit as x approaches 2 and g(2).

Reveal answer and reasoning

The limit is 4; the assigned value is 9. Changing one point does not alter the values around it.

Common trap. Substitution gives the point value only after continuity has been established.

Limit laws and algebraic repair

Principle

Limit laws permit arithmetic with finite limits. Direct substitution is valid for continuous expressions where defined. The indeterminate form 0/0 signals that more analysis is needed.

Why and when

Try substitution first. If 0/0 appears, factor, combine fractions, or rationalize. Simplify on x≠c before taking the limit; the function need not be defined at c.

Worked solution

lim as x→3 of (x²−9)/(x−3) equals lim(x+3)=6 because x²−9=(x−3)(x+3) away from 3.

Try it

Find lim as x→0 of [√(1+2x)−1]/x.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Multiply by the conjugate: 2/[√(1+2x)+1], which tends to 1.

Common trap. 0/0 is neither zero nor a limit value; it is a prompt to transform the expression.

Practice

Question types

Each style examines something different. These selected patterns cover the lesson objectives, It is not all that can be asked about the topic.

The first type: direct substitution

You know when the matter is finished in one step.

Find lim(x→4) (2x² − 3x + 1).

The expression is a polynomial, so direct substitution is : 2(4²) − 3(4) + 1 = 32 − 12 + 1 = 21. There is no need for factoring or any trick, because the substitution did not give an unspecified picture.

Mode 2 · indeterminate form 0/0 is removed by factoring

Shift the expression before the substitution instead of stopping at 0/0.

Find lim(x→5) (x² − 25)/(x − 5).

Substitution gives 0/0. Factor the numerator : x² − 25 = (x − 5)(x + 5), then eliminate (x − 5) Because x ≠ 5 is inside the limit. x + 5 remains, and substituting in gives 5 + 5 = 10.

The third type: value versus limit

Distinguish between what a function is equal to at a point and what it approaches around it.

A function defined as : f(x) = (x² − 4)/(x − 2) for each x ≠ 2 and f(2) = 7. Find lim(x→2) f(x).

the limit does not look at x = 2 at all, the value of f(2) = 7 does not enter the calculation. For every x ≠ 2 there is f(x) = (x − 2)(x + 2)/(x − 2) = x + 2, so the limit is 2 + 2 = 4. This is a function whose limit is 4 and whose value is 7 at the same point, which are two different things.

Fourth style · One-sided limit

One aspect is examined carefully, and it is the basis for ruling that there is no limit.

Let f(x) = |x − 5|/(x − 5)., find the limit from the left as lim(x→5⁻) f(x).

To the left of 5, x < 5, so x − 5 is negative, and the absolute value of a negative number is negative: |x − 5| = −(x − 5)., the fraction becomes −(x − 5)/(x − 5) = −1 for every x < 5, and the limit is −1. On the right, the result is +1. The two sides are different, so the general limit at 5 does not exist.

Type 5: A square root that requires a conjugate

Choose the right tool when factoring fails.

Find lim(x→0) (√(x + 9) − 3)/x. Write the result as a decimal number.

substitution gives 0/0, and the factoring is not useful due to the presence of the root. Multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate (√(x + 9) + 3), so the numerator becomes (x + 9) − 9 = x, and the fraction becomes x/(x(√(x + 9) + 3)). Remove x, leaving 1/(√(x + 9) + 3), and when x = 0 equals 1/(3 + 3) = 1/6 ≈ 0.1667.
