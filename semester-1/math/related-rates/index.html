Before you start

What will you be able to do after this lesson?

  • Convert the description of the change of related quantities into an equation and time rates in their units.
  • Differentiate the relationship with respect to time before substituting values at a particular instant.
  • Interprets the rate sign and verifies the consistency of units in geometry and motion.

What this lesson does not cover

  • The lesson assumes fixed geometric shapes and differentiable relationships; Models in which the shape changes require additional equations.
  • The solution of differential equations over a time interval is studied later; Here we calculate the rate at a specific moment.

We mentioned them so that you do not think that you have completed the whole topic.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

First write a relation among the changing quantities. Differentiate with respect to time, including every chain-rule factor. Only then insert the quantities and rates at the specified instant.

Worked example

For a square with side s, A=s² gives dA/dt=2s·ds/dt. At s=3 m and ds/dt=0.2 m/min, dA/dt=1.2 m²/min.

Engineering connection Tank level and stored volume are related by geometry. A level rate is not the same quantity as a volumetric flow rate.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

Start with a small drawing and map out what stays constant and what changes. Write the data in two separate forms: instantaneous values, such as r=2, and rates, such as dr/dt=0.1. The two symbols answer two different questions: What is the volume now? How quickly does it change? Do not derive a rate by dividing a value by time unless an average is required and the starting point is appropriate.

In a geometrically similar cone the radius of the water surface does not remain constant as it rises. If R/H=1/2 then r=h/2 at every moment. Plug this relationship into V=πr²h/3 before differentiation to get a function with one variable. Using the radius of the full cone instead of the radius of the water surface creates an incorrect cylindrical model.

After differentiation, substitute the values, then solve for the unknown rate. Check the sign: If the bottom of a ladder moves away from the wall, the top should go down in the usual position. Also check the unit: Dividing m³/s by m² gives m/s. the numerical result Without these two checks you may be hiding switching rates or forgetting the chain rule.

The pattern is local: a rate may become large near a critical geometric position. In the scale the denominator appears y; As the top approaches Earth, the formula predicts a high speed, but the model assumes continued contact and restricted motion. We do not extend the formula to y=0 as it loses its validity.

Related rates
When an equation relates two or more quantities, changing one of them limits the change of the rest. We use the derivative of that equation with respect to time to relate rates of change. We do not always need to know quantities as explicit functions of time. Rather, the geometric relationship and the values ​​of the given moment and rate are sufficient for us.
Instantaneous value
The value of the quantity at the moment of question, such as the height of the water 2 m., is not constant throughout the process. Substituting it before the differentiation eliminates the change we are trying to measure. As for the radius of a solid cylindrical tank, it is a real constant that may be treated as a constant from the beginning.
Volumetric rate
The derivative of the volume with respect to time, such as dV/dt, in the unit m³/s. The sign is positive when the volume increases and negative when it decreases. Height has a rate in m/s, so the volume rate cannot be equated with the height rate without space connecting them.
Implicit time differentiation
We differentiate each variable quantity as a function of time: The derivative of x²+y²=L² is 2x dx/dt+2y dy/dt=0 when L is constant. The other side is zero because the length of the ladder is constant, not because x or y do not change.

The relationships you will use

  • dV/dt = 4πr² dr/dtBall expandingderived V=4πr³/3; The radius is variable.
  • dV/dt = πR² dh/dtSolid cylinderR is constant, and h is fluid height.
  • x dx/dt + y dy/dt = 0Fixed length ladderFrom x²+y²=L².

How does surface area convert to a link between two rates?

In a sphere, a small increase in radius adds a thin layer of volume.

  1. V=4πr³/3Why: The volume depends on the variable radius.
  2. dV/dt=(4π/3)·3r²·dr/dtWhy: The force rule and then the chain rule.
  3. dr/dt=(dV/dt)/(4πr²)Why: For the same volumetric flow, a larger ball increases slower in radius.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

Define the variables, write the relationship, differentiate time, and then plug in the instant values. Don't start by making amends in the original relationship.

Cylindrical tank

The radius 2 m is fixed. Water enters at a rate of 0.4 m³/s. Calculate the rate of rise.

  1. V=π(2)²h=4πhWhy: The radius is geometrically constant.
  2. dV/dt=4π dh/dtWhy: The height alone changes.
  3. dh/dt=0.4/(4π)=0.03183 m/sWhy: We divide the average volume by the area of the base.

Output: 0.03183 m/s

Sliding ladder

A ladder has a length of 5 m, a distance of 3 m below it and is moving away at a speed of 0.2 m/s. Calculate the speed of change in height above.

  1. y=√(25−9)=4 mWhy: Moment values from Pythagoras.
  2. 2x dx/dt+2y dy/dt=0Why: The length is fixed but the distances are variable.
  3. dy/dt=−3(0.2)/4=−0.15 m/sWhy: Negative indicates a decline at the top of the ladder.

Output: -0.15 m/s

Questions often left unexplained

Can the volume be fixed and the height variable?

Yes if the bowl area changes; Therefore, assumptions must be established before choosing a relationship. The cylinder formula here assumes a constant radius.

Implicit differentiation

Meaning

A curve can define y without a simple explicit formula. Differentiate both sides with respect to x, treating y as a changing function of x.

Method

Whenever a differentiated term contains y, apply the chain rule and include y′. Collect every y′ term, factor, and solve. A resulting slope may depend on both x and y; evaluate it at a point only after finding the general relation.

Worked reasoning

For x²+y²=25, differentiation gives 2x+2yy′=0, so y′=−x/y when y≠0. At (3,4), the tangent slope is −3/4.

Check yourself

For xy=6, find y′.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Product rule gives y+xy′=0, hence y′=−y/x where x≠0.

Common error. Differentiating y² as 2y rather than 2yy′ silently treats y as a constant.

Related rates

Meaning

Related rates connect several quantities changing with time through one geometric or physical equation.

Method

Draw and label a diagram; write a relation between variables; differentiate with respect to time before inserting the instant’s measurements; solve for the requested rate and state units and direction. If the geometry changes width, a constant input volume rate need not produce a constant height rate.

Worked reasoning

For a sphere at radius 5 ft expanding at 0.1 ft/min, dV/dt=4π(5)²(0.1)=10π ft³/min.

Check yourself

A circle radius grows at 2 cm/s. How quickly does area grow when radius is 3 cm?

Reveal answer and reasoning

A=πr², so dA/dt=2πr(dr/dt)=12π cm²/s.

Common error. Substituting r before differentiating can incorrectly turn a changing quantity into a constant.

Reference and worked examples

Adapted Book material selected for this topic.

Implicit differentiation

Principle

A curve may be defined by an equation without solving explicitly for y. Differentiate both sides with respect to x, treating y as a function of x, then solve for y′.

Why and when

Every derivative of a term involving y needs a chain-rule factor y′. Product terms involving x and y need the product rule. Evaluate a slope only at a point that satisfies the original relation.

Worked solution

For x²+xy+y²=7, differentiate to 2x+y+xy′+2yy′=0. Thus y′=−(2x+y)/(x+2y). At (1,2), the slope is −4/5.

Try it

For x²+y²=25, find the tangent slope at (3,4).

Reveal answer and reasoning

2x+2yy′=0, so y′=−x/y=−3/4 at the given point.

Common trap. Differentiating y² as 2y omits the rate at which y changes with x.

Related rates with changing geometry

Principle

Several quantities can change together while satisfying one geometric relation. Differentiate the relation with respect to time before inserting values from a single instant.

Why and when

Draw and label the geometry; identify given rates with signs and units; differentiate; substitute current dimensions and solve for the unknown rate.

Worked solution

A spherical balloon has V=(4/3)πr³. If r=2 cm and dr/dt=0.5 cm/s, then dV/dt=4πr² dr/dt=8π cm³/s.

Try it

A circular stain has radius 3 cm and grows at 2 cm/s. How fast does its area grow?

Reveal answer and reasoning

A=πr² gives dA/dt=2πr dr/dt=12π cm²/s.

Common trap. Substituting the current radius before differentiating makes a changing variable look constant.
