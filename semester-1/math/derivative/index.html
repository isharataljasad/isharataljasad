Derivative as a rate

Connect a tangent slope to an instantaneous rate.

What will you be able to do after this lesson?

  • Interpret a derivative as tangent slope and instantaneous rate of change.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

The derivative is the limit of a difference quotient: [f(x+h) − f(x)]/h as h tends to zero. Average change uses two distinct points; instantaneous change uses their limiting slope. Keep the output unit divided by the input unit.

Worked example

For f(x)=x², the difference quotient becomes 2x+h. Its limit is 2x. At x=3 the slope is 6.

Engineering connection If concentration is measured in mol/L and time in minutes, its derivative has units mol/(L·min).

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

Average rate of change
The amount of change of f divided by the amount of change of x between two points : (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a). It is the slope of the secant line connecting the two points, and it describes the entire interval, not a specific moment.
Derivative at a point
the limit of the average rate of change when the two points converge: ‎f′(a) = lim(h→0) (f(a + h) − f(a))/h‎. It is a single number that describes the behavior of the function at a alone, not over an interval.
Difference quotient
The expression [f(a+h)−f(a)]/h measures the average rate of change over a nonzero increment h. Substitution of h=0 makes this quotient undefined. The derivative, when it exists, is its limit as h approaches zero.
Tangent line
The line whose slope is f′(a) and passes through the point (a, f(a)), and its equation ‎y − f(a) = f′(a)(x − a)‎. Its definition is not that it “touches the curve at one point”; It may intersect it at other points and remain tangent at a.
Differentiability
Differentiability at a means the derivative exists there as a finite two-sided limit. Differentiability implies continuity. The converse fails: |x| is continuous at 0, but its one-sided slopes are −1 and +1, so it is not differentiable there.

The relationships you will use

  • f′(a) = lim(h→0) (f(a + h) − f(a)) / hDefinition of the limitThis is the reference from which all the rules for the following lesson are derived.
  • average rate = (f(b) − f(a)) / (b − a)Average over a intervalNeeds two points; The derivative requires one point.
  • y − f(a) = f′(a)(x − a)Tangent equationThe slope is from the derivative, and the point is from the function itself.
  • v(t) = s′(t)Instantaneous speedderivative of position with respect to time; This is the physical reading of the derivative.

Why don't we put h = 0 directly, and why is it correct to delete after simplification

The question that the explanation usually jumps to: If h is going to be zero, why not set it to zero from the beginning?

  1. Put h = 0 into (f(a + h) − f(a))/h and you get 0/0.Why: The denominator is null, and division by zero is undefined. Direct substitution is always closed here, not sometimes.
  2. Take f(x) = x² when a = 3: is the numerator (3 + h)² − 9 = 6h + h².Why: Expanding the parentheses showed that each term in the numerator has a factor h; This is not a coincidence, but the reason for the success of the method.
  3. Divide by h: (6h + h²)/h = 6 + h, and this is true for all h ≠ 0.Why: The limit is taught that h is close to zero and not equal to it, so division by h is permissible within it.
  4. Take the limit of 6 + h at h → 0 and you get 6.Why: After simplification, the expression became a polynomial, and substitution is legitimate. the limit has not changed, but the form in which we calculate it has changed.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

Write the difference quotient and find a way to calculate its limit. In polynomial examples, we expand the parentheses and reduce the factor h when h≠0. In roots, we may need conjugates, and in other functions we use the properties of limits. The non-appearance of a reducible factor does not prove that algebra is wrong nor does the derivative exist; Check out the limit itself.

Example 1 · is a quadratic function

Find f′(3) where f(x) = x², from the definition.

  1. Type f(3 + h) = (3 + h)² = 9 + 6h + h².Why: A correct jaw is half the work; Neglecting the middle term 6h gives an incorrect derivative.
  2. Subtract f(3) = 9:, leaving the numerator 6h + h².Why: The fixed term always goes away, leaving only what carries h. This makes the following division possible.
  3. Divide by h: (6h + h²)/h = 6 + h, by h ≠ 0.Why: Within the limit h ≠ 0, division by it is permissible. This is where the condition is forgotten.
  4. Take the limit at h → 0: resulting 6.Why: The expression has many limits, so substitution is legitimate. The resultant is the slope of the tangent at x = 3.

Output: f′(3) = 6

Example 2 · is a rational function

Find f′(2) where f(x) = 1/x, from the definition.

  1. The numerator 1/(2 + h) − 1/2. unites the denominators : (2 − (2 + h))/(2(2 + h)) = −h/(2(2 + h)).Why: The standardization of the denominators is what reveals the disappearing h factor; Without it, deletion is not possible.
  2. Divide by h: to result in −1/(2(2 + h)).Why: Dividing by h means multiplying by 1/h, so h is removed from the numerator and the negative remains.
  3. Replace h = 0: −1/(2 × 2) = −0.25.Why: The position after substitution is not non-existent, as substitution is legitimate. The sign is negative because 1/x is decreasing at x = 2.

Output: f′(2) = −0.25

Questions often left unexplained

Why do we use h and not use points a and b and bring b closer to a?

The two formulas are equivalent: put b = a + h and one converts to the other, b → a becomes h → 0. Rather, the h format was preferred because it makes the denominator a single letter that is easy to delete after simplification.

Does the tangent touch the curve at only one point?

No, this is a common and inaccurate description. The tangent to y = x³ at zero is the horizontal axis, and it intersects the curve At that same point. A tangent is defined by the slope at a point, not by the number of intersection points.

What does it mean for the derivative to be negative?

The function is decreasing at that point: increasing x slightly decreases f(x). In physical reading, it means movement in the negative direction. The magnitude of the derivative says how fast, and the sign says where.

Derivative from its limit definition

Meaning

The derivative is the limit of difference quotients and represents local slope or instantaneous rate. Differentiability requires a finite common limit from both sides.

Method

Substitute f(a+h), subtract f(a), simplify the numerator, cancel h while h≠0, then take h→0. For radicals, multiply by the conjugate. At a corner or cusp, compare one-sided slopes.

Worked reasoning

For f(x)=√x and a>0, [√(a+h)−√a]/h becomes 1/[√(a+h)+√a] after rationalization. Hence f′(a)=1/(2√a).

Check yourself

Use the definition to find the derivative of f(x)=x²+1 at x=3.

Reveal answer and reasoning

The quotient is [(3+h)²+1−10]/h=6+h; its limit is 6.

Common error. Substituting h=0 before simplifying creates 0/0 and loses the limiting slope.

Rates of change in context

Meaning

A derivative inherits context and units. Position yields velocity; velocity yields acceleration. Speed is |velocity|, so direction and speed are distinct.

Method

Write the physical quantity as a function of time, differentiate, evaluate at the requested time, then report units. For landing or a turning point, solve the relevant equation first and reject times outside the physical domain.

Worked reasoning

If s(t)=80−16t² feet, then v(t)=−32t feet/second. The object reaches ground when 80−16t²=0, so t=√5 seconds; its impact velocity is −32√5 feet/second.

Check yourself

For s(t)=t³−6t² meters, find velocity and acceleration at t=2 seconds.

Reveal answer and reasoning

v(t)=3t²−12t, so v(2)=−12 m/s. a(t)=6t−12, so a(2)=0 m/s².

Common error. A negative velocity means direction, not negative speed.

From secant to tangent

Idea

A derivative is the limit of average change over a shrinking interval. It is the tangent slope and the instantaneous rate, with output units divided by input units.

First move

When a problem asks for instantaneous slope or rate, compute f′ at the given input. A tangent line also needs the point (c,f(c)).

Worked reasoning

For f(x)=x², [f(c+h)−f(c)]/h=[(c+h)²−c²]/h=2c+h, so f′(c)=2c. At c=3, the tangent through (3,9) has slope 6: y−9=6(x−3).

Your turn

Find the tangent line to f(x)=x²+1 at x=2.

Reveal answer and reasoning

f(2)=5 and f′(2)=4, so y−5=4(x−2).

If it went wrong. A tangent slope alone is not the tangent line; include the function’s point value.

Read derivative graphs and corners

Idea

The sign of f′ reports increasing or decreasing behavior. A zero derivative gives a horizontal tangent but does not by itself prove an extremum. A corner or jump can prevent differentiability.

First move

Read the slope of f to infer f′, and check one-sided slopes at a corner. Differentiability implies continuity, but continuity alone does not imply differentiability.

Worked reasoning

For f(x)=|x|, the left slope at 0 is −1 and the right slope is +1. The function is continuous at 0, but f′(0) does not exist. For x³, f′(0)=0 yet there is no local extremum.

Your turn

For f(x)=x³−3x, identify intervals where f rises.

Reveal answer and reasoning

f′(x)=3x²−3=3(x−1)(x+1), positive on (−∞,−1) and (1,∞).

If it went wrong. A critical point is a candidate; classify it by sign change or another valid test.

Derivative from the tangent limit

Principle

The derivative is the limiting slope of secant lines and the instantaneous rate of output change. The tangent line at a uses both that slope and the point (a,f(a)).

Why and when

Form the difference quotient, simplify before taking the limit, then use point-slope form. Units of f′ are output units per input unit.

Worked solution

For f(x)=x²+1, [(a+h)²+1−(a²+1)]/h=2a+h; hence f′(a)=2a. At a=2, point (2,5), the tangent is y−5=4(x−2).

Try it

Find the tangent line to f(x)=x³ at x=1.

Reveal answer and reasoning

f(1)=1 and f′(1)=3, so y−1=3(x−1).

Common trap. A tangent slope alone is not a line; include the point on the original graph.

Derivative as a function and differentiability

Principle

The derivative function records tangent slope at each input where that slope exists. Its sign describes direction. Differentiability implies continuity, but a continuous corner can still lack a derivative.

Why and when

Compare one-sided slopes at corners, cusps, and vertical tangents. At a jump there can be no derivative. A graph of f′ is above the axis where f rises and below it where f falls.

Worked solution

For f(x)=|x|, the left slope at zero is −1 and the right slope is +1. The function is continuous there, but its derivative is undefined at zero.

Try it

For f(x)=x³−3x, where is f increasing?

Reveal answer and reasoning

f′(x)=3x²−3>0 for x<−1 and x>1, so f rises on (−∞,−1) and (1,∞).

Common trap. A zero derivative does not itself prove a maximum or minimum; check signs.

Practice

Question types

Each style examines something different. These selected patterns cover the lesson objectives, It is not all that can be asked about the topic.

The first type: derived from the definition

Perform all four steps on a quadratic function.

Find f′(5) from the definition, where f(x) = x².

f(5 + h) = 25 + 10h + h², so the numerator after subtraction is 10h + h², Dividing by h gives 10 + h, and the limit at h → 0 is 10.

The second type is the average rate of change

You use two points and divide by the length of the interval.

What is the average rate of change of f(x) = x² over the interval from x = 1 to x = 4?

‎(f(4) − f(1))/(4 − 1) = (16 − 1)/3 = 15/3 = 5‎. This is the slope of the secant between the two points, not the slope of the tangent at either point.

The third type is a polynomial with two terms

Track every term, and do not neglect the linear term.

Find f′(2) from the definition, where f(x) = 3x² − 2x.

‎f(2 + h) = 3(4 + 4h + h²) − 2(2 + h) = 12 + 12h + 3h² − 4 − 2h‎. Subtract f(2) = 8 and you get 10h + 3h², divide by h and you get 10 + 3h. And the limit is 10.

Fourth type: Physical reading

The derivative is translated into instantaneous unit velocity.

The position of an object in meters s(t) = 5t² and the time in seconds. What is its instantaneous speed at t = 3 s?

Instantaneous speed s′(t). from definition : s(3 + h) − s(3) = 30h + 5h², Dividing by h gives 30 + 5h, and the limit is 30 m/s.

The fifth type is a rational function with a negative sign

It unifies the maqamat and preserves the sign.

Find f′(2) from the definition, where f(x) = 1/x. Write the result as a signed decimal number.

Numerator 1/(2 + h) − 1/2 = −h/(2(2 + h)). Divide by h and you get −1/(2(2 + h)), and when h = 0 equals −1/4 = −0.25.
