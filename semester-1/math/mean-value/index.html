Rolle and Mean Value Theorems

Check conditions before claiming an interior derivative value.

Book →Pearson →Educator →

The idea

The Mean Value Theorem needs continuity on [a,b] and differentiability on (a,b). It guarantees some interior c with f′(c)=[f(b)−f(a)]/(b−a). Rolle’s theorem adds equal endpoint values and guarantees f′(c)=0.

A first example

For f(x)=x² on [1,3], average slope is (9−1)/2=4. Since f′(x)=2x, c=2 satisfies the conclusion.

Engineering connection. An average rate can guarantee an instantaneous matching rate only when the mathematical conditions hold.

What this explanation develops

  • Check Rolle's conditions and the mean value theorem before using the result.
  • Find a point at which the instantaneous slope equals the average slope and verify its range.
  • Apply the theorem to motion and explain why a failed hypothesis removes its guarantee without necessarily disproving its conclusion.

Definitions & meaning

Before solving any equation, write the name of the function and the interval. Polynomials are continuous and differentiable everywhere, but rational functions need checking the denominator, radical functions need checking the field, and segmental functions need checking the joining point. Neglecting the conditions makes correct algebra answer a question that the theorem did not allow.

In Roll, the curve starts and ends at the same height. If it is not constant, the presence of an internal peak or trough with differentiability results in a zero derivative. If it is constant, every interior point is valid. Therefore, the expression “there is a point” allows for a point, several points, or an infinite number of them.

To get the mean value of Roll, subtract the secant line from the function. The difference becomes zero at both ends, so Roll is applied to it. Differentiation of the difference says that the derivative of a function is equal to the slope of the secant at a point. This trick explains why the result uses the difference of the values ​​of the two sides divided by the length of the interval.

In motion, if a body has a displacement of 120 km in two hours and the position is continuous and differentiable in the interval, then its instantaneous vector velocity is 60 km/h at some instant. If the body returns in the opposite direction, we do not confuse the displacement with the total distance; Position theorem describes velocity and its average.

After solving f′(c)=m, examine all the roots: reject what lies outside (a,b), and do not accept both sides. The result may not give the only or exact moment in a general physical description; It is a guarantee of existence unless we explicitly know the function.

Rolle’s theorem
If f is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b) and f(a)=f(b), then there is a point c inside the interval that satisfies f′(c)=0., meaning there is at least one horizontal tangent. The theorem does not say that the point is single or that it is the middle of the interval.
Mean Value Theorem
If f is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b), then c lies within the interval. Check f′(c)=(f(b)−f(a))/(b−a). There is a tangent parallel to the secant between the two ends. The theorem is not about the average values ​​of the function, but about the average rate of its change.
Open and closed intervals
[a,b] includes both parties, (a,b) excludes both parties. We need the values ​​of the two parties and their continuity from within the field to build the segment. As for the guaranteed point, it lies between them; We do not need a two-sided derivative at one end of a closed field.
Sufficient conditions
When the conditions are met, the theorem guarantees the result. If a condition is absent, we do not automatically conclude that the result is impossible; We conclude that this theorem does not guarantee it. The equation can be checked directly to see if there is a suitable point in the particular case.

Details that matter

There may be a vertex or point whose derivative is zero. The absence of a condition invalidates the inference of the theorem, and does not prove the negation of the result.
