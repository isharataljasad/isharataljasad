CHEM 101 · TOPIC 02

Quantum theory

Connect light energy and the electronic model.

Book →Pearson →Educator →

The idea

Photons have energy E=hf=hc/λ. Shorter wavelength means higher photon energy. Atomic electrons occupy allowed energy states; orbitals describe probability rather than miniature planetary paths.

A first example

If wavelength is halved, photon energy doubles because E is inversely proportional to λ.

Engineering connection. Spectroscopy uses the relation between absorbed or emitted light and changes in electronic energy.

What this explanation develops

  • Relates wavelength, frequency, and photon energy with unit conversion.
  • Interpret discrete spectra and transitions between energy levels.
  • Determine allowed quantum numbers and electron capacities of shells, subshells and orbitals.
  • De Broglie and uncertainty are used without converting the quantum model to a planetary orbit.

Definitions & meaning

The photoelectric effect reveals a limit to interpreting light as a classical wave only: if the energy of the photon is less than the work function of the substance, no electron will be released with a single photon, no matter how many of those photons are increased in the simple model. Above the threshold, the greatest kinetic energy is KEmax=hν−Φ. Increasing the frequency raises the energy of the electrons, and increasing the intensity at the same frequency increases their potential number.

In hydrogen En=−13.6/n² eV. a negative sign means that the electron is bound relative to the zero chosen upon separation. Going up from n=1 to n=2 +10.2 eV needs corn. Return releases a photon with energy +10.2 eV, while changing the energy of the atom −10.2 eV. The energy of the photon is not written negative.

Each l has as many orbitals as 2l+1, and each orbital has a maximum of two electrons. So s can accommodate 2, p can accommodate 6, d can accommodate 10, and f can accommodate 14. The sum of the capacity in one principal level is 2n², but the order of filling in real atoms does not mean that one principal level is always filled completely before the next.

The uncertainty principle ΔxΔp≥ℏ/2 describes a quantum constraint on the dispersion of two measurements in a single state. Just because the device is poor, it doesn't mean we can't know anything about the electron. The narrower the position distribution, the wider the minimum of the momentum distribution. This result confirms the difference between the quantum description and the classical particle trajectory.

Photon
How much energy does electromagnetic radiation have? E=hν. Frequency determines the energy of one photon, but increasing the number of photons at the same frequency increases the total energy. Light intensity and photon energy are not the same quantity. In the vacuum c=λν; Within a medium, the speed of propagation changes, so we do not use c without specifying the model.
Energy quantization
The bound states allowed in the atom have discrete energies. Absorption of a photon corresponding to a permissible difference can raise the energy, and emission lowers it. Therefore, spectral lines appear instead of a continuous range for all energies. Ionization is a transition to an unbound state and requires an appropriate threshold energy.
Atomic orbital
A quantum state is described by a wave function, the square of whose magnitude gives the probability density of the electron's position. An orbital is not a circular path drawn for an electron. The common shapes s and p describe the probability distribution, and do not mean that the electron is a ball moving on the surface of that shape.
Quantum numbers
n=1,2,… sets the key level; l=0,…,n−1 sets subshell; mₗ=−l,…,+l defines one of the orbital states; mₛ=±1/2 describes the spin projection. No two electrons in an atom can have the same four numbers according to the Pauli Principle.
De Broglie wavelength
A particle with momentum p has a wavelength of λ=h/p. at non-relativistic velocities p=mv. This is a relation to matter and not a use of the speed of light instead of the speed of the electron. The short wavelength of large objects explains why we do not observe clear wave behavior in everyday conditions.
