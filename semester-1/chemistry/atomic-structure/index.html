Atomic structure

Count protons neutrons and electrons consistently.

Before you start

What will you be able to do after this lesson?

  • Extracts the number of protons, neutrons and electrons from the nuclear symbol and from the charge.
  • Differentiate between atomic number, mass number, and average atomic mass.
  • The average atomic mass is calculated from the masses and abundances of isotopes.
  • Convert between the number of particles, moles, and mass and interpret chemical formulas.

What this lesson does not cover

  • Quantum numbers are covered in the quantum theory lesson. Electron configurations, Hund’s rule and ions are covered in the periodic table lesson.
  • Nuclear binding energy and radioactive decay are beyond this lesson.
  • Polyatomic ions such as sulfates are not here; We limit ourselves to monoatomic ions.

We mentioned them so that you do not think that you have completed the whole topic.

Prerequisites

  • Read the nuclear symbol

    You need it because: Every calculation in this lesson begins by reading two numbers of the symbol, so a mistake in reading spoils what comes after it.

    The symbol is written with two numbers to the left of the element symbol: the upper one is the mass number A, The bottom one is the atomic number Z., so A is the sum of protons and neutrons, and Z is the number of protons alone. Remember that Z defines the element: every atom that has a proton 17 is chlorine, no matter how many neutrons it has.

  • Weighted average

    You need it because: Atomic mass is an average weighted by abundance, and whoever calculates it as an arithmetic average is mistaken in every issue of isotopes.

    The arithmetic mean treats the values equally, and the weighted gives each value its weight. If 90% from the sample has a mass of 20 and 10% has a mass of 30, then the weighted average 0.90 × 20 + 0.10 × 30 = 21, not (20 + 30)/2 = 25. The result is always close to the most abundant value, which is a sign that quickly reveals your error.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Atomic number counts protons. Mass number counts protons plus neutrons. Ion formation changes the electron count, while changing neutrons produces a different isotope. Read superscripts and charge separately.

Worked example

An ion with atomic number 11, mass number 23 and charge +1 contains 11 protons, 12 neutrons and 10 electrons.

Engineering connection Composition and charge are the starting information for every later chemical balance.

Approach 1 · Reference

Definitions and relationships

Mole is the amount of substance containing 6.02214076×10²³ of the specified entities. Name the entity: atoms, molecules, formula units, or ions. Molar mass collects the atomic masses according to the lower numbers in the formula: for water it is about 18.015 g/mol, and for one mole of water molecules there are two moles of hydrogen atoms and one mole of oxygen atoms.

The balanced equation preserves the number of atoms of each element and the charge. Change the parameter in front of the material and do not change the lower numbers because that creates another material. In 2H2+O2→2H2O, the moles ratio is 2:1:2, not the mass ratio. Convert masses to moles before comparing. The elementary formula is the simplest integer ratio of atoms, and the molecular formula is an integer multiple of it determined from the molar mass.

Atomic number (Z)
The number of protons in the nucleus. It is the identity of the element: it does not change in chemical reactions. Changing it means another element. In a neutral atom, the number of electrons is also equal to But this is a casual equality that ends as soon as the atom becomes an ion.
Mass number (A)
The sum of protons and neutrons in the nucleus, which is always an integer because it is a count, not a measurement. Hence: the number of neutrons = A − Z.
Isotope
Atoms of the same element differ in the number of neutrons, so they agree in Z and differ in A. Their chemical behavior is almost identical because chemistry is governed by electrons, not neutrons. But their mass varies.
Atomic mass
Weighted average of the masses of an element's isotopes according to their abundance in nature, in atomic mass unit u. It is not usually an integer, and this is the difference that distinguishes it from the mass number: The mass of chlorine is 35.45 u and not a single chlorine atom has a mass of 35.45.
Ion
An atom that has lost or gained electrons, so its balance is disturbed. The loss of electrons gives a positive charge because there are more protons. Acquiring it gives a negative charge. The nucleus does not change in either case.

The relationships you will use

  • protons = ZProtonsIt is read directly from the atomic number, and is not affected by the charge at all.
  • neutrons = A − ZNeutronsSubtract, not add. It is the only one that differs between isotopes of the element.
  • electrons = Z − chargeElectronsNote the subtraction with the sign: +3 charge means Z − 3, and −2 charge means Z + 2.
  • atomic mass = Σ (fraction × isotope mass)Average atomic massAbundances are converted from percentages to decimals before multiplying.
  • n=m/Mm; N=nNAA mole is a bridge between mass and particlesMass and molar mass in compatible units.

Why is the mass of chlorine 35.45 and there is no chlorine atom with this mass?

A question that confuses many students: The periodic table gives a fractional number, and the nucleus contains only integers.

  1. Chlorine has two major isotopes: ³⁵Cl, with abundance 75.77%, and ³⁷Cl, with abundance 24.23%.Why: Natural chlorine is a mixture, not one type, so any number that represents it must represent the mixture.
  2. Convert abundance to decimals : 0.7577 and 0.2423.Why: The percentage is not multiplied directly; A decimal fraction represents each isotope's share of the unit.
  3. Multiply the mass of each peer by its quota and add: 0.7577 × 34.97 + 0.2423 × 36.97.Why: This is the weighted average: each isotope is affected to the extent of its presence, not equally.
  4. The output is 26.50 + 8.96 = 35.46, which is close to the supported value 35.45.Why: The result is closer to 35 than to 37 because the light isotope is more abundant. This is a quick sign that reveals the error in the calculation before reviewing it.

Approach 3 · Step-by-step teaching

From zero to solution

Start with the atomic number Z: is protons, which is a constant that is not affected by charge and is not changed by isotopes. Then neutrons by subtraction A − Z. and finally electrons, which are the only ones affected by charge. In this order, you will not confuse the three, because you start with the constant and end with the variable.

Example: 1 · is a positive ion

How many protons, neutrons and electrons are in ²⁷Al³⁺?

  1. The atomic number of aluminum is Z = 13, and the protons are 13.Why: Protons are the identity of the element, and do not change by losing or gaining electrons.
  2. Neutrons = A − Z = 27 − 13 = 14.Why: The mass number is the sum of the protons and neutrons. Subtracting the protons leaves the neutrons.
  3. Electrons = Z − charge = 13 − 3 = 10.Why: The charge +3 means the loss of three electrons, so the positive becomes three more than the negative.

Output: 13 protons, 14 neutrons, 10 electrons

Example 2 · average atomic mass

There are two isotopes : ⁶³X with mass 62.93 u with abundance 69.15%, and ⁶⁵X with mass 64.93 u with abundance 30.85%. Calculate its atomic mass.

  1. Convert the two numbers into two fractions : 0.6915 and 0.3085.Why: A decimal is the isotope's share of a unit, and a percentage does not lend itself to direct multiplication.
  2. Multiply and add : 0.6915 × 62.93 + 0.3085 × 64.93.Why: Each isotope contributes as abundant as it is; This is the meaning of weighting.
  3. Output 43.52 + 20.03 = 63.55 u.Why: The result is closer to 62.93 because the lighter isotope is more abundant, so this is a quick check of the validity of the calculation.

Output: 63.55 u

Mass and number of moles

The mass of water is 36.0 g, use Mm=18.0 g/mol. What are the moles of water and the atoms of H?

  1. n(H2O)=36/18=2 molWhy: We divide the mass by the molar mass.
  2. n(H atoms)=2×2=4 molWhy: Each molecule carries two H. atoms
  3. N(H atoms)=4×6.022×10²³Why: We specify that the entity is an atom, not a molecule.

Output: 2 mol H2O; 4 mol H atoms

Questions often left unexplained

Why don't protons change in chemical reactions?

Because a chemical reaction is an exchange or sharing of electrons, and does not reach the nucleus. Changing protons means changing the element itself, and this is a nuclear reaction, not a chemical one. It requires orders of magnitude more energy.

Is the mass number the same as the atomic mass?

No. The mass number is an integer count of the particles in a single nucleus. Atomic mass is a weighted measurement of the mixture of isotopes as they exist in nature, and is often fractional. Confusing them is the most common mistake in this topic.

Why is the mass of the isotope not a strictly integer even though the particles are counted?

Because part of the mass of the particles is converted into binding energy that holds the nucleus together, The mass of the nucleus is slightly less than the sum of the masses of its individual components. Detailing this is beyond the scope of this lesson, as mentioned above.

Another explanation

Adapted Educator material selected for this topic.

Build atoms and ions from three counts

Idea. Atomic number Z counts protons and identifies the element. Mass number A counts protons plus neutrons in one isotope. Electrons set charge: losing electrons makes a cation; gaining them makes an anion. Isotopes of an element differ in neutron count.

Decision. Read the element symbol to obtain Z, subtract Z from A for neutrons, then adjust the electron count for the signed ionic charge. Use the sign of charge as a check.

Worked case. For an ion with Z=17, A=37, and charge −1, there are 17 protons, 20 neutrons, and 18 electrons.

Try this before revealing the answer

How many protons, neutrons, and electrons are in an ion with Z=12, A=25, charge +2?

Reveal answer and reasoning

There are 12 protons, 25−12=13 neutrons, and 12−2=10 electrons.

Misconception check. Mass number belongs to one isotope; it is not the decimal average atomic mass printed in a periodic table.

Use isotopic abundance as a weighted average

Idea. The atomic mass of a natural element is the abundance-weighted mean of its isotope masses. Abundance percentages must be converted to fractions before multiplying.

Decision. Set up one mass times one fractional abundance for each isotope. Check that fractions sum to one and that the answer lies between the lightest and heaviest isotope masses.

Worked case. An element with 75.0% isotope mass 10.0 u and 25.0% isotope mass 11.0 u has mean mass 0.750(10.0)+0.250(11.0)=10.25 u.

Try this before revealing the answer

An element has 20% isotope mass 50 u and 80% isotope mass 52 u. Find its average atomic mass.

Reveal answer and reasoning

0.20(50)+0.80(52)=51.6 u. The result is closer to 52 u because that isotope is more abundant.

Misconception check. A weighted average is not the simple midpoint unless the abundances are equal.

More decision practice

Adapted Pearson material selected for this topic.

Read atoms, isotopes, and ions

Question cue. A symbol has a mass number or charge, or a question asks for particles.

Meaning. Atomic number Z counts protons and defines the element. Mass number A counts protons plus neutrons. An ion differs from the neutral atom only in electron count; isotopes differ in neutron count.

First move. Read Z and A first. Set protons=Z and neutrons=A−Z. Let zion be the signed ionic charge number, such as +3 or −1; then electrons=Z−zion.

Worked case. For ⁵⁶Fe³⁺, Z=26: it has 26 protons, 30 neutrons, and 23 electrons.

Try this before revealing the answer

How many protons, neutrons, and electrons are in ³⁵Cl⁻?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Chlorine has Z=17. The ion has 17 protons, 35−17=18 neutrons, and 18 electrons because its charge is −1.

If stuck. A positive ion lost electrons; its nucleus did not lose protons.

Identify atoms and ions

Principle. Atomic number Z counts protons; mass number A counts protons plus neutrons. An ion changes electron count, not proton count. Isotopes differ in neutrons.

Decision. For a cation of charge +q, electrons = Z − q; for an anion of charge −q, electrons = Z + q.

Worked case. ²⁷Al³⁺ has 13 protons, 14 neutrons, and 10 electrons.

Check. Periodic-table atomic mass is an isotope-weighted average, not a mass number.

Practice

Question types

Each style examines something different. These selected patterns cover the lesson objectives, It is not all that can be asked about the topic.

The first type: Counting neutrons

It applies subtraction, not addition, and distinguishes A from Z.

How many neutrons are in a ⁵⁶Fe atom? Note that the atomic number of iron is 26.

Neutrons = A − Z = 56 − 26 = 30.

The second type: Ion electrons

The charge is applied to the electrons alone, and with the correct sign.

How many electrons are in the ion ³²S²⁻? Note that the atomic number of sulfur is 16.

Electrons = Z − charge = 16 − (−2) = 18. A negative charge means gaining two electrons, so there are two more electrons than protons.

The third type: average atomic mass

Weight by abundance rather than taking an average.

An element has two isotopes: the first has a mass of 10.0 u and its abundance is 20.0%, and the second has a mass of 11.0 u and its abundance is 80.0%. What is its atomic mass in u?

‎0.200 × 10.0 + 0.800 × 11.0 = 2.00 + 8.80 = 10.8 u‎. Note that the output is close to 11.0 because the second isotope is the most abundant.

The fourth type: Inferring the charge

Reverse the relationship: from numbers to charge instead of vice versa.

An ion in which 20 is a proton and 18 is an electron. What did you charge? Write the number with a sign.

charge = protons − electrons = 20 − 18 = +2. More protons, so the charge is positive.

Type 5: Isotope or ion

It determines which number has changed, which is what distinguishes an isotope from an ion.

An atom ⁴⁰Ca has an atomic number of 20. Another atom has the same atomic number and mass number 44. How many neutrons are in this other type?

The stability of Z and the variability of A means that they are isotopes, and the difference is in the neutrons alone. Neutrons = 44 − 20 = 24.

Every molecule has two atoms H, so the ratio is 2.
