CHEM 101 · TOPIC 06

Properties of solutions

Choose a concentration measure and preserve solute amount.

LearnCheckApply
THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Molarity is amount of solute divided by solution volume in litres. For dilution of the same solute without reaction or loss, the amount stays constant: M₁V₁=M₂V₂. Intermolecular interactions and dissolved particle count influence solution properties.

Worked example

Dissolving 0.20 mol to make 0.50 L of solution gives 0.40 mol/L. Adding solvent lowers molarity without changing the solute amount.

Engineering connection Concentration links the volume of a process stream to the amount of material it carries.

Make and dilute solutions by amount

Idea. Molarity measures solute amount per liter of final solution, not per liter of solvent. When a solution is diluted without losing solute, its moles remain constant while concentration and total volume change.

Decision. Convert final volume to liters for molarity. Use M1V1=M2V2 only when the same dissolved solute amount is retained; chemical reaction or mixing with a reacting solute needs a new mole balance.

Worked case. Diluting 25.0 mL of 2.00 M NaCl to a final volume of 100.0 mL gives 0.500 M NaCl.

Try this before revealing the answer

How much 1.50 M stock is needed to prepare 250 mL of 0.300 M solution?

Reveal answer and reasoning

V1=(0.300 M)(250 mL)/(1.50 M)=50.0 mL; add solvent to a final volume of 250 mL.

Misconception check. Do not add “250 mL of water” to 50 mL stock and assume the final solution volume is exactly 250 mL.

Connect solubility and particle count

Idea. Solubility depends on solute–solvent interactions and conditions. Ideal colligative changes depend mainly on the number of dissolved particles, not their identity. The van ’t Hoff factor i approximates the number of effective dissolved particles per formula unit.

Decision. Distinguish molality m, moles solute per kilogram solvent, from molarity M. For an ideal dilute electrolyte, estimate i from dissociation, then note that real ion pairing can reduce the effective value. Gas solubility at fixed temperature often rises with its partial pressure under Henry’s law.

Worked case. At the same low molality, ideal NaCl with i≈2 lowers freezing temperature about twice as much as a non-electrolyte with i≈1, if both obey the dilute model.

Try this before revealing the answer

At equal molality in the ideal dilute model, which has greater freezing-point depression: glucose or CaCl2?

Reveal answer and reasoning

CaCl2 dissociates ideally into three ions and has i≈3, so its depression is about three times that of glucose with i≈1.

Misconception check. Colligative formulas require the right concentration unit and an appropriate dilute-solution approximation.

Convert solution volume into reaction amount

Question cue. A liquid concentration and volume appear in the question.

Meaning. Molarity is solute moles per liter of solution. Dilution preserves solute moles when no reaction or loss occurs. Mixing with a reacting solute requires stoichiometry after calculating each amount.

First move. Convert mL to L, calculate n=MV, and apply the reaction ratio if there is one. For a pure dilution, set starting and final solute moles equal.

Worked case. 20.0 mL of 0.100 M HCl contains 0.00200 mol HCl. It requires 10.0 mL of 0.200 M NaOH for 1:1 neutralization.

Try this before revealing the answer

What volume of 0.500 M stock contains 0.100 mol solute?

Reveal answer and reasoning

V=n/M=0.100/0.500=0.200 L, or 200 mL.

If stuck. Molarity uses liters of complete solution, not kilograms of solvent.

Choose a concentration scale

Question cue. The problem specifies solution volume, solvent mass, or a mole fraction.

Meaning. Molarity depends on final solution volume and therefore temperature. Molality uses kilograms of solvent; mole fraction is moles of one component divided by all component moles.

First move. Circle the denominator named in the question. Convert mass to moles and volume to liters only where the chosen scale requires them.

Worked case. Dissolving 0.50 mol solute in 2.0 kg solvent gives 0.25 mol/kg molality. Final solution volume was not supplied, so molarity cannot yet be calculated.

Try this before revealing the answer

A solution has 0.20 mol solute and 0.80 mol solvent. What is solute mole fraction?

Reveal answer and reasoning

0.20/(0.20+0.80)=0.20.

If stuck. Molality uses solvent mass; using the mass of the entire solution changes the result.

Count dissolved particles in a colligative effect

Question cue. Freezing or boiling point shifts after adding a solute.

Meaning. Ideal colligative changes depend on the concentration of dissolved particles. An electrolyte may produce several ions per formula unit, summarized approximately by the van ’t Hoff factor i.

First move. Determine the solvent constant, molality, and realistic particle factor. Compare the result with the pure-solvent transition temperature in the correct direction.

Worked case. For an ideal nonelectrolyte at 1.0 m in water, freezing-point depression is about 1.86 °C, so the solution freezes near −1.86 °C.

Try this before revealing the answer

Which ideally makes a larger freezing-point depression at equal molality: glucose or NaCl?

Reveal answer and reasoning

NaCl, because it ideally produces about two dissolved ions per formula unit, whereas glucose remains one molecule.

If stuck. Real electrolytes may have i below the simple ion count because of ion pairing and nonideal behavior.

Use molarity and dilution

Principle. Molarity is solute moles per liter of solution. Dilution changes volume and concentration while conserving solute moles if no reaction occurs.

Decision. Use C₁V₁ = C₂V₂ only for dilution of the same solute.

Worked case. 25.0 mL of 2.00 M stock diluted to 100.0 mL becomes 0.500 M.

Check. Solution volume is not necessarily solvent volume.

Connect forces to properties

Principle. Dispersion exists for all particles; dipole interactions require permanent molecular dipoles; hydrogen bonding needs H attached to N, O, or F and a suitable acceptor.

Decision. For comparable size and shape, stronger attractions usually raise boiling point and lower vapor pressure.

Worked case. Water hydrogen bonds; methane has only dispersion forces.

Check. A polar bond does not automatically make an entire molecule polar.
