CHEM 101 · TOPIC 04

Chemical bonding

Account for electrons before assigning shape and polarity.

Book →Pearson →Educator →

The idea

Count valence electrons, connect atoms, distribute electrons and check formal charges. Electron-domain geometry and molecular shape are different when lone pairs are present. Molecular polarity depends on both bond polarity and shape.

A first example

CO₂ has 4+2(6)=16 valence electrons. Two double bonds give a linear arrangement; equal opposite bond dipoles cancel.

Engineering connection. Bonding and molecular shape help explain properties used in separation and material selection.

What this explanation develops

  • Count valence electrons, draw Lewis diagrams, and verify formal charge.
  • Use electron domains to determine molecular geometry and polarity.
  • Distinguish ionic and covalent bonds and attractive forces between molecules.
  • It relates the bond order to its length and strength and understands the introduction to the molecular orbitals.

Definitions & meaning

Lewis Plan: Calculate the sum of valence electrons, choose the structure of the atoms, draw single bonds, complete the shells of the peripheral and then the central atoms, and convert lone pairs to multiple bonds when needed. Recount the electrons and charges at the limit. Hydrogen suffices with two, boron may be minus eight, and radicals may carry a single electron; The octet rule is not a law without exception.

Four domains give a tetrahedral arrangement at a perfect angle 109.5°. With three bonds and a lone pair the shape is tripyramidal like NH3, and with two bonds and two free pairs it is curved like H2O. The actual angles are affected by different repulsions, so we are not literally transferring the perfect angle to each molecule.

The hybridization input connects two domains to sp, three to sp², and four to sp³ in examples of simple parent atoms. The σ bond results from axial overlap, and the π bond results from lateral overlap. The dual consists of one σ and one π, and the triplex consists of one σ and two π. These are modeling descriptions, not photographs of electrons.

In the molecular orbital model, electrons are distributed into bonding and antibonding orbitals. bond order =( Number of antibonding electrons −)/2. This explains the presence of paramagnetic unpaired electrons O2, something that the simple Lewis diagram does not reveal. Increasing the bond order often shortens or strengthens the bond when comparing the same two atoms.

Dispersion forces exist between all polarizable particles. There are also dipole forces between polar molecules, and appropriate hydrogen bonds when H binds to a N, O, or F atom and interacts with a suitable acceptor. Boiling water weakens the intermolecular attraction and does not loosen all of the O–H bonds within the molecule.

Covalent and ionic bonding
In covalent, a shared electronic density is concentrated between the atoms, and in an ionic compound, electrostatic attraction stabilizes a network of ions. Negativity difference helps describe bond polarity, but it is not an absolute limit that turns every bond into a pure class.
Formal charge
Accounting tool in a Lewis diagram: valence electrons for a free atom minus the non-bond minus the half-bond. The sum of the formal charges must equal the charge of the chemical species. The formal charge is not a direct measure of the true charge distribution or oxidation number.
Resonance
When the same connected atoms allow more than a suitable distribution of electrons, we use resonance formulas. The actual structure does not shift chronologically between separate drawings; It is an electronic distribution that is not described by a single local fee. In resonance, the electrons move in the drawing, but the atomic nuclei do not move.
VSEPR
The electron fields are arranged around the central atom to reduce repulsion in the model. A single, double or trinomial bond is one domain. Domain geometry includes lone pairs, while molecular shape describes the positions of atoms only.
Molecular polarity
Molecular polarity depends on the vector sum of the bond dipoles and the distribution of charge. CO2 has polar bonds, but its symmetric linear geometry makes their dipoles cancel. Water has a bent geometry, so its bond dipoles do not cancel. The elements present alone do not determine polarity; molecular geometry matters.

Worked examples

Electrons → Lewis → formal charge → fields → shape → polarity. Each step needs the result of the previous one.

Ammonium ion

Calculate the valence electrons and formal charge on N in NH4⁺.

  1. 5+4−1=8 electrons

    A positive charge subtracts an electron.

  2. Four N–H bonds consume 8 electrons

    No lone pair on N.

  3. FC(N)=5−0−8/2=+1

    The sum of the charges equals the charge of the ion.

Result: 8 electrons; FC(N)=+1

Resonance in ozone

How do the Lewis formulas for O3 differ?

  1. Total 18 valence electron

    Three O. atoms

  2. A single bond and a double bond while saving atoms

    The position of the double bond can be interchanged.

  3. The middle oxygen is +1 and the single end is −1

    The sum is zero; The actual association is distributed, not a succession of graphics.

Result: Two principal resonance forms

Useful background

Valence electrons and charge

Atoms in the 1 group typically have one valence electron, and the 17 group has seven. For a negative charge, we add an electron to the total, and for a positive charge, we subtract an electron. For example, NH4⁺ has 5+4−1=8 valence electrons, not 10.
