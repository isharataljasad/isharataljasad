CHEM 101 · TOPIC 03

Periodic table

Use electron arrangement to explain trends.

Book →Pearson →Educator →

The idea

Across a period, increasing effective nuclear attraction generally reduces atomic radius and raises first ionisation energy. Down a group, additional occupied shells usually increase size. Exceptions need electron-configuration reasoning.

A first example

An electron arrangement 2,8,1 has one outer-shell electron. Losing that electron gives a common +1 ion.

Engineering connection. Valence behaviour helps predict bonding and reactivity before a reaction is tested.

What this explanation develops

  • The period and number of valence electrons are determined from the atomic number.
  • The effective nuclear charge is approximately calculated from the atomic number and the number of blocking electrons.
  • Compares two elements in radius and ionization energy based on effective nuclear charge.
  • Write the electron configuration and explain ions and jumps in ionization and magnetism.

Definitions & meaning

We construct the ground distribution by filling the lowest energy orbitals, with a maximum of two oppositely spind electrons in the Pauli orbit. According to Hund, the electrons of equal energy orbitals occupy single orbitals with parallel spins before pairing. Oxygen 1s²2s²2p⁴ has two lone electrons, so it is paramagnetic; The coupling of all gives diamagnetism.

For iron, Fe: [Ar]3d⁶4s², and when making Fe²⁺, the 4s letter is removed first, so it becomes [Ar]3d⁶; The order of filling is not the order of stripping that is constant for all cases. Chromium [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ and copper [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹ are two important exceptions to the mechanistic application of simple arrangement, resulting from convergent energies and interaction of electrons.

The second ionization energy removes an electron from the positive ion, not from a new atom. The large jump after the removal of valence electrons indicates the start of the removal of an inner electron; A closed heart has greater attraction. The direction of ionization across the period is general with exceptions such as Be/B and N/O due to the orbit type and pairing of electrons. In an isoelectronic chain, increasing the number of protons often reduces the radius.

Period
A period is a horizontal row of the periodic table. In the main-group examples used here, the period identifies the highest occupied principal shell. Moving across a period changes nuclear charge and valence-electron count.
Group
A vertical column, and its elements are similar in the number of valence electrons and thus in chemical behavior. This similarity is why the table is arranged in the first place: chemistry is governed by valence electrons.
Shielding
The inner electrons shield part of the nucleus's attraction from the valence electrons. The outer electron does not feel all the charge of the nucleus, but rather what remains of it after the inner electrons block it.
Effective nuclear charge (Z_eff)
The net attraction felt by a valence electron is : Z_eff ≈ Z − S, where S is the number of blocking electrons. It is the key that explains all the table trends in this lesson.
Atomic radius
Atomic radius describes atomic size using a stated convention, such as half the distance between two bonded identical atoms. Across a main-group period it generally decreases as effective nuclear attraction increases. Down a group it generally increases as additional shells are occupied.
Ionization energy
First ionization energy is the energy required to remove an electron from a gaseous ground-state atom. It generally increases across a main-group period and decreases down a group, with exceptions explained by electron configurations. Radius alone does not determine every comparison.

Why does an atom get smaller as we add electrons through the cycle?

The result appears to be inverted: we add electrons and the volume becomes smaller. The reason is that the addition does not occur alone.

  1. Go from sodium Z = 11 to chlorine Z = 17 in the third cycle.

    Comparison within a single cycle fixes the number of levels and isolates the effect of charge alone.

  2. Note that the added electrons enter the same level, not a new level.

    The distance from the nucleus does not increase by adding a level; This is the difference from going down in a group.

  3. The number of internal electrons remained ten in both, so the blocking remained almost unchanged. While the protons increased by six.

    The electrons of the same level mask each other weakly, so the increase in charge appears net in Z_eff.

  4. Z_eff rises from 1 to 7, and gravity pulls the cloud inward, making the radius smaller.

    volume is not determined by the number of electrons, but by the intensity of attraction on them. This is why the chlorine atom is smaller than the sodium atom, even though it contains more electrons.

Worked examples

Start from the atomic number and distribute the electrons : 2, then 8, then 8. The number of occupied levels is the period, and the number of electrons of the last level is the valence electrons. Then calculate Z_eff = Z − S where S is the sum of electrons in the internal levels. Then every comparison became easy: a larger Z_eff in the same cycle means a smaller atom and greater ionization energy.

Example 1 · The location of an item and its effective charge

For the element Z = 16., determine its period, number of valence electrons, and approximately its effective nuclear charge.

  1. Distribute the electrons: 2, 8, 6.

    The first level accommodates two, the second eight, leaving six for the third. Distribution is the source of everything that follows it.

  2. The number of occupied levels is three, so the element is in the third cycle.

    The period is the number of the highest level occupied, neither the number of electrons nor the atomic number.

  3. The last level has six electrons, so they are valence electrons.

    It determines the chemical behavior and location of the element in the group.

  4. Blocking S = 2 + 8 = 10, Z_eff ≈ 16 − 10 = 6.

    Inner electrons alone are shielding; The electrons of the same level are weakly shielded and we neglect them.

Result: Period 3, six valence electrons, Z_eff ≈ 6

Example 2 · Comparing two sizes

Which is smaller: the sodium atom Z = 11 or the chlorine atom Z = 17? And why?

  1. They are both in the third period, and each has ten inner electrons.

    Fixing the cycle isolates the effect of charge; Otherwise, the effect of adding a level would be mixed with the effect of increasing the protons.

  2. Z_eff(Na) ≈ 1 and Z_eff(Cl) ≈ 7.

    The difference is seven times in net attraction, which is a huge difference that cannot be neglected.

  3. Chlorine is smaller because a stronger attraction pulls the electron cloud inward.

    This is despite the fact that chlorine contains six more electrons; The volume is determined by the intensity of attraction, not the number of electrons.

Result: Cl is smaller (Z_eff 7 vs 1)

Useful background

Atomic number and number of electrons

The atomic number Z is the number of protons, and is equal to the number of electrons in a neutral atom. Chlorine Z = 17 has seventeen electrons. As for the ion, its number of electrons is Z, with a minus charge.

Arrangement of electrons in levels

The first level holds two electrons, the second has eight, and the third has eight in the first elements. For sodium, Z = 11 and the shell arrangement is 2, 8, 1: three occupied shells place it in period 3, And one electron at the end, so it is in the first group. and sulfur, Z = 16, has the shell arrangement 2, 8, 6: period 3 with six valence electrons.
