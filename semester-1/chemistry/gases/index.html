CHEM 101 · TOPIC 07

Properties of gases

Use absolute temperature and state model assumptions.

TryLearnApply
TRY FIRST

Start with a short challenge

An ideal gas at 2 bar occupies 3 L. At constant temperature it is compressed to 1.5 L. Find pressure.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

The ideal gas equation PV=nRT relates state variables. Use compatible units and temperature in kelvin. At constant amount and temperature, pressure varies inversely with volume. Real gases depart from ideal behaviour, especially when interactions or molecular volume matter.

Worked example

For a fixed ideal-gas amount at fixed temperature, reducing volume from 4 L to 2 L doubles pressure.

Engineering connection Gas calculations require a stated pressure basis and temperature; a number without conditions is incomplete.

Use the ideal gas model conditionally

Idea. The ideal gas model treats particles as having negligible volume and no intermolecular attraction between collisions. Pressure, volume, amount, and absolute temperature determine its equilibrium state.

Decision. Convert temperature to kelvins and match the gas constant to pressure and volume units. If the amount is fixed between two states, compare PV/T; at high pressure or low temperature, expect larger deviations from ideal behavior.

Worked case. One mole of ideal gas at 300 K in 24.6 L has pressure near 1.00 atm because R≈0.08206 L·atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Try this before revealing the answer

A fixed amount of ideal gas at constant volume warms from 300 K to 450 K. What is P2/P1?

Reveal answer and reasoning

At fixed n and V, P scales with absolute T. Thus P2/P1=450/300=1.50.

Misconception check. Never insert Celsius temperature directly into the ideal-gas equation or a gas-law ratio.

Add partial pressures and notice nonideal gases

Idea. Each ideal gas in a mixture contributes a partial pressure proportional to its mole fraction. Real gases deviate when intermolecular attractions or particle volume matter; the ideal model improves at lower pressure and higher temperature.

Decision. For a dry gas mixture, use mole fractions. For gas collected over water, subtract water vapor pressure from total pressure before calculating the dry gas amount.

Worked case. A sample collected over water has total pressure 750 torr and water vapor pressure 24 torr. Dry gas pressure is 726 torr.

Try this before revealing the answer

A mixture contains 2 mol He and 3 mol Ne at total pressure 1.0 atm. What is He partial pressure?

Reveal answer and reasoning

He mole fraction is 2/5, so PHe=0.40 atm.

Misconception check. Partial pressure of a wet gas is not the measured total pressure when water vapor is present.

Choose a gas model with its conditions

Question cue. Pressure, volume, temperature, and moles are related.

Meaning. An ideal gas relates P, V, n, and absolute T. It is most reliable when particles are dilute and intermolecular effects are small.

First move. Convert temperature to kelvin, choose consistent pressure and volume units, and use an R value that matches them. State which quantities stay fixed if using a two-state law.

Worked case. At 1.00 atm and 273 K, 1.00 mol ideal gas occupies about 22.4 L with R=0.08206 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Try this before revealing the answer

At fixed n and T, pressure doubles. What happens to volume?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Volume halves because PV=nRT remains constant for the stated model.

If stuck. Never insert Celsius directly into a gas-law temperature ratio.

Separate total gas pressure into components

Question cue. A mixture has partial pressures or mole fractions.

Meaning. For an ideal-gas mixture, each component contributes a pressure proportional to its mole fraction at common T and V. Collected gas over water includes water-vapor pressure.

First move. Find total gas moles and each mole fraction. Multiply by total pressure, or subtract vapor pressure when isolating a dry gas.

Worked case. A mixture with 1 mol N₂ and 3 mol O₂ at 2.0 atm has x(N₂)=1/4 and P(N₂)=0.50 atm.

Try this before revealing the answer

Gas is collected over water at total pressure 760 mmHg; water vapor is 24 mmHg. What is the dry-gas pressure?

Reveal answer and reasoning

736 mmHg, because the total is dry gas plus water vapor.

If stuck. A partial pressure is not the same as the pressure that component would have at a different volume.

Read gas trends from particle motion

Question cue. A question compares gas speed, effusion, or temperature.

Meaning. At equal temperature, gases share mean translational kinetic energy, but lighter molecules have larger root-mean-square speed. Effusion rate has an approximate inverse square-root molar-mass relation.

First move. Compare absolute temperatures for kinetic energy. For rms speed, use molar mass in kg/mol with SI R. For two gases at equal T, compare speeds or effusion rates using molar-mass square roots.

Worked case. At the same temperature, helium (M≈4 g/mol) has about sqrt(40/4)=3.16 times the rms speed of argon (M≈40 g/mol).

Try this before revealing the answer

At equal T, which effuses faster: H₂ or O₂, and by what approximate factor?

Reveal answer and reasoning

H₂; the idealized rate ratio is sqrt(32/2)=4.

If stuck. Equal temperature does not imply equal molecular speed; it implies equal average translational kinetic energy.

Connect gas variables

Principle. Ideal gas behavior relates pressure, volume, moles, and absolute temperature. At fixed T and n, P is inverse to V; at fixed P and n, V follows T.

Decision. Convert °C to K and use a gas constant consistent with pressure and volume units.

Worked case. At 1.00 atm and 298 K, 1.00 mol ideal gas occupies about 24.5 L.

Check. At high pressure or low temperature, intermolecular forces and finite particle size matter.
