THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Balance atoms and charge before applying mole ratios. Strong soluble electrolytes can be represented as ions; spectator ions cancel in a net ionic equation. Available amounts determine the limiting reactant.

Worked example

For Ag⁺+Cl⁻→AgCl(s), 0.10 mol Ag⁺ needs 0.10 mol Cl⁻. With only 0.06 mol Cl⁻, at most 0.06 mol precipitate can form.

Engineering connection Reactant amounts set the maximum material conversion, even when the equation is correctly balanced.

التعريفات والعلاقات

عند خلط محلولي AgNO3 وNaCl تتغير Ag⁺ وCl⁻ لتكوين AgCl(s)، بينما Na⁺ وNO3⁻ يبقيان في المحلول. تكتب المعادلة الجزيئية أسماء المركبات، لكن المعادلة الصافية تركز على التغير. لا تحذف أيونًا لمجرد وجود العنصر نفسه في الجانبين؛ يجب أن يكون النوع الكيميائي نفسه.

حفظ الشحنة شرط مستقل عن حفظ الذرات. في Ag⁺+Cl⁻→AgCl تكون الشحنة الكلية صفرًا على الطرفين. وفي تفاعلات التأكسد والاختزال نستعمل أنصاف تفاعلات ونوازن الإلكترونات المفقودة والمكتسبة قبل الجمع. لا تترك إلكترونات في المعادلة الكلية عندما جمعت نصفي تفاعل مكتملين.

قبل حساب الراسب اكتب المعادلة ثم احسب مولات كل متفاعل بوحدات متوافقة. استخدم النسبة الستوكيومترية لا النسبة الحجمية إلا إذا كانت التراكيز والمعاملات تسمح بذلك. نحول مولات الراسب إلى كتلة في آخر خطوة؛ الكتلة المولية تخص الراسب لا الملح الذي بدأنا به.

في معايرة حمض قوي بقاعدة قوية تكون نقطة التكافؤ حين تتساوى المكافئات الستوكيومترية، وليس بالضرورة حين تتساوى الأحجام. إذا بقي OH⁻ أوH⁺ بعد التفاعل، نحسب مولاته الزائدة ثم نقسم على الحجم الكلي للمحلول بافتراض جمع الحجوم في المسألة المبسطة. نقطة نهاية الدليل تقريب تجريبي لنقطة التكافؤ وليستا تعريفًا واحدًا.

إلكتروليت Electrolyte
مذاب ينتج أيونات متحركة في الماء فيسمح بالتوصيل. القوي يتفكك أو يتأين بدرجة كبيرة عند الذوبان، والضعيف جزئيًا، وغير الإلكتروليت يبقى غالبًا جزيئات متعادلة. كلمة قوي تصف مدى التأين لا كون المحلول مركزًا.
معادلة أيونية صافية Net ionic equation
تصف الأنواع التي تتغير فعليًا بعد حذف الأيونات التي تظهر بالحالة والصيغة والمعامل نفسيهما على الطرفين. نفكك الإلكتروليتات القوية الذائبة في المعادلة الأيونية، ونبقي الراسب والغاز والماء والأحماض الضعيفة جزيئات أو صيغًا كاملة.
ترسيب Precipitation
تكوّن طور صلب قليل الذوبان عند مزج محاليل تحتوي الأيونات المناسبة. كثير من النترات وأملاح الفلزات القلوية والأمونيوم ذائبة. كثير من الكلوريدات ذائبة مع استثناءات مثل AgCl. كثير من الكربونات قليلة الذوبان عدا أملاح القلويات والأمونيوم.
تعادل حمض وقاعدة Acid–base neutralization
تفاعل انتقال بروتون. في حمض قوي وقاعدة قوية تكون المعادلة الصافية H⁺+OH⁻→H2O. في حمض ضعيف مثل CH3COOH نبقي الحمض غير مفكك في الطرف المتفاعل: CH3COOH+OH⁻→CH3COO⁻+H2O.
متفاعل محدد Limiting reactant
المتفاعل الذي يسمح بأقل عدد من مرات حدوث التفاعل وفق معاملاته. نقارن n/المعامل لكل متفاعل. الأصغر يحدد كمية المنتج النظرية، وقد يبقى متفاعل آخر بعد انتهاء التفاعل. الأقل حجمًا ليس بالضرورة المحدد.
تأكسد واختزال Oxidation and reduction
التأكسد فقد إلكترونات وارتفاع عدد التأكسد، والاختزال اكتسابها وانخفاضه. العامل المؤكسد يُختزل، والعامل المختزل يتأكسد. عدد التأكسد محاسبة تساعد على تتبع الإلكترونات ولا يساوي دائمًا شحنة فعلية على الذرة.

من الصفر إلى الحل

حدّد نوع التفاعل، زن المعادلة، حوّل المعطيات إلى مولات، ثم افحص المتفاعل المحدد والأنواع الباقية.

كتلة راسب

امزج 25.0 mL من AgNO3 تركيزه 0.100 M مع 40.0 mL من NaCl تركيزه 0.100 M. M(AgCl)=143.32 g/mol.

  1. n(Ag⁺)=0.0250×0.100=0.00250 molلماذا: الحجم باللتر.
  2. n(Cl⁻)=0.0400×0.100=0.00400 molلماذا: المقارنة مولية 1:1.
  3. Ag⁺ محدد؛ n(AgCl)=0.00250 molلماذا: لا يكفي Ag⁺ لاستهلاك كل الكلوريد.
  4. m=0.00250×143.32=0.3583 gلماذا: نحول المنتج إلى كتلة.

الناتج: 0.3583 g

معايرة

كم mL من NaOH بتركيز 0.200 M تعادل 20.0 mL من HCl بتركيز 0.100 M؟

  1. n(H⁺)=0.0200×0.100=0.00200 molلماذا: نحسب مولات الحمض.
  2. n(OH⁻)=0.00200 molلماذا: التعادل 1:1.
  3. V=0.00200/0.200=0.0100 L=10.0 mLلماذا: القاعدة ضعف التركيز فتحتاج نصف الحجم.

الناتج: 10.0 mL

Balance a reaction by conserving atoms

Idea. A balanced chemical equation states a whole-number ratio of reacting entities. The formulas identify substances; coefficients count them. Chemical reactions conserve each element and total charge.

Decision. Confirm the reactant and product formulas first. Change coefficients only, not subscripts. Balance elements appearing in fewer species first, leave H and O for later when practical, then check every count and reduce coefficients.

Worked case. For propane combustion, C3H8 + 5 O2 → 3 CO2 + 4 H2O. Both sides contain 3 C, 8 H, and 10 O atoms.

Try this before revealing the answer

Balance Fe + O2 → Fe2O3 with smallest whole-number coefficients.

Reveal answer and reasoning

4 Fe + 3 O2 → 2 Fe2O3. Both sides have four Fe atoms and six O atoms.

Misconception check. A balanced equation does not tell you whether a reaction proceeds or how quickly it happens.

Read aqueous reactions as ions

Idea. Strong soluble electrolytes are mostly dissociated in water. A net ionic equation retains the species that change and removes spectator ions. Precipitation, gas formation, and weak-electrolyte formation can drive a visible reaction.

Decision. Balance the molecular equation, split only soluble strong electrolytes into ions, cancel identical species on both sides, and verify both atoms and net charge.

Worked case. AgNO3(aq)+NaCl(aq)→AgCl(s)+NaNO3(aq). Cancelling Na+ and NO3− gives Ag+(aq)+Cl−(aq)→AgCl(s).

Try this before revealing the answer

Give the net ionic equation for BaCl2(aq)+Na2SO4(aq)→BaSO4(s)+2 NaCl(aq).

Reveal answer and reasoning

Ba2+(aq)+SO4²−(aq)→BaSO4(s). The Na+ and Cl− ions are spectators.

Misconception check. Do not split solids, liquids, gases, or weak molecular acids just because their formulas contain ions.

Track electrons with oxidation numbers

Idea. Oxidation numbers are a bookkeeping tool for electron transfer. An increase in oxidation number is oxidation; a decrease is reduction. The oxidizing agent is reduced, and the reducing agent is oxidized.

Decision. Assign common values first: free element 0, monatomic ion its charge, O usually −2, H usually +1 with nonmetals. Use the total charge to solve the remaining value, then compare reactants and products.

Worked case. In Zn + Cu2+ → Zn2+ + Cu, Zn changes 0→+2 and loses two electrons; Cu changes +2→0 and gains two. Cu2+ is the oxidizing agent.

Try this before revealing the answer

In 2 Mg + O2 → 2 MgO, name what is oxidized and what is reduced.

Reveal answer and reasoning

Mg goes 0→+2 and is oxidized. O goes 0→−2 and is reduced. The O2 is the oxidizing agent.

Misconception check. An oxidizing agent gains electrons; its name describes what it does to the other reactant.

Use coefficients as mole ratios

Idea. A balanced equation connects amounts of substances in moles, not directly in grams. The reaction coefficient ratio is the bridge between different species.

Decision. Convert given mass or volume to moles of the known species, apply the coefficient ratio, then convert to the requested unit. Include the substance name in each factor so accidental inverse ratios are visible.

Worked case. For N2 + 3 H2 → 2 NH3, 0.50 mol N2 can produce 1.0 mol NH3 if enough H2 is available.

Try this before revealing the answer

For 2 H2 + O2 → 2 H2O, how many moles of water can 1.5 mol O2 produce with excess H2?

Reveal answer and reasoning

The ratio is 2 mol H2O per 1 mol O2, so 1.5 mol O2 can produce 3.0 mol H2O.

Misconception check. A coefficient is not a mass multiplier. Convert grams to moles before using the balanced equation.

Find the limiting reagent and yield

Idea. The limiting reagent sets the maximum product, because it is consumed first in the ideal reaction. Theoretical yield is calculated from it; actual yield is measured. Excess reagent remains after the reaction.

Decision. Calculate the possible product from each reactant separately, in moles. The smaller product amount determines the limit. Then compute theoretical mass and compare with measured mass.

Worked case. For N2+3 H2→2 NH3, 2.0 mol N2 could make 4.0 mol NH3, while 3.0 mol H2 could make 2.0 mol NH3. H2 limits production. If 1.5 mol NH3 is collected, percent yield is 75%.

Try this before revealing the answer

For 2 H2+O2→2 H2O, 3.0 mol H2 reacts with 1.0 mol O2. Which limits, and how much H2O forms ideally?

Reveal answer and reasoning

O2 can make 2.0 mol H2O; H2 could make 3.0 mol. O2 limits, so theoretical yield is 2.0 mol H2O.

Misconception check. The reactant with fewer moles is not necessarily limiting; compare its required coefficient ratio.

Balance without changing chemical identity

Question cue. A skeletal equation is given.

Meaning. Coefficients specify how many formula units or molecules react. Balancing conserves each element and, for ionic equations, total charge. Subscripts belong to the substance and must remain fixed.

First move. Confirm formulas, then adjust coefficients. Tally each element on both sides and reduce to the smallest integer set.

Worked case. For propane combustion, C₃H₈+5O₂→3CO₂+4H₂O. The two sides each have 3 C, 8 H, and 10 O atoms.

Try this before revealing the answer

Balance Fe+O₂→Fe₂O₃ with smallest integer coefficients.

Reveal answer and reasoning

4Fe+3O₂→2Fe₂O₃. Both sides contain four iron atoms and six oxygen atoms.

If stuck. Changing Fe₂O₃ to FeO would describe a different product, not a balanced equation.

Use coefficients as mole ratios

Question cue. An amount of one species must predict another species.

Meaning. The balanced equation relates amounts in moles. Grams require a molar-mass conversion before and after the mole ratio.

First move. Convert the known amount to moles, apply product and reactant coefficients, then convert to the requested unit. State the excess-reactant assumption when only one reactant amount is supplied.

Worked case. N₂+3H₂→2NH₃ predicts 1.00 mol NH₃ from 0.500 mol N₂ if H₂ is sufficient.

Try this before revealing the answer

For 2H₂+O₂→2H₂O, how many moles of H₂O can 1.5 mol O₂ make with excess H₂?

Reveal answer and reasoning

The 2:1 water-to-oxygen ratio gives 3.0 mol H₂O.

If stuck. A coefficient is a mole multiplier, not a direct gram multiplier.

Find the limiting reagent and percent yield

Question cue. Two reactant quantities or actual product amount are given.

Meaning. Each reactant predicts an ideal product amount. The smaller prediction sets theoretical yield. Actual yield is what was collected and may be lower.

First move. Predict the same product separately from each reactant. Use the smaller result, convert it to the requested unit, and compare actual product with this theoretical maximum.

Worked case. For N₂+3H₂→2NH₃, 2.0 mol N₂ predicts 4.0 mol NH₃; 3.0 mol H₂ predicts 2.0 mol NH₃. H₂ limits. Collecting 1.5 mol gives 75% yield.

Try this before revealing the answer

For 2H₂+O₂→2H₂O, 3.0 mol H₂ reacts with 1.0 mol O₂. Which limits and how much water can form?

Reveal answer and reasoning

O₂ limits: it predicts 2.0 mol H₂O, while H₂ could predict 3.0 mol H₂O.

If stuck. The reactant with fewer moles is not always limiting; coefficients determine the comparison.

OPTIONAL SHORT VIDEOLimiting Reagent Concept · 01:30 ↗

Write the species that actually react

Question cue. A molecular equation includes aqueous ionic substances.

Meaning. A complete ionic equation separates soluble strong electrolytes. Spectator ions appear unchanged on both sides and disappear from the net ionic equation.

First move. Balance the molecular equation. Split only aqueous strong electrolytes, cancel unchanged ions, and check atoms and charge on the net equation.

Worked case. AgNO₃(aq)+NaCl(aq)→AgCl(s)+NaNO₃(aq) reduces to Ag⁺(aq)+Cl⁻(aq)→AgCl(s).

Try this before revealing the answer

Give the net ionic equation for BaCl₂(aq)+Na₂SO₄(aq)→BaSO₄(s)+2NaCl(aq).

Reveal answer and reasoning

Ba²⁺(aq)+SO₄²⁻(aq)→BaSO₄(s). Sodium and chloride are spectators.

If stuck. Do not split a solid, liquid, gas, or weak molecular acid into free ions.

Track electrons with oxidation numbers

Question cue. A reaction asks what is oxidized, reduced, or transferred.

Meaning. Oxidation raises an oxidation number; reduction lowers it. The oxidizing agent accepts electrons and is itself reduced.

First move. Assign oxidation numbers before identifying agents. Check that electron loss and gain balance in the finished equation.

Worked case. In Zn+Cu²⁺→Zn²⁺+Cu, Zn rises from 0 to +2 and loses two electrons. Cu falls from +2 to 0 and gains them.

Try this before revealing the answer

For 2Mg+O₂→2MgO, which species is the reducing agent?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Mg is the reducing agent because its oxidation number rises from 0 to +2 as it supplies electrons to oxygen.

If stuck. Oxygen is not always −2, notably in peroxides; use the compound context.

Reference and worked examples

Adapted Book material selected for this topic.

Balance, then convert

Principle. A balanced equation conserves each atom and total charge. Coefficients supply mole ratios; mass ratios come only after molar-mass conversion.

Decision. Convert given mass → moles → coefficient ratio → desired unit.

Worked case. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O: 3 mol O₂ could form 6 mol H₂O with enough H₂.

Check. A coefficient never means a gram ratio.

Find the limiting reactant

Principle. Calculate the product possible from each reactant separately; the smaller product amount is the theoretical yield. Actual yield divided by theoretical yield gives percent yield.

Decision. Compare product moles, not raw reactant masses.

Worked case. N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃: 2 mol N₂ and 3 mol H₂ give at most 2 mol NH₃; H₂ limits.

Check. Excess reagent can remain even when the reaction stops.

Write a net ionic equation

Principle. Separate strong aqueous electrolytes into ions, preserve solids, liquids, gases, and weak electrolytes, then cancel unchanged spectator ions.

Decision. First predict whether a precipitate, gas, or weak electrolyte forms.

Worked case. Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s).

Check. Do not split a solid product into ions.

Track acid, base, and redox

Principle. A Brønsted acid donates H⁺ and a base accepts H⁺. Oxidation loses electrons; reduction gains electrons. Oxidation numbers expose the electron transfer.

Decision. Separate electron transfer from proton transfer before selecting a method.

Worked case. Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu: Zn oxidizes; Cu²⁺ reduces.

Check. The oxidizing agent itself is reduced.
