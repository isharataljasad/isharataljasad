Electrochemistry

Track oxidation reduction and the cell-potential convention.

Book →Pearson →Educator →

The idea

Oxidation loses electrons at the anode; reduction gains electrons at the cathode. With tabulated reduction potentials, E°cell=E°cathode−E°anode. Do not multiply an electrode potential by a balancing coefficient. Electrolysis relates charge to electron amount.

A first example

Using cathode reduction potential +0.34 V and anode reduction potential −0.76 V gives E°cell=1.10 V.

Engineering connection. Electrochemical reasoning supports later study of corrosion, electrolysis and energy conversion.

What this explanation develops

  • Identify the anode, cathode and electron-flow direction, and distinguish galvanic from electrolytic cells.
  • The cell potential and Gibbs energy are calculated from the reduction potentials.
  • Nernst is used at 25°C with the correct reaction quotient.
  • The amount of substance in electrolysis is calculated from the current and time.

Definitions & meaning

A galvanic cell spontaneously converts a chemical change into electrical work. In a zinc-copper cell, the zinc is oxidized and electrons are transferred from its electrode through the wire to the copper, where the copper ions are reduced. The mass of the zinc electrode may decrease and the mass of the copper electrode may increase. No free electrons from the solution move across the bridge to achieve this path.

To balance a cell with two halves with different numbers of electrons, we multiply the chemical equations until the lost and gained electrons are equal, but we do not multiply the reduction potentials. When the direction of the overall reaction is reversed, the sign E is reversed and the sign ΔG. is changed. The relationship ΔG°=−nFE° shows that a positive potential means a negative Gibbs energy for the reaction written under standard conditions.

Standard conditions use standard activities and standard pressures, and do not automatically mean any mixture is at room temperature. The general Nernst equation is E=E°−RT ln(Q)/(nF), and when 25°C becomes E=E°−0.05916 log10(Q)/n., Q must be dimensionless based on the reference activities, even if the concentration ratios were written to simplify the calculation.

If the products of the reaction increase compared to its reactants, Q increases and the voltage decreases in the written direction. When E=0 and Q=K. are balanced, therefore, log10(K)=nE°/0.05916, when 25°C., changing the concentration may change the actual voltage; Do not judge all conditions from E° alone.

In electroplating problems, write the metal deposition half-reaction first:. Cu²⁺ requires two electrons, and Ag⁺ requires one. For the same charge, the moles of the metal vary according to this number, and then the mass varies according to the molar mass. Calculate the time in seconds and the current in amperes to be the product in coulombs.

Anode and cathode
The anode is the site of oxidation and the cathode is the site of reduction in both cells. In a galvanic cell, the anode is negative and the cathode is positive, and in an analytical cell, the external source imposes the opposite signs. The fixed definition is the type of interaction not the electrode reference.
Standard reduction potential
Half-reaction potential written in the direction of reduction relative to a reference. We use the values ​​as in the reduction table, then E°cell=E°cathode−E°anode. Voltage is an intensive property; We do not multiply it by the electron balance coefficients.
Salt bridge
It allows the transfer of ions to help maintain electrical neutrality and close the internal circuit. Electrons travel in the outer wire, not through the salt bridge as a wire. The ions move according to the accumulation of charges and the composition of the two halves of the cell.
Reaction quotient
Q is based on the activities of species in the overall balanced equation; Neither pure solids nor pure liquids are included in the activity quotient. Approximating activity by concentration requires an appropriate model of the solution. In Zn+Cu²⁺→Zn²⁺+Cu it is Q≈[Zn²⁺]/[Cu²⁺].
Electrolysis
Using an external source to drive a non-spontaneous reaction in the desired direction. Charge q=It, moles of electrons q/F. Moles of product equal moles of electrons divided by the number of electrons needed per unit of product, taking into account efficiency if less than 100%.

Worked examples

Weigh the two half-reactions, determine its direction, use the reduction potentials without multiplying them, then choose the energy, Nernst, or charge relationship as required.

Zn/Cu cell

E°(Cu²⁺/Cu)=+0.34 V, E°(Zn²⁺/Zn)=−0.76 V. Calculate cell voltage.

  1. Copper is reduced and zinc is oxidized

    Higher copper reduction potential.

  2. E°cell=0.34−(−0.76)=1.10 V

    We subtract the anode reduction potential.

  3. n=2، ΔG°=−2×96485×1.10≈−212 kJ/mol

    Mole here is the total reaction written.

Result: 1.10 V; ΔG°≈−212 kJ/mol

Silver plating

Stream 1.00 A for 965 s, Ag⁺+e⁻→Ag, M=107.87 g/mol. Efficiently estimate mass 100%.

    One electron per Ag.

Details that matter

Doubling the reaction doubles ΔG and the number of electrons n together, and the ratio −ΔG/(nF) remains the same.

Useful background

Balance of electrons and moles

The Zn→Zn²⁺+2e⁻ half-reaction loses two electrons per Zn atom, while Cu²⁺+2e⁻→Cu gains them. When combined, they cancel. mole of electrons carrying a charge F≈96485 C; Do not confuse this number with the number of moles of the metal.
