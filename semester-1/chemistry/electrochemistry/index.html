Electrochemistry

Track oxidation reduction and the cell-potential convention.

TRY FIRST

Start with a short challenge

Cathode and anode reduction potentials are +0.50 V and −0.20 V. Find E°cell.

THE IDEA

Understand the decision

Oxidation loses electrons at the anode; reduction gains electrons at the cathode. With tabulated reduction potentials, E°cell=E°cathode−E°anode. Do not multiply an electrode potential by a balancing coefficient. Electrolysis relates charge to electron amount.

Worked example

Using cathode reduction potential +0.34 V and anode reduction potential −0.76 V gives E°cell=1.10 V.

Engineering connection Electrochemical reasoning supports later study of corrosion, electrolysis and energy conversion.

Math step getting in the way?

  • Numerical: subtract a negative potential →
  • Algebraic: keep cathode minus anode order →

Build a galvanic cell from half reactions

Idea. In a spontaneous galvanic cell, oxidation occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode. Electrons travel through the external wire from anode to cathode; ions in a salt bridge maintain charge balance. Standard reduction potentials are tabulated as reductions.

Decision. Write and balance the two half reactions. Choose the more positive standard reduction potential as the cathode for a favorable standard cell, and subtract the anode reduction potential. Multiply electrons for atom balance but never multiply electrode potentials.

Worked case. For Zn2+/Zn at −0.76 V and Cu2+/Cu at +0.34 V, Zn oxidizes, Cu2+ reduces, and E°cell=0.34−(−0.76)=+1.10 V.

Try this before revealing the answer

A cell has cathode reduction potential +0.50 V and anode reduction potential −0.20 V. Find E°cell.

Reveal answer and reasoning

E°cell=+0.50−(−0.20)=+0.70 V.

Misconception check. Anode means oxidation in both galvanic and electrolytic cells; the electrode sign differs between those devices.

Calculate electrolysis from charge

Idea. An external power supply can drive an otherwise unfavorable redox change. The charge passed determines moles of electrons, which connect to the amount of product through its balanced half reaction. Faraday constant F≈96485 C/mol electrons.

Decision. Find Q from current and time in seconds, divide by F, and use electrons per product ion. Check which half reaction is actually possible in the specified electrolyte, especially in water.

Worked case. Depositing Cu from Cu2+ + 2e−→Cu with 1930 C ideally uses 1930/96485≈0.0200 mol electrons and gives 0.0100 mol Cu, about 0.635 g.

Try this before revealing the answer

How many moles of Ag can form ideally from Ag+ + e−→Ag when 9648.5 C passes?

Reveal answer and reasoning

n(e−)=9648.5/96485=0.100 mol, so 0.100 mol Ag forms if current efficiency is 100%.

Misconception check. Do not divide charge by F and call the result moles of metal before using the half-reaction electron ratio.

Assign cell electrodes before calculating voltage

Question cue. Two standard reduction potentials form a galvanic cell.

Meaning. Oxidation occurs at the anode, reduction at the cathode. Electrons flow through the external wire from anode to cathode. Tabulated potentials are reductions, so subtract the anode reduction potential.

First move. Choose the more positive reduction potential as the cathode for a spontaneous standard galvanic cell. Write and balance half reactions, then calculate E°cell; never multiply a potential to balance electrons.

Worked case. Zn²⁺/Zn is −0.76 V and Cu²⁺/Cu is +0.34 V. Zn oxidizes, Cu²⁺ reduces, and E°cell=0.34−(−0.76)=+1.10 V.

Try this before revealing the answer

Cathode reduction is +0.50 V and anode reduction is −0.20 V. Find E°cell.

Reveal answer and reasoning

+0.70 V. Subtract the listed anode reduction potential: 0.50−(−0.20).

If stuck. Changing half-reaction coefficients does not multiply electrode potentials.

Connect nonstandard composition to cell voltage

Question cue. A cell operates away from standard concentrations.

Meaning. Cell voltage depends on reaction quotient. A product-heavy composition reduces a positive cell potential; at equilibrium the cell voltage for that reaction is zero.

First move. Balance the overall electron transfer to get n and Q. Use the Nernst equation with kelvin and consistent R/F units, then check the sign of ln Q.

Worked case. For a two-electron cell at 298 K, Q=10 gives a voltage about 0.0296 V below E° because (0.0592/2)log₁₀10=0.0296 V.

Try this before revealing the answer

At 298 K, a one-electron cell has Q=0.1. Is E greater or less than E°?

Reveal answer and reasoning

Greater. log₁₀(0.1)=−1, so subtracting the negative term raises E by about 0.0592 V.

If stuck. Use Q for the balanced overall cell reaction, not a concentration ratio invented from half reactions.

Convert electric charge into chemical product

Question cue. Current and time determine electrolysis amount.

Meaning. Charge passed is current times time. Faraday’s constant converts charge to moles of electrons; the balanced half reaction converts electrons to product moles.

First move. Compute Q in coulombs, divide by F≈96485 C/mol e⁻, then use the electron-to-product coefficient. Apply current efficiency only if specified.

Worked case. Cu²⁺+2e⁻→Cu. Passing 1930 C ideally gives 1930/96485≈0.0200 mol e⁻ and 0.0100 mol Cu, about 0.635 g.

Try this before revealing the answer

How many moles of Ag form ideally from Ag⁺+e⁻→Ag after 9648.5 C passes?

Reveal answer and reasoning

0.100 mol e⁻ passes and the ratio is 1:1, so 0.100 mol Ag forms.

If stuck. Moles of electrons are not automatically moles of metal; read the half-reaction ratio.

Reference and worked examples

Adapted Book material selected for this topic.

Construct a galvanic cell

Principle. Oxidation occurs at the anode and reduction at the cathode. Electrons travel externally from anode to cathode; ions migrate through the salt bridge to maintain charge balance.

Decision. Compute E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode using reduction potentials; never multiply a potential by stoichiometric coefficients.

Worked case. Cu|Cu²⁺ || Ag⁺|Ag has E° = 0.80 − 0.34 = 0.46 V.

Check. A galvanic cell has positive Ecell for the spontaneous written reaction.

Understand electrolysis

Principle. An external power supply drives an otherwise nonspontaneous redox change. The electrode names still follow oxidation at anode and reduction at cathode.

Decision. Charge in coulombs divided by Faraday's constant gives moles of electrons.

Worked case. 2 F of charge can deposit 1 mol Cu from Cu²⁺ if current efficiency is 100%.

Check. Electrode polarity differs between galvanic and electrolytic setups.
