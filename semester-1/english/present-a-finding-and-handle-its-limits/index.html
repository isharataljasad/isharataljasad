Present a finding and handle its limits

← Give a clear spoken explanationEnglish contentsMake spoken meaning easier to follow →

What this lesson helps you do

Explain a small set of results to an audience and respond honestly when the evidence is incomplete.

Written models only. Written strategy and model. No audio recording is available yet, so this lesson shows the method in writing; it does not yet teach listening or pronunciation through sound.

Before this lesson

This lesson builds on:

  • 11 Describe a chart with an overview and accurate comparisons
  • 14 Use evidence without overstating it
  • 18 Give a clear spoken explanation

The explanation

A short presentation can follow a clear line: what we examined, what the information shows, and what it means within its limits. Name the chart or table before discussing its details. Say the units aloud when needed, and highlight the comparison the audience should notice.

Use a slide or figure to support the message rather than to display a full script. A listener should not have to read a dense paragraph while listening to different words. Labels, a short title and the relevant data can be enough. Match every spoken claim to the visible figure.

When information is missing, separate what is known from what would need further investigation. Acknowledging a limit is more useful than improvising a confident but unsupported answer. The models below are complete demonstration statements, not an interactive oral test.

Language pattern

Pattern
Purpose → key finding → supporting comparison → limitation → next information need.
What it does
Make the evidence and its boundaries audible.
Limits

A first worked example

Present the invented travel dataset

Situation. Use the invented data in ENG-11; this is not a report of a real campus survey.

Why it works

  • The opening states that the data are invented.
  • One main pattern guides the presentation.
  • The numbers match the table and their units are named.
  • The final sentences distinguish description from explanation.

What it shows: A clear presentation tells the audience both what the data show and what they do not show.

A second worked example

Handle a missing explanation

Situation. Demonstration response when someone attributes the bus increase to lower fares, which the data do not report.

Why it works

  • Possible labels the explanation as a hypothesis.
  • The response returns to the evidence available.
  • The final sentence identifies an information need without pretending the question is settled.

What it shows: An honest limit can be explained clearly and professionally.

Common mistakes

  • Draft (not correct): The audience can infer every unit from the slide.

    Revision: Name the unit when introducing the quantity.

    Listeners may not read the figure at the same pace as the speaker.

  • Draft (not correct): A confident answer is better than admitting an unknown.

    Revision: State the known result and the missing evidence.

    Confidence does not replace evidence.

Keep in mind

  • Guide attention to one main finding.
  • Make every number match the visual.
  • Explain uncertainty directly.
