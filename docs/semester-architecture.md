# Chemical engineering learning architecture

## Active scope

Semester 1 science only: MA 101 Calculus I, PHY 101 General Physics I, and CHEM 101 General Chemistry I. These represent 12 credits, not the full university semester. Biology and English are absent from active navigation. Existing source collections remain at their original URLs.

The published Yanbu Industrial College Chemical Engineering plan and descriptions govern scope. The student's actual current outline takes priority when supplied. Mathematics and chemistry topic groups follow published descriptions; the detailed mechanics sequence is provisional because the published PHY 101 description is broad.

## Student journey

Semester → course → needed topic → short challenge → explanation and worked example → fresh question → next topic.

Each topic has one outcome, an explanation, a worked model, an engineering connection, targeted foundation support, and selected source material. The student starts with a topic. Optional source extracts retain their separate provenance.

This release contains 26 topic pages and 52 numerical checks. They are introductory practice, not a complete examination bank or evidence of examination readiness. Lecturer assessments and laboratory instructions remain authoritative.

## Content model

`semester-1/curriculum.json` stores the program, plan version, semester, source evidence, courses and stable topic keys. Topics contain outcomes, evidence status, sequence, suggested previous topic, foundation links, separate resource mappings and versioned checkpoints. Suggested sequence does not lock access or assert an official prerequisite.

Book, Educator and Pearson collections supply selected explanations and practice. Their catalog order does not define the university curriculum. Replacing an explanation does not require changing the course structure.

## Growth each semester

Math Foundations is a shared optional library across semesters. See [the skill map and learning mechanism](math-foundations.md). Topic routes link to the specific missing skill and return to the topic; the library is not duplicated per semester.

1. Obtain and record the semester's official course outlines.
2. Map required outcomes before selecting resources.
3. Add a semester manifest and course/topic routes with stable keys.
4. Reuse relevant foundation refreshers and source material.
5. Add worked examples and changed-problem checks; verify answers and coverage.
6. Review the student experience, then publish the semester.

The proposed mathematics catalog is a future resource pool. Algebra, geometry and trigonometry support current gaps. Integral calculus belongs with MA 102; multivariable calculus with MA 201; probability and statistics with MA 202; differential equations with MA 203, subject to the student's plan. AP Physics B/C material is selected by the required college topic. English can be added separately when needed.

## Progress and student feedback

Progress is saved in the current browser, without cross-device synchronization. Passing the fresh check records “Check passed.” Revealing its reasoning before solving it records “Review needed,” even if the displayed answer is then entered. This is practice evidence, not a grade or mastery claim. Checkpoint revisions must invalidate incompatible saved records.

Evaluate whether the student can find the required topic, understand the example, explain the method and solve a changed problem. Use that feedback to improve subsequent lessons. Additional question variants and delayed checks can follow this initial evaluation.
